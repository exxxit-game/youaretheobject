## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: not stated. Stroop was 480 trials and flanker 288, at up to 3 s per reach plus a 1 s cross, so likely 20–40 min. Two or three blocks of 48 trials would take about 6–10 min.
- Task/tension: mild. A fast "touch the colour" reaching game, easy to frame as sorting under time pressure.
- Within-person reveal: strong and visual. Replay the player's own hand paths: on "GREEN written in red" trials the hand bends toward the green square before correcting. The group effects on start time and curvature were large, so the bend should usually be visible for one player.
- Works if the player expects tricks: yes. Stroop interference is automatic and is known to persist when people know the task. The paper excluded one person who avoided reading the word (for example by squinting), so the game should log head and gaze-proxy behaviour and flag such play.
- Space tier: seated (table, screen 48 cm away, start block 27 cm in front of it).
- VR or mixed reality: mixed reality suits the original slightly better: a seated table task with a real start block. On Quest 3 the start pad can sit on the player's real table and the response panel can float above it. In both VR and MR the reach ends in mid-air instead of on the original's Plexiglass screen, a small change (no touch at the end of the reach).
- Quest 3 feasibility: feasible. Controller tracking is precise enough for path curvature (the original sampled at 160 Hz, Quest at 72–120 Hz). The index fingertip from hand tracking would be closer to the original's finger marker but is noisier. Movement onset uses a speed threshold of 25.4 cm/s; curvature is the maximum deviation divided by path length. All of it is automatic.
- Replication: the Stroop effect is one of the most replicated findings in psychology (MacLeod 1991 review; reproduced in Many Labs 3). Hand-path curvature toward the distractor was found here for both Stroop and flanker, and in later reach- and mouse-tracking work. The trial-to-trial sequence effects reported here are disputed (repetition and learning confounds; the paper's own flanker pattern changed when repetitions were included), so the reveal should use only the basic congruent/incongruent difference and the bend.
- Ethics: none.
- Verdict: room. It is seated, automatic and robust, with a vivid personal replay of the hand pulled by the word. It could be a first-room candidate if the replay looks convincing on Quest; many players already know Stroop, which lowers the surprise.
