# shams-2000 — One flash, two beeps, two flashes (sound-induced flash illusion)

- paper: shams-2000.txt
- citation: Shams, Kamitani & Shimojo (2000). What you see is what you hear. Nature, 408, 788 (14 December 2000).
- status: read

## Facts
| What | Value | Quote (verbatim from the .txt) | Page |
|---|---|---|---|
| Participants | 8 naive observers; each combination repeated 5 times | "The trials were randomized and each stimulus combination was run five times on eight naive observers." | 788 |
| Procedure: picture | White disk, 2° wide at 5° from the centre, flashed 1–4 times, 50 ms apart, on black | "flashing a uniform white disk (subtending 2 degrees at 5 degrees eccentricity) for a variable number of times (50 milliseconds apart) on a black background" | 788 |
| Procedure: sound | Beeps 57 ms apart, number varied | "Flashes were accompanied by a variable number of beeps, each spaced 57 milliseconds apart." | 788 |
| Procedure: task | Count the flashes | "Observers were asked to judge how many visual flashes were presented on each trial." | 788 |
| Duration | Not stated (number of trials and session length not given) | "The trials were randomized and each stimulus combination was run five times on eight naive observers." | 788 |
| Main result | One flash with two or more beeps is seen as several flashes | "observers consistently and incorrectly reported seeing multiple flashes whenever a single flash was accompanied by more than one beep" | 788 |
| Main result: strength | The illusory double flash looks like a real double flash | "suggesting that the illusory double flash is perceptually equivalent to the physical double flash" | 788 |
| Knowing does not help | Persisted in observers who knew there was one flash (data not shown) | "the illusion persisted even when subjects were aware of the fact that the disk was actually flashed only once." | 788 |
| Timing window | Works while beep and flash are within about 100 ms | "illusory flashing occurred as long as the beep and flash were within approximately 100 milliseconds of each other" | 788 |
| One direction only | Two flashes with one beep are not fused into one | "sound did not have a fusing effect when multiple flashes were accompanied by a single beep" | 788 |
| Controls | Catch trials rule out guessing from the sound | "Control conditions and catch trials (Fig. 1 legend) indicate that the illusory flashing phenomenon is a perceptual illusion" | 788 |
| Robustness | Survived changes of many stimulus settings | "The illusion is also surprisingly robust to variation in the many parameters we manipulated" | 788 |

## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: not stated; with 1 flash × 0–4 beeps plus controls, 5 repeats each, likely 5–10 min.
- Task/tension: clear but dry — count flashes and press a button; no story tension by itself.
- Within-person reveal: strong — replay the same trial with sound off (one flash), then on (two again); since it reportedly survives knowing, the player can feel the illusion while knowing the truth.
- Works if the player expects tricks: yes per the paper, but that claim rests on non-naive data "not shown".
- Space tier: seated (a screen task; posture not stated in the paper).
- Quest 3 feasibility: picture side is easy (50 ms is 4–6 frames at 72–120 Hz; the disk can be head-locked to keep 5° eccentricity, but fixation cannot be checked without eye tracking). The risk is sound-to-picture delay in the Quest browser: the illusion needs the beep within about 100 ms of the flash, so the delay must be measured on the headset (light sensor + microphone) before building. Answers are button presses, so measurement is automatic.
- Replication: a one-page report with 8 observers; independent replications are not checked in this card.
- Ethics: brief flashing light — show a photosensitivity warning first.
- Verdict: first-room candidate — seated, short, measured by button presses and reported to survive knowing the trick; build only after the headset's sound-to-picture delay is measured.
