## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: duration not stated; three short text vignettes with a few questions each — likely 10–15 min including demographics; the core judgments are a few minutes.
- Task/tension: low — reading short stories and judging "knows / only believes". In VR the stories could be lived (the player sees the squirrel, the fake barns, the diamond), which is a different experiment from reading vignettes.
- Within-person reveal: weak — each player gives three judgments; the effect is a group difference (Gettier vs. knowledge condition), so one player cannot see "their" effect.
- Works if the player expects tricks: probably yes — it asks for a judgment, not a perception that can be spoiled; but players who know the Gettier problem (philosophy students) answer differently, and nothing in the design catches them except comprehension and hypothesis-guess checks.
- Space tier: seated (online/lab text survey; no movement in the procedure).
- Quest 3 feasibility: easy as text or narrated panels with a slider; automatic logging. Turning the vignettes into lived 3D scenes would add vision and agency and must be treated as a new conceptual replication, not as PSA004.
- Replication: strong — a registered, multi-lab, 37-site replication; the effect is real but small and story-dependent; almost half of completers failed comprehension checks.
- What it would take to call it science: keep the registered design (three vignettes, within-person, randomised condition per vignette, same know/believe question and comprehension checks), pre-register exclusions, and plan a sample in the thousands, because the effect is small (odds ratio about 1.9) and comprehension losses are large. A VR "lived" version would need its own pre-registration and a text-vignette control arm.
- Ethics: none special.
- Verdict: reject as a room — a small, between-condition effect from reading stories, with no within-person reveal and heavy comprehension losses; at most a short text interlude for collecting data at scale.
