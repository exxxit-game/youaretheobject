## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: the original is 720 trials (time not stated, probably 40–60 min); a game would need far fewer, and the effect (about 5 percentage points) would then be hard to see per player.
- Task/tension: none; repeated size judgments.
- Within-person reveal: weak. The design is within-person, but the shift is small (.51 vs .46), shown by 59–69% of participants, and needs hundreds of trials to see.
- Works if the player expects tricks: probably yes for a perceptual judgment, but the effect is too small to matter.
- Space tier: seated.
- VR or mixed reality: neither adds to the original (rectangles on a screen). A shop-shelf version with 3D products would be a different experiment, and visual product attributes are where consumer decoy effects are reported to weaken.
- Quest 3 feasibility: trivial technically.
- Replication: contested. Later perceptual work reported the reverse ("repulsion") effect with other display layouts (Spektor, Kellen & Hotaling 2018, and a 2025 preprint titled "Perceptual decoys do not reliably bias choice"; seen as search snippets, not read). The consumer attraction effect itself is known to weaken with visual or realistic stimuli (Frederick, Lee & Baskin 2014, not read).
- Ethics: none.
- Verdict: reject: small, fragile and display-dependent; it needs hundreds of trials and does not use the headset.
