## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: the whole session was 50 min including the real-room half; the 27 VR trials alone are probably 10–15 min.
- Task/tension: look at a disk on the floor, go dark, imagine walking to it, press stop. Calm and repetitive; little tension.
- Within-person reveal: moderate — the player's own "real vs headset" gap (about 7% on average here) and how their distances shrink as the view narrows.
- Works if the player expects tricks: partly; imagined-walking time is under voluntary control, so a player who knows the hypothesis can push it.
- Space tier: large (a 10 m straight walk for the walking-speed calibration; the trials themselves are standing in a 2 × 6.5 m strip, with targets up to 5 m away).
- VR or mixed reality: the original compares a real room with its VR copy. On Quest 3 the "real" side can only be camera passthrough, which is not the same as bare eyes — but it is exactly the paper's future-work request of the same headset weight in both conditions.
- Quest 3 feasibility: the VR side is easy (aperture mask for the field of view, button for the stopwatch, head pitch logged to reject trials where the player looks down). The mixed-reality side needs about 5 m of clear real floor in front of the player, and the 10 m walking-speed calibration needs even more; most homes lack both.
- Replication: VR distance compression is widely reported (the paper cites Renner et al. 2013, about 27% on average); here it was smaller (7%) and both conditions were overestimated, unlike earlier work.
- Ethics: none special.
- Verdict: room (large space only for the real/passthrough comparison) — a clean MR-vs-VR question, but weak tension and a voluntary measure; the VR-only field-of-view part could be a standing interlude.
