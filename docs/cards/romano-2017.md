## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: not stated. 14 quick decisions after short instructions, probably 5–10 minutes. Fits as a short piece.
- Task/tension: mild: "how much do I send to someone from country X?". The tension is moral more than strategic, since the outcomes were mostly hypothetical.
- Within-person reveal: good. All 14 decisions are within-person, so the player's own "home country minus foreigner" gap and their reputation effect (partner knows vs does not know) can be shown next to the paper's averages.
- Works if the player expects tricks: partly. The labels make the hypothesis obvious, and a player can hide favoritism. The original had the same transparency and still found the effect.
- Live players needed: 0 at the same time. The original is asynchronous (one-shot decisions, strategy method, partners matched afterwards only in the paid test). A game could add real partners from other countries online at the same moment, but that is a new design, not this one.
- Space tier: seated.
- VR or mixed reality: neither suits better; the original is an online survey. A desktop version is closest. Showing the partner as an avatar would add presence, which the original did not have.
- Quest 3 feasibility: easy: sliders for amounts, all logged automatically. The real requirement is the player's nationality, which is personal data.
- Replication: one large study in 17 countries. Ingroup favoritism in cooperation is supported by a meta-analysis of more than 100 studies that the paper cites; independent replications of this study are not checked here.
- Ethics: no deception if partners are real or decisions are labelled hypothetical. Nationality must be optional, asked with consent, stored coarsely (country only) and never shown to other players unless the condition says so and the player agreed.
- Verdict: interlude — short, within-person and fully automatic, with a clear "you vs your country" reveal; it needs no live players as published, and nationality is sensitive data.
