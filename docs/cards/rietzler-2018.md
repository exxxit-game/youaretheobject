## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: the lift-and-compare part of Study 1 is a few minutes; a bowling game about 4 min; a 20–30-trial version of the two-choice task about 5 min (estimate; the original 120 trials are too long).
- Task/tension: game-like (lifting, bowling); weight can work as a natural obstacle.
- Within-person reveal: weak — the offset is visible by design, so "your real hand was 8 cm higher" is little surprise to a player who already saw the lag.
- Works if the player expects tricks: partly — the method does not depend on hiding, but a suspicious player will read it as a visual sign rather than feel weight; even the authors call it metaphorical.
- Space tier: standing (space not stated; no walking is described — lifting spheres and bowling with balls reset in place).
- Quest 3 feasibility: very good — built for consumer controllers, software only, no eye tracking or force feedback.
- Replication: two small studies (10 and 18) in one paper; no independent replication in the text.
- Ethics: none special; long holds with large offsets may tire the arm (authors suggest staying near 24 cm).
- Verdict: interlude — trivially feasible and fun inside a game moment, but it is an openly visible metaphor rather than a hidden illusion, so the reveal is weak.
