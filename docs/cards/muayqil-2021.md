## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: 36 lines at own pace, probably 3–5 minutes.
- Task/tension: none; a calm precision task. Interest comes only from the reveal and the comparison with others.
- Within-person reveal: yes — the game measures every mark and can show the player's average shift (left or right of true centre, in % of half-line) and their line-by-line marks. The cultural difference is a between-player statistic.
- Works if the player expects tricks: probably yes; the bias is about 1% of the half-line, too small to steer deliberately without measuring.
- Space tier: seated (sheet on a table at 30–45 cm).
- VR or mixed reality: mixed reality suits the original better: the lines can lie on the player's real table and be marked with a fingertip (hand tracking) or the controller tip touching the real surface, close to pen on paper. In VR the "paper" floats or lies on a virtual table and the finger passes through it.
- Quest 3 feasibility: good; plane detection or hit-testing puts the sheet on the real table; marks are logged automatically. Precision of hand tracking (millimetres matter: 1% of a 6 cm line is 0.3 mm of a half-line of 3 cm) is unverified on our headset and must be measured first; longer virtual lines reduce the problem but change the stimulus.
- Replication: leftward pseudoneglect in healthy people is the well-known baseline (the paper cites the Jewell & McCourt 2000 meta-analysis); the reading-direction/culture modulation has supporting studies (Chokron & Imbert 1993, cited) but the paper itself notes conflicting results for line position; this is a single cross-sectional comparison where language direction, religion-linked right-favouring and ethnicity are confounded.
- Ethics: only reading direction and handedness should be asked, never ethnicity or religion; results shown as group averages.
- Verdict: interlude — a short, calm, automatic task with a personal number; the cultural comparison becomes a game-wide statistic once players with right-to-left scripts are present.
