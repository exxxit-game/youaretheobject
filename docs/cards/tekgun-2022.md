## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: probably 10–15 minutes for one posture (baseline, 1 min body adaptation, 1 min touch, elevation, five drops, questions); the paper does not state it.
- Task/tension: strong scene: seeing your own body from far above in the dark. The measure (imagined ball drops) is calm and implicit.
- Within-person reveal: yes — the player's own drop times before and after the elevation, against the real fall time from 14 m (1.689 s), are personal numbers.
- Works if the player expects tricks: partly; a player who knows the design can deliberately lengthen the imagined fall. The measure is implicit but not protected against strategy.
- Space tier: standing (feet stationary; the supine condition needs a bed or stretcher and is not offered).
- VR or mixed reality: VR; the room is deliberately dark and boundless, and the elevated viewpoint cannot exist in passthrough (a web game has no camera frames to lift). Mixed reality only for the baseline drops in the real room, which the paper did with eyes closed.
- Quest 3 feasibility: partial. The original needs an experimenter touching the abdomen in sync with the visual touch; at home the closest version is self-touch (the player taps their own abdomen with the tracked controller and sees the virtual controller do the same) or visuo-motor synchrony — both are different inductions and need their own evidence. Quest 3's field of view is much narrower than the 200° headset. Drop times are logged automatically (button press and release). Full-body avatar must be animated from head and hands only.
- Replication: the elevated-viewpoint increase in drop times follows Lenggenhager et al. 2009 and Bourdin et al. 2017 (cited, not carded); this study (N = 30, one lab) adds the posture comparison with one-sided tests; the authors ask for replication.
- Ethics: strong out-of-body imagery; warn players with vertigo or fear of heights; standing in the dark needs the boundary and a quick exit.
- Verdict: room — only as a standing, self-touch variant clearly labelled as different from the original; the supine part is not offered.
