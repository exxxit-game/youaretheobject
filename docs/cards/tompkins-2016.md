## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: five clips under 30 s each plus answers, about 5 min.
- Task/tension: watch a magician and describe what happened; no task tension, the surprise is in the reveal.
- Within-person reveal: yes for those who fall for it — the player's own words ("he took out a coin") next to a frame-by-frame replay of an empty hand. Only about a third get this moment (11% with a named object); the rest have nothing to be revealed.
- Works if the player expects tricks: partly — participants were told they were watching magic tricks and 32% still reported a phantom object; the non-magic control drew none.
- Space tier: seated (online, own computer).
- Quest 3 feasibility: the original was a flat, silent video on the participant's own screen, so a video screen inside the headset is a faithful copy; a 3D animated magician would be a new experiment, since the effect rests on a skilled magician's mime. The hard part is the measure: free written descriptions judged by people. Typing in VR is clumsy; spoken description with speech-to-text and automatic coding is possible but untested; a multiple-choice "what did he take out?" would be the leading question the authors deliberately avoided.
- Replication: one study (420 online); a related effect (vanishing ball, 63%) is cited; no replication of the Phantom Vanish itself in the paper.
- Ethics: none special; the authors' videos would need their permission to reuse.
- Verdict: interlude — short, seated, faithful as an in-headset video, clean replay reveal, but only a third fall for it and the free-description measure is hard to automate without leading questions.
