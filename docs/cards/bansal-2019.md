## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: about 50 min (three 10-min time tests and two 10-min game sessions). Shortening it would change the dose of exposure, so it would be a different experiment.
- Task/tension: the exposure is fun (a world that freezes when you freeze), but the measure is a dry reproduction task on a laptop.
- Within-person reveal: weak. Each player has a before/after score, but the change appeared only for the continuous test at 3–6 s, the overall effect of the manipulation was not significant, and groups were 12–13 people.
- Works if the player expects tricks: unknown; not tested.
- Space tier: large 2.4 × 2.4 m (the calibrated capture space; the paper gives no smaller number, though the game is mostly played standing).
- VR or mixed reality: VR, as in the paper (a full game world whose speed is tied to the player's movement). Slowing virtual objects inside the player's real room in mixed reality would be untested.
- Quest 3 feasibility: the time-flow mechanic is easy (scale the simulation speed by head and hand speed). But the original measured time outside VR on a laptop with a mouse; doing the test inside the headset would be a change.
- Replication: none found; one small study. A related VR time claim, that time flies in VR (Mullen & Davidenko 2021), failed to replicate in two experiments by the same group (Mullen, Davidenko & Kingstone 2025, Timing & Time Perception; abstract read, the publisher blocks scripted download of the full text). Time effects specific to VR look fragile.
- Ethics: a fast shooter game; check for motion sickness.
- Verdict: reject. It is long, small, not replicated, and the effect showed only in one sub-condition. The "time moves when you move" mechanic is worth keeping as a game idea, not as a replication room.
