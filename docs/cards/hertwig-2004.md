## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: not stated; 3 problems with a median of 15 draws each is likely 3–5 min.
- Task/tension: low to mild — draw from two boxes, then pick one to "play for real"; tension depends on a stake the game cannot provide (the paper paid real money).
- Within-person reveal: the headline effect (experience vs description) is between groups — one player cannot see it in themselves. What one player can see: the hidden distribution of each box next to their own draws ("you drew 7 times and never saw the 32, which was there 1 time in 10"). That shows the mechanism (small samples), not the gap.
- Works if the player expects tricks: doubtful — a suspicious player will draw many times, and with large samples the rare event shows up and the effect shrinks; the effect relies on short, self-chosen search.
- Space tier: seated (computer screen with two buttons).
- Quest 3 feasibility: easy — two boxes or urns, every draw and the final choice logged automatically. No real-money payoff; points instead change the incentive.
- Replication: the paper reports agreement with Barron & Erev (2003), r = .93 between experience-group choice rates.
- Ethics: none special; no deception.
- Verdict: interlude — a short seated sampling game with an automatic personal reveal of the rare outcome the player never drew; the paper's main finding is between groups and needs real stakes.
