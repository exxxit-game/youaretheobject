## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: one session of 3–6.5 minutes, plus watching the previous person for one session (another 3–6.5 minutes).
- Task/tension: low; "find out how to earn points". The interest is in the reveal: the points never depended on you.
- Within-person reveal: yes — the log shows the player's clicks in the "points" colour versus the "no points" colour and their 0–10 control rating next to the true control (none). The chain part is a group-level effect (alone vs after a model), shown as "you vs others".
- Works if the player expects tricks: doubtful; a suspicious player may test for randomness. In the paper the alone group's superstition already faded within sessions; Ono (1987, room 01) found persistent superstition in only 3 of 20.
- Space tier: seated (computer task).
- VR or mixed reality: either; the original is a computer screen in a room, so a booth in VR or a panel on the player's own table in mixed reality are both close. Watching the previous person is the new part: a live observer as in the paper needs two players at once; replaying the previous player's recorded hands and clicks as a "ghost" is a variant (a recording, not a live person) and should be labelled as such.
- Quest 3 feasibility: easy for the task itself (the Ono booth, one button and a counter, already exists). The chain needs either two simultaneous players (Supabase realtime) or recorded replays; the first model in every chain is scripted (a confederate), which is a disclosed deception.
- Replication: none independent; N = 38, small groups, two chain conditions with different timings. Related: the same lab's 2017 study (alone, not carded), Higgins et al. 1989 with children (cited, not carded).
- Ethics: a scripted first model is a deception; debrief at the reveal. Recording and replaying another player's hands needs consent and must not carry identity.
- Verdict: room — revives the parked Ono booth with short sessions and a social, multi-player twist; evidence is a single small study, so the reveal must say so.
