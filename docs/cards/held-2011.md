## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: not stated; a match-to-sample block with 20 shape pairs per condition would take minutes.
- Task/tension: a simple two-choice matching task. The drama lies in who the participants are (children seeing for the first time), not in the task.
- Within-person reveal: within each patient, yes (98% and 92% within one sense against 58% across senses). A sighted player already has the touch–vision link, so they would score at ceiling in all three conditions, and there is nothing to reveal.
- Works if the player expects tricks: irrelevant. The effect is a property of newly sighted people, not a trick.
- Space tier: seated (table-top objects at about 30 cm; posture not stated in the paper).
- VR or mixed reality: neither reproduces it. VR has no touch at all. Mixed reality would let a player feel real objects in their room, but there is no standard shape set and no unseen haptic presentation. The participants are the missing ingredient in either case.
- Quest 3 feasibility: not feasible as a replication. Hand tracking and controllers give no shape by touch (no force feedback), and home players are not newly sighted.
- Replication: one study of 5 patients (3 retested) by one group; the paper itself says earlier neonate studies were hard to replicate.
- Ethics: not applicable to the game; the original involved patients after surgery.
- Verdict: reject — the effect exists only in newly sighted patients, and Quest 3 cannot present shapes by touch; at most a story or philosophy moment about Molyneux's question, with no data to collect.
