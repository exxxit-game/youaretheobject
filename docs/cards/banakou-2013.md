## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: 45–60 min per session and two sessions a week apart. One body block (baseline estimates, 5 min in the body, estimates again) would be about 10 min; both bodies in one sitting about 20 min (estimate; Exp. 2 did both bodies in one session, but out of sync).
- Task/tension: show cube sizes with your hands — simple and physical, little tension.
- Within-person reveal: good — the game can show the player their own hand spans before and after ("you showed the 30-cm cube as N cm while you were small").
- Works if the player expects tricks: unknown; the size measure is indirect, but a suspicious player might correct deliberately; not tested.
- Space tier: standing (one marked spot, no walking).
- Quest 3 feasibility: partial — the hand-span measure is automatic with controllers or hand tracking, and lowering the eye height to 91.5 cm is software. But the original tracked the whole body with 34 markers; Quest 3 gives head and hands and only estimates the legs, while Exp. 2 shows that an out-of-sync body removes the effect — a head-and-hands body is untested. The child-vs-adult comparison needs both bodies, which does not fit well into 15 min. The IAT needs personal details typed in first.
- Replication: in-paper control (Exp. 2) only; no independent replication of the child effect in the text (the shrunk-body overestimation agrees with earlier work it cites).
- Ethics: avoid collecting personal details for the IAT; do not copy the "you could fail" pressure of the door task; being in a child body is mild but should be explained.
- Verdict: room — a clean, automatic within-person size measure while standing on one spot, but a synchronous body from head-and-hands tracking and fitting both bodies into 15 min are untested.
