## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: not stated. The errand (enter, fetch a watering can, find a room, water plants, leave) suggests 5–10 minutes with training. My estimate.
- Task/tension: a calm errand, then an alarm and the words "leave the building". No fire, smoke or other people, so tension is low.
- Within-person reveal: one decision (the way back or the signed way) and one door. A replay from above can show the player's route against the signed route. The paper gives the condition effects only as model numbers and figures; the share who retraced is not in the text, so the reveal has no plain number to compare with.
- Works if the player expects tricks: badly. The participants were kept unaware that an evacuation would come. A player who expects an emergency will look for signs on the way in, which changes the very thing measured.
- Space tier: seated. Travel was by keyboard and mouse (whether people sat is not stated); nobody walked.
- VR or mixed reality: VR. The original is a virtual hotel the player must not know beforehand; the player's own room cannot stand in for an unfamiliar building.
- Quest 3 feasibility: easy: a small hotel, thumbstick travel instead of keyboard and mouse, position logging. Expect motion sickness: 9 of 81 stopped in the original with artificial travel.
- Replication: one study, about 18 per version. The manipulated effect (corridor width) is just under p = .05. The sign effect rests on self-reported noticing, so it is not a tested effect of the signs themselves.
- Ethics: an alarm only; motion sickness was the main harm. Keeping the purpose hidden needs a debrief.
- Verdict: reject — the manipulated effect is marginal (p = .048, about 18 per version), the sign effect is self-reported, and the design needs players who do not expect an evacuation.
