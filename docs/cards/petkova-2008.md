## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: 5–10 minutes (two or four 2-minute handshake periods plus a questionnaire).
- Task/tension: two people shake hands and squeeze in time with a metronome; the tension is the strange moment of seeing your own body from outside, from the partner's eyes.
- Within-person reveal: yes, subjectively ("I was shaking hands with myself"); the measurable part is the questionnaire and, in the game, possibly the reach or flinch of each hand when a virtual knife appears (the paper used skin conductance, which the headset cannot measure).
- Works if the player expects tricks: probably; the illusion comes from what the eyes and hand feel, but questionnaire answers are easy to bias.
- Space tier: standing (two people face to face at arm's length; no walking).
- VR or mixed reality: the original is a live video "passthrough" from someone else's head. The Quest browser cannot send camera images, so the game can only render the scene from the partner's tracked head pose with avatars; that is VR with two co-located players whose real hands really touch. Mixed reality as such does not reproduce it.
- Quest 3 feasibility: possible only for two headsets in the same room with a shared coordinate frame (both players' head and hand tracking streamed live, under about 50 ms). Each player sees the scene from the other's head: their own avatar from outside and the partner's arm in front of their eyes. The real handshake supplies the synchronous touch. Skin conductance is unavailable; the questionnaire (Exp. 1 statements) is the measure. A one-headset version needs a helper who strokes the player's belly with a tracked controller while the player sees a virtual body being stroked (the mannequin experiment).
- Replication: in the paper, five experiments with separate naive groups; full-body ownership illusions by synchronous touch have since been reproduced by many groups (reviewer's knowledge).
- Ethics: the knife threat is mild but should be announced in the consent; the swap may feel uncanny for some players, so a stop button is needed.
- Verdict: room (two co-located headsets only) — a striking two-player body experiment, but rare at home and without its objective measure; an interlude version with a helper is the realistic first step.
