## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: 35–45 min including the questionnaire, with 192 trials of about 5 s (1 s fixation, 2 s display, 2 s gap). About 64–96 trials would take 5–8 min.
- Task/tension: mild. Spot yoghurt or soda on a kitchen counter as fast as possible.
- Within-person reveal: modest. "The object at the side, which you were told to ignore, slowed you by X ms." The millisecond values are only in the figures, not the text. With about 24 trials per cell the player's own difference should usually show, but the effect is a delay, not a visible mistake. The reaching version (erb-2016) makes the same conflict visible in the hand path.
- Works if the player expects tricks: yes. The flanker effect occurs despite the instruction to ignore, and the eye-tracking experiment suggests it works without looking at the flanker.
- Space tier: standing (on one carpet tile, no walking).
- VR or mixed reality: VR, as in the original virtual kitchen. But the original was a CAVE, where people see their own body, not a headset. Mixed reality, with objects on the player's real kitchen counter or table, would fit the paper's own goal of daily-life validity, but it is untested.
- Quest 3 feasibility: easy. Objects on a counter 1.1 m from the eyes and two controller buttons; the 2 s displays have no frame-timing risk. Fixation on the central cup cannot be checked without eye tracking; the VR experiment did not check it either.
- Replication: the flanker effect is among the most robust in attention research. Within this paper the VR effects were reproduced on a 2D screen with no difference between the two. Whether people's daily-life distractibility (CFQ) relates to the effect: no, in both experiments.
- Ethics: none.
- Verdict: interlude. It is robust and easy, but the reveal is a small delay. It is better used as a short task or combined with the reaching version for a visible personal effect.
