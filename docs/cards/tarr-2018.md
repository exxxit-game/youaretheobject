## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: 3 minutes of movement plus a short questionnaire; about 5–7 minutes.
- Task/tension: none; a slow group exercise. The manipulation is hidden (the "others" are delayed copies of you).
- Within-person reveal: weak. Synchrony was between groups and the result is a self-report; the hidden behavioural measures (mimicry, distance) showed nothing. A strong reveal is still possible in a different form ("the two people were you, delayed by half a second"), but that reveal is not a result of the paper.
- Works if the player expects tricks: partly. In the paper most participants already did not believe the characters were people and the closeness effect still appeared; a player hunting for tricks may spot that the partners copy them.
- Space tier: standing (in place in a triangle; the lab was 2.96 × 3.4 m but the task needs no walking).
- VR or mixed reality: VR suits the original (a virtual room with a virtual mirror and avatars). Mixed reality could put the two partners in the player's room, but the player's own body would then be their real body, not an avatar, so the "cloned body" logic changes.
- Quest 3 feasibility: feasible for arm movements: head and two hands (controllers or hand tracking) drive an upper-body avatar by inverse kinematics; the original used a 37-marker full-body suit. Cloning with delay and logging distance and mimicry are automatic; the main measure is a rating, which the game can ask in VR.
- Replication: one VR study; it fits a larger literature on synchrony and affiliation outside VR (several studies cited; reviews and meta-analyses not read here). The behavioural measures did not replicate the self-report.
- Ethics: mild deception (cover story; implied that others would join); a debrief is needed.
- Verdict: interlude: VR-native and short, but the effect is between groups and self-reported, and the hidden behavioural measures were null; best as a "you vs others" closeness statistic with the delayed-copy reveal.
