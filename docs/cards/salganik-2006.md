- status: read

## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: self-paced and short (a few songs), perhaps 5–10 minutes per player.
- Task/tension: low: browse and listen to songs, download the ones you like.
- Within-person reveal: none in the paper's sense; the result exists only at the level of worlds of thousands of people. The reveal for one player is social: "in your world this song was number 1; in the world next door it was number 30", and "you chose the songs others had chosen".
- Works if the player expects tricks: yes; seeing download counts is not a trick, and the effect is about the crowd.
- Space tier: seated.
- VR or mixed reality: neither adds much (the original was a web page); a virtual record shop with spatial sound is a change of presentation, not of method.
- Quest 3 feasibility: technically simple (audio, a list, counters), but the design needs a separate server-side "world" per group and many thousands of players over weeks (the paper ran 69 and 83 days); the game's cross-player statistics exist but sending is off until the privacy page, and the songs need licences.
- Replication: a famous single study with two experiments; the same group's later MusicLab experiments built on it (not read here).
- Ethics: harmless; licensed music, anonymous counts only.
- Verdict: reject for now (needs thousands of players in parallel worlds and licensed music; nothing for a single player to see in themselves); reconsider only as a long-running background feature once the game has a large player base.
