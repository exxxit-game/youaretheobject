## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: about 9–10 minutes (30 rounds).
- Task/tension: a slot machine with a private choice and a temptation in every round; the tension is moral, and the player knows nobody can check a single round.
- Within-person reveal: medium. One player's 30 rounds give a statistical picture (an honest player claims the higher slot about 15 times; 20 or more is under 5% likely by chance). The observer effect itself was between groups and small (only active vs passive differed), so one player cannot see "the watcher made me honest".
- Works if the player expects tricks: weakly. In a game with points and no money there is little reason not to claim the higher slot, and a suspicious player may play "honest" for the score of the reveal; the paper's real-euro stake cannot be reproduced.
- Space tier: seated.
- VR or mixed reality: VR suits the original (a virtual pub). Mixed reality could place the slot machine and the watching avatar in the player's own room, which may make the observer feel more present; untested.
- Quest 3 feasibility: easy. Pointing and clicking with a controller, an avatar with head-follow gaze (head tracking gives the gaze target), automatic logging. The original's projection room had no headset weight or face cover; otherwise close.
- Replication: the cheating level matches the desktop original (Jiang 2013), so the "most people cheat a little" part is robust; the virtual-observer effect is a single study with a mixed, partly null result.
- Ethics: sensitive. The reveal tells a player, in effect, "you probably lied"; it must stay statistical, never single out a round, and should not be stored or shared per person. With no money, the "lie" costs nothing, which makes it less fraught but also less meaningful.
- Verdict: interlude: a 10-minute VR dishonesty task that is easy to build, but the paper's key manipulation is between groups and largely null, and without real money the incentive to lie is different; useful mainly as a "you vs others" statistic.
