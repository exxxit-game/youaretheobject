## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: the original is about an hour; a game duel would need to be cut to 10–15 minutes (perhaps 40–60 duels), which gives fewer reactive and initiated trials per player than the paper's 100–170.
- Task/tension: strong and playful: a western duel at a table against a virtual opponent, "draw" by hitting three buttons.
- Within-person reveal: good in principle (every player has both kinds of trials, so "your hand moved 10% faster when you reacted, yet you still lost because reacting took 200 ms"). The 21 ms effect is small: Quest tracking frames are 11–14 ms apart, so movement times must be measured from interpolated hand paths or contact times, and the per-player difference with fewer trials may be noisy.
- Works if the player expects tricks: yes; the advantage is an automatic motor effect, and in Exp. 3 it held against a known computer.
- Space tier: seated (at a table, buttons within 35 cm).
- VR or mixed reality: mixed reality suits the original better: the buttons can sit on the player's real table, giving a real surface to touch, as the original touch buttons did; the opponent can be a virtual figure across the table (Exp. 3 shows a non-human opponent works).
- Quest 3 feasibility: feasible: hand tracking or controllers, virtual buttons on a detected table, a computer opponent that replays the player's own earlier timing as in Exp. 3, all measured automatically. Without a physical surface (pure VR), hand movements end in the air and timing is fuzzier.
- Replication: replicated by several later studies with button tasks and with karate punches and side-steps (listed in Wakatsuki & Yamada 2020, Frontiers in Psychology, intro read, not carded), always with the same caveat that the gain is smaller than reaction time.
- Ethics: none special (a toy duel; no weapons need to be shown, buttons suffice).
- Verdict: room (pilot first): a game-like, within-person, automatic motor effect that survives a computer opponent; the effect is about 21 ms, so the room depends on a pilot showing Quest timing and a shortened session still reveal it per player.
