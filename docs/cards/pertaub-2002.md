## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: one 5-min talk — fits; the original's days-ahead preparation must become an in-game topic with prompts.
- Task/tension: high for many people — speak aloud for five minutes while eight listeners visibly lose interest or turn hostile.
- Within-person reveal: weak. The paper's result is between groups (first talk only) and rests on questionnaires (PRCS, somatic checklist, self-rating). Nothing the paper measured is automatic. Voice pauses, speech time or head turning away from the audience could be logged on Quest, but the paper did not measure them, so there is no published effect to reveal.
- Works if the player expects tricks: partly. The hostility is overt and participants knew the audience was virtual, yet anxiety rose; a player who expects "the audience will be mean" may brace and be less affected. Unknown.
- Space tier: standing (HMD group spoke standing in place).
- Quest 3 feasibility: good technically — standing, eight animated avatars, recorded comments; the hidden operator's timing can be replaced by microphone voice-activity triggers (a change, but a small one). No eye tracking needed: the avatars' gaze follows the player's head, as in the original.
- Replication: only the same lab's smaller pilot; cells are tiny (Table 1: 2–5 people per audience × display × gender cell); half the sample used a desktop monitor, not an HMD.
- Ethics: deliberately raises anxiety; the paper screened out psychological disorders and always ended on a friendly audience. At home there is no screening: needs an opt-out, a content note for social anxiety, the same friendly-audience ending, and voice kept on the device.
- Verdict: interlude — a strong standing scene that is safe with the paper's friendly ending, but its effect is self-reported and between groups, so there is no automatic, personal result to reveal.
