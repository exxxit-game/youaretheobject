## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: seconds per pick; Study 2's 28 trials would take a few minutes. As used in a room, under 2 minutes.
- Task/tension: "pick any card" — almost no tension; the hook is the magician revealing the card.
- Within-person reveal: yes — a slow-motion replay of the riffle shows that the player's card was the one held longest; works only for players who took the target (98% live, 30% on a screen).
- Works if the player expects tricks: doubtful — the authors avoided the word "magic" in Study 2 because people are more sceptical of magic tasks; on screen most participants noticed the long card when asked directly. A single one-shot riffle is safer than 28 trials.
- Space tier: standing (in place) — Study 1 on the street, posture not stated; Study 2 at a desktop computer; no walking in either.
- Quest 3 feasibility: a virtual magician riffling a 3D deck at eye level is easy to build and the choice can be recorded automatically (pick the card from a spread). The original strong result (98%) was live with a real magician; the on-screen version fell to 30%; a VR avatar sits between the two and its rate is unknown. Study 2's shortest timings (10–20 ms masks) are close to a single headset frame, so that version can only be approximated.
- Replication: two studies in this paper; an earlier lab found 45% live; no independent replication of 98%.
- Ethics: none special.
- Verdict: interlude — a 30-second card force with a slow-motion replay reveal; strong live but only 30% on a screen, so a VR version is uncertain and cannot carry a room alone.
