## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: not stated. Three rounds of 24 two-second prompts plus one minute of turning and the learning walk — probably 8–12 minutes.
- Task/tension: learn where six things are, then point to them blind; the spin makes the player feel lost. Mild tension.
- Within-person reveal: yes. A top view of the player's "star" of pointing lines before and after the spin: after spinning the star is not just rotated, it is bent; pointing to the corners of the room (Exp. 6) stays intact. Automatic and personal.
- Works if the player expects tricks: probably yes — this is a limit of updating, not a deception; knowing about it should not keep your sense of the objects after a minute of blind turning. Not tested.
- Space tier: standing (Exp. 1 pointed from the centre of a 1.9 × 1.9 m chamber; Exp. 2 was seated on a swivel chair). The learning walk around the chamber was in a larger room whose size is not stated.
- VR or mixed reality: mixed reality suits the original better. The targets were real objects around the participant in a real room, and the corners task used the real walls. In MR the player learns virtual objects placed among their own furniture and points to the real corners of their own room (plane detection gives the walls, so corner directions can be scored). A fully virtual booth has to invent the room, and whether a layout learnt in a virtual room is held like a real one is exactly what is uncertain. During the test the screen is black in both, so MR's gain is in the learning phase and in the corners task. Exp. 7 already used an ordinary furnished room with tables and a couch, which is close to a player's own room.
- Quest 3 feasibility: pointing with a controller and heading from the headset — automatic and more precise than video coding. One minute of spinning in a headset is uncomfortable and a fall risk; Exp. 2's swivel chair gave the same result and is the version to build. Corners need the walls of the player's room (plane detection: Meta status, unverified on our headset).
- Replication: inside the paper (Exp. 2, and Exps. 5–7 extending it), each with 8–10 people. Mou et al. (2004; card mou-2004) dispute the interpretation; an independent replication of the configuration-error rise itself was not checked.
- Ethics: dizziness; use the seated swivel version and warn players prone to motion sickness.
- Verdict: room — short, automatic, a personal before/after-spin picture, and closer in MR (real room, real corners) than in a virtual booth; but samples of 10, a disputed interpretation, and blind spinning in a headset needs the seated version.
