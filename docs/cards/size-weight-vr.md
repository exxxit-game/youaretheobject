## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: one condition is 37 lifts with a 2 s hold and a spoken rating each, likely about 10 min.
- Task/tension: low — lift, hold, say a number; the surprise is only "the small and the big ones weighed the same".
- Within-person reveal: possible in principle (show the true masses), but only if the player really lifted weighted objects.
- Works if the player expects tricks: not tested in this paper.
- Space tier: seated (at a table, height-adjustable chair).
- Quest 3 feasibility: not possible as published — it needs four physical cylinders of set sizes and masses, each tracked, placed by an experimenter between lifts while the player's eyes are closed. A home Quest 3 has no weighted props and no force feedback, and its two controllers are identical in size and mass. Drawing the two controllers as a small and a big object would be a different experiment (seen size against felt size), not tested here.
- Replication: the paper finds the illusion in VR at about the real-world size; an earlier study it cites (Heineken & Schulte, 2007) found a large reduction in VR, so the VR size of the effect is not settled.
- Ethics: none special.
- Verdict: reject — the illusion needs real weighted props and an experimenter swapping them; a home Quest 3 cannot supply weight.
