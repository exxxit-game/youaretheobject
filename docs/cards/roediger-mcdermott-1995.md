## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: Exp. 1 is 6 lists × (about 18 s listening + 2.5 min recall) plus a 42-item recognition test — roughly 15–20 min; with fewer lists or shorter recall it fits 5–15 min. Exp. 2 (16 lists, 45 min) is too long.
- Task/tension: mild — a memory test the player wants to do well on; the tension comes from confidence ("I'm sure it was there").
- Within-person reveal: yes, strong — the player recalls or recognises "sleep" (or rates it "sure old"), and the game shows the list where it never appeared. The original procedure already ends with exactly this reveal.
- Works if the player expects tricks: risky — the paper's one participant who noticed the design had no false recall; players who know the DRM task (it is well known) may detect the theme word. Best placed early, before players learn to expect tricks.
- Space tier: seated (lists heard, recall written, recognition on a sheet).
- Quest 3 feasibility: good — audio playback, recognition with old/new and confidence buttons logged automatically; free recall needs voice input or a virtual keyboard (slower; recognition alone may be enough, Exp. 2 shows strong false recognition without recall). Word lists come from English association norms; other languages need their own lists, not translations.
- Replication: within the paper (Deese 1959 confirmed, Exp. 2 with 24 lists); later replications not checked here.
- Ethics: benign; the paper debriefs right after.
- Verdict: first-room candidate — seated, short, automatically measured, and one player sees their own false memory; risks are savvy players and the need for per-language word lists.
