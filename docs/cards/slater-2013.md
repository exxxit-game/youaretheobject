## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: about 6–7 min in VR — fits 5–15.
- Task/tension: high — a friendly chat turns into an aggressive confrontation in front of the player; the player may speak, step in, or do nothing.
- Within-person reveal: weak. The paper's finding is between groups (in-group vs out-group victim); one player sees one condition. The player's own counts (steps toward the pair, reaching out, utterances) can be shown, but there is no individual baseline to compare against, and the paper's per-cell means are only in a garbled table.
- Works if the player expects tricks: poor. In a psychology game, "will you step in?" is the obvious test, and stepping in costs nothing; demand pulls toward intervening. Some participants already discounted it ("Deep down I knew it was virtual reality").
- Space tier: large 3 × 3 m (Cave floor; participants walked toward and between the characters).
- Quest 3 feasibility: only for players with a 3 × 3 m boundary. Physical interventions (moving closer, reaching out) can be measured automatically from head and hand positions, and speaking from the microphone (voice activity, not content) — but the paper's counts were made by human coders with rules, so automatic counts are an approximation. Two harder problems: the opening conversation was driven by a hidden human operator choosing lines (at home it needs speech recognition or a scripted dialogue, a different interaction), and the in-group manipulation used a real, pre-screened team loyalty that a game would have to ask the player about.
- Replication: none in the paper; n = 38 in four cells of 9–10; physical effect P = 0.016, verbal not significant.
- Ethics: moderate — verbal abuse and a violent push; the paper warned participants beforehand and excluded under-18s. At home: content warning and age gate needed; the scene ends at the push, as in the paper.
- Verdict: interlude — a strong, short scene, but the paper's effect is between groups, depended on a live operator and real team loyalty, and is easy to game when the player expects a test.
