## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: four 5-min exposures plus questionnaires, roughly 25 min or more; one synchronous trial alone would be about 5–7 min.
- Task/tension: none — move freely for 5 min, then answer questions; the knife and table threats produced no clear reaction.
- Within-person reveal: weak — the main measure is the player's own rating; the only behavioural measure (Exp. 3) needs someone to lead the player backwards blind.
- Works if the player expects tricks: unknown; the effect was already weak (0.95 of 3) in naive students.
- Space tier: standing for Exp. 1–2 (moving arms and legs on the spot, the figure is virtual 2 m ahead); large, about 3.5 m long, for Exp. 3 (led back up to 3.5 m and walking back).
- Quest 3 feasibility: poor — the socks must follow the real feet (Kinect v2 here); Quest 3 tracks head and hands, and legs are only estimated, so the socks would not move with the player's feet. Exp. 3 needs a second person, and Object is single-player. A hands-only version would be a different experiment.
- Replication: within this paper only (male students from one university).
- Ethics: none special (the knife animation is mild).
- Verdict: reject — needs real foot tracking that Quest 3 lacks and a helper for the only behavioural test; the ownership effect itself was weak and measured by questionnaire.
