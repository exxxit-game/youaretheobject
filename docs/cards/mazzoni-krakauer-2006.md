## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: not stated; Taylor & Ivry (2011) describe their 320-trial strategy block as nearly four times Mazzoni and Krakauer's, so the original strategy phase was roughly 80 movements. With baseline and washout likely 5–8 min.
- Task/tension: good — the player is handed a cheat that works perfectly, then slowly fails; the player fights a growing miss they cannot explain.
- Within-person reveal: strong — replay the player's own aim lines: they believed they aimed at the neighbour, but the hand slid further each trial; then the first "aim straight" trial misses by about 20°.
- Works if the player expects tricks: yes — the trick is fully explained before the drift starts; the drift is in the motor system, not in belief.
- Space tier: seated (wrist movements at a desk; a VR version would use short arm reaches with the controller, as the arm-reaching replication did).
- VR or mixed reality: VR. The real hand must be hidden and shown only as a rotated cursor; mixed reality passthrough would show the true hand and controller.
- Quest 3 feasibility: good — invisible controller, 8 visible target circles (Taylor & Ivry found the drift depends on the visible neighbour targets, so they must stay on screen), cursor rotated 45° about the start; direction logged automatically. A headset study (Anglin et al. 2017) found visuomotor rotation adaptation works in HMD-VR but with more explicit aiming; whether the drift is as large in a headset is untested.
- Replication: Taylor & Ivry (2011, PLoS Comput Biol, read in full, 3 groups of 10, arm reaches on a tablet) reproduced the drift; drift was largest with always-visible aiming targets, and with 320 trials participants eventually corrected it. Voets et al. (2020, Experimental Results, "An implicit plan still overrides an explicit strategy…") report a further replication (abstract only read; full text behind a bot check).
- Ethics: mild frustration (the paper reports it); include a washout block so the aftereffect is gone before the next room.
- Verdict: room — short, seated, automatic and survives full disclosure, with an excellent "you got worse while following the right rule" reveal; small original groups (9 each) and an untested headset size keep it below first room, and it overlaps with morehead-2017 (build one of the two first).
