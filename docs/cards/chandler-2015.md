## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: 10–13 minute online surveys; not a scene.
- Task/tension: none. It is a method study of 12 short judgement tasks, not one experiment to stage.
- Within-person reveal: not applicable as a room. Its lesson is about how the game collects data.
- Works if the player expects tricks: this is the paper's topic. After one exposure the effects shrank by about a quarter. They shrank more when the person had seen the other condition (an effect-size drop of 0.23, against 0.14 for the same condition). Asking "have you done this before?" did not find the affected people. It tested only survey judgement tasks, not perception or body illusions, so it says nothing direct about those rooms.
- Space tier: seated (online survey).
- VR or mixed reality: neither; it is not a room.
- Quest 3 feasibility: not a room. As a rule for the game: record by account or device which experiments and which condition each player has seen. In between-groups rooms and interludes, give a player one condition only, ever. Report repeat players separately. Do not rely on the player's own answer about earlier play.
- Replication: one two-wave MTurk study (638 people, 12 effects) with open data; the drop was consistent in direction but significant for only 5 tasks one by one.
- Ethics: none for the game. The original did not debrief wave 1.
- Verdict: reject (as a room) — a method paper with no scene; but it sets a binding rule for every between-groups room: one condition per player, ever, with exposure tracked by account rather than asked.
