## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: the original is 40 or 60 trials of up to 60 s, so roughly 15–25 min of trials (estimate; total not stated). Too long. A 12–20-trial version would fit 5 min.
- Task/tension: an overt "find the change" search under time pressure. It is game-like but familiar.
- Within-person reveal: yes. Every player gets both conditions: "you found changes in front of you in 12 s, but changes that happened behind your back took 18 s". Removal vs colour vs size can be shown the same way.
- Works if the player expects tricks: yes. The player is told there is a change and must find it, so expecting tricks is the task. The surprise is only how slow they are.
- Space tier: standing.
- VR or mixed reality: both are original (between groups). On Quest 3 the same headset can run both a VR and an MR block (switching needs a button press), which removes the paper's field-of-view confound (Vive Pro vs HoloLens 2). Quest 3 MR is video passthrough, not the HoloLens's optical see-through, so the AR numbers may not transfer.
- Quest 3 feasibility: very good. The shelf, flicker, head-yaw trigger (no eye tracking needed), controller ray selection and timing are all automatic.
- Replication: flicker change blindness is a classic, well-replicated paradigm. This paper's VR-vs-AR comparison is one study of 2 × 22, between groups, on different headsets.
- Ethics: full-view grey flicker at 2 Hz. That is below the common 3-flashes-per-second limit, but the game should still warn about flashing. Otherwise none.
- Verdict: interlude: a feasible, fully automatic change-detection mini-task that works in VR and MR on the same headset. But it is an overt search task with a familiar result, and a short reveal rather than a hidden effect.
