## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: Study 1 is four 3-minute anagram blocks plus introductions, so about 15 min. Study 2 is about 2 min: the agent walks in and talks for 41 s, then the player sits down.
- Task/tension: Study 1 is anagrams while a virtual person sits and "watches". Study 2 has no tension at all: it is a hidden measure (which chair do you take, which way do you turn) under a cover story.
- Within-person reveal: Study 1: weak. The audience effect showed only between groups on the first block, and the within-person comparison was not significant, so one player's "watched vs alone" scores will often not show it. Study 2: personal ("you never sat where Chris sat, and you turned to face him"). But the paper has no no-agent control, and facing the agent was also facing the room centre, so the reveal must not claim more than "you treated it like a person, as everyone in the study did".
- Works if the player expects tricks: Study 2 doubtfully. A player who knows it is a psychology game will suspect the chairs. Also, sitting "into" a visible person is odd whatever one believes. Study 1 does not depend on naivety, but its effect is small and fades after the first block.
- Space tier: Study 1 seated (the participant sits; the agent and poster are at the far end of the 5.6 × 6.4 m room). Study 2 large 5.6 × 6.4 m. That is the room the paper gives; the exact walking path to the chairs is not stated.
- VR or mixed reality: mixed reality suits the original better. It was AR with real chairs and a real door, and the effect after removing the headset (the real chair keeps the agent's "trace") only exists in MR. The original was optical see-through. Quest 3 shows the room through cameras, which is a different kind of AR.
- Quest 3 feasibility: Study 2 is feasible only if the player has two similar real chairs about 1 m apart and marks them (chairs are not reliably detected; room mesh is unverified). Head height drop and position show which chair, and head yaw shows which way they turned, all automatically. Study 1 was scored by a human listening to spoken answers. Automatic scoring would need speech recognition or typed answers with a pointer, and that changes the task.
- Replication: Study 1 is itself a replication (Park & Catrambone) in AR, but only the between-groups analysis held. Study 2 is a single study without controls.
- Ethics: mild deception (cover story about the questionnaire), debrief required. The passthrough view of the player's room must not be recorded. Real chairs only; never ask a player to sit where no real chair is.
- Verdict: interlude: the chair-avoidance moment (about 2 min, measured by head tracking) can sit inside an MR room for players with two chairs. The anagram audience effect held only between groups on the first block, so it cannot be shown to one player.
