## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: the VR scene itself is about 90 s, plus a short control task — fits easily (an interlude of 2–3 min).
- Task/tension: very high — a 10-second decision to push a person to his death, with victims shouting.
- Within-person reveal: weak in the original form. The paper's gap (70% push in VR vs 10–20% on paper) is between two groups. A within-person version (text question first, then the VR scene, or the reverse) is a different design with order effects; the paper's text group showed no difference between "acceptable?" and "would you?".
- Works if the player expects tricks: poor to moderate. Everyone knows the trolley problem; pushing a virtual character costs nothing, and the at-home study (kissel-2023) recorded a player who "tested the VR world" rather than deciding morally. Gaming experience did not predict pushing in this paper.
- Space tier: seated (orientation-only head tracking on the DK1 and a joystick; posture not stated; nothing requires walking).
- VR or mixed reality: VR — a footbridge over railway tracks cannot be placed in the player's room; mixed reality would only add the player's walls.
- Quest 3 feasibility: easy to build; the push and its latency are logged automatically. Keep a button/thumbstick push to match the joystick (a physical hand push is the different "personal force" experiment of Francis 2017). Heart rate cannot be measured on Quest 3 and is dropped.
- Replication: Study 2 repeats Study 1 in a public sample. A 2025 narrative review (PMC12719411, read only as a summary) lists later footbridge studies finding the same action > judgment gap (Francis 2017, 2019; Terbeck 2021; Maćkiewicz 2023) and the at-home Quest study kissel-2023 (60% pushed). Known confounds: the voice frames pushing as saving, and the VR group sees the consequences.
- Ethics: high. Killing a virtual person and watching five deaths with screams. Needs a content warning and a clear opt-out before the scene, an exit available at all times, no gore (occlusion as in kissel-2023), adults only, and a debrief.
- Verdict: interlude — a strong, short, automatically measured moral-action scene, but the paper's effect is between groups, the instructions were one-sided, and a player expecting a test can push just to see what happens; only with strict warning and exit.
