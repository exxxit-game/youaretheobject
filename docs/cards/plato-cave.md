## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: not applicable to the text; a room built on it fits 5–15 min (a few minutes of shadow-prediction rounds, the turn, the return).
- Task/tension: strong and ready-made — the "honours" passage is already a game: watch shadows pass, predict which comes next, get ranked. The tension is whether to keep winning at shadows or turn round.
- Within-person reveal: yes — the player turns and sees the fire, the wall and the carriers; then, back at the wall, their prediction score can be shown against the score they had while "chained".
- Works if the player expects tricks: yes for the frame (everyone knows the cave, nothing is hidden); no for any hidden manipulation — the whole story is famous.
- Space tier: seated (the prisoners are chained and cannot move; the reveal is turning the head/body, which a swivel chair or standing in place allows). The ascent out of the cave is a separate scene and could use teleport.
- Quest 3 feasibility: easy — a wall, a fire, moving silhouettes, controller input for predictions; prediction accuracy, reaction times and the moment the player turns round are logged automatically.
- Replication: none — this is a thought experiment, not a study; there is no measured effect to reproduce.
- What it would take to call it science: turn the text's two claims into one testable question with a manipulation and a measured choice, e.g. "Do honours (a visible score/rank for predicting shadows) make players stay with the shadows longer before turning round?" — randomise honours vs. no honours between players, measure time to turn and whether they return to the contest, pre-register the hypothesis, the exclusions and the sample size, and pilot in the headset first. Without that it is staging, not data.
- Ethics: none special (no deception needed; avoid a real "glare" — no bright flashes, given photosensitivity).
- Verdict: interlude — an excellent frame with a built-in measurable choice, but no tested effect behind it; it becomes a room only as a new pre-registered experiment (honours vs. no honours → who turns round, and when).
