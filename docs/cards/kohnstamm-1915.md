# kohnstamm-1915 — The arm that floats up by itself (Kohnstamm phenomenon)

- paper: kohnstamm-1915.txt
- citation: Kohnstamm, O. (1915). Demonstration einer katatonieartigen Erscheinung beim Gesunden (Katatonusversuch). Neurologisches Centralblatt, 34, 290–291. ## Facts
| What | Value | Quote (verbatim from the .txt) | Page |
|---|---|---|---|
| Participants (discovery) | Not stated as a number: one patient, an aviator, noticed it on himself; Kohnstamm then tried it on others | "Ein Patient meines Sanatoriums, der als Flieger und als scharfer Beobachter ein gutes Auge für die Vorgänge an seinem Körper hat" | 290 |
| Participants (Rothmann) | "A series of persons" in whom the effect was strong; no number given | "habe ich nun bei einer Reihe von Personen, bei denen das KoHNSTAMM'sche Phänomen sehr ausgeprägt hervortrat" | 424 |
| Procedure: the push | Stand next to a wall, press the back of the hand of the straight, lowered arm against it hard for 5–60 s | "den Handrücken bei gesenktem gestrecktem Arm gegen die Wand unter starker Spannung andrückt, 5 bis 60 Sekunden lang" | 290 |
| Procedure: the release | Turn the body away so the arm is free to move sideways | "gibt dann durch eine Wendung des Körpers dem Arm freien Spielraum, sich seitlich zu bewegen" | 290 |
| Procedure: best direction (Rothmann) | Strongest when the hand pushes up and outwards; almost absent when pushing up and inwards | "Am stärksten tritt es bei Andrücken der Hand in der Richtung nach oben und außen in die Erscheinung" | 422 |
| Measure (Csiky) | Arm angle read off a scale drawn on the wall, seconds counted with a metronome | "daß die Versuchsperson neben einer Wand stand, an der der Erhebungswinkel der Armbewegung abgelesen werden konnte" | 776 |
| Duration | Per trial: 5–60 s push, then rise and fall within roughly 15 s; whole session not stated | "dann bei der 12. bis 15. Sekunde beginnt ein ziemlich steiler Abfall" | 776 |
| Time course (Csiky) | Latency of 2–3 s, then an accelerating rise that slows at 8–10 s | "die zuerst eine Latenz von 2 bis 3 Sekunden aufweist, dann kommt eine ziemlich rasch sich beschleunigende Erhebung" | 776 |
| Main result | The arm rises on its own, 2° to 120° depending on the person and the length of the push | "so bemerkt man, daß der Arm sich automatisch erhebt: 2, 45, 90, 120° hoch, verschieden nach der Versuchsperson und der Dauer der Anspannung" | 290 |
| Main result: the feeling | It feels as if a mysterious force lifts the arm | "Man hat das Gefühl, als ob der Arm von einer geheimnisvollen Macht gehoben würde." | 290 |
| Replication (Rothmann, Berlin) | Easy to produce; works strikingly in naive people | "bei normalen Menschen, die nicht wissen, worauf es ankommt, oft in verblüffend ausgeprägter Form" | 422 |
| Replication (Csiky, Budapest): naive subjects | Most objective when the person is not told what will happen | "wenn man die Versuchsperson von der kommenden Nachbewegung nicht unterrichtet" | 775 |
| Variability (Oppenheim, discussion) | Repeated on several people: very different between individuals | "an verschiedenen Versuchspersonen wiederholt und dabei gefunden, daß es doch individuell sehr verschieden ausfällt" | 445 |
| Failure on repetition (Schuster, discussion) | Worked on himself only the first time | "Er gelang mir nur zum erstenmal." | 445 |

Note: the 1915 reports give no participant counts and no statistics; they are demonstrations and self-observations by four physicians.

## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: 2–5 minutes for three or four pushes of up to 60 s with rests; well inside 5–15 min.
- Task/tension: a simple bodily instruction ("push the wall as hard as you can for 30 s, then step away and let the arm hang"); the tension is the wait after the release.
- Within-person reveal: strong. Hand tracking (or the controller) records the arm angle every frame, so the game can replay the player's own rising arm with the latency, peak angle and the moment it fell, and compare it with a voluntary lift of the same arm.
- Works if the player expects tricks: partly. Rothmann and Csiky stress naive subjects, and Schuster says it worked for him only the first time, so a player who knows the trick may suppress it; a first-trial-only measure is the honest one.
- Space tier: standing (in place next to a real wall or door frame; no walking).
- VR or mixed reality: mixed reality suits the original better: the procedure needs a real wall to push against, and passthrough lets the player see and use their own wall or door frame while the game draws the angle scale on it (Csiky's wall scale). In VR the player cannot see the wall they push.
- Quest 3 feasibility: good. Arm elevation from the wrist or controller position relative to the shoulder (estimated from the head) is enough for a 2°–120° movement; plane detection or a touch on the wall can place the scale. Pushing with a controller in the hand is possible but changes the grip; hand tracking is closer to the original (back of the bare hand on the wall). The game must stop the player from walking into the wall while blind in VR, another reason for passthrough.
- Replication: replicated within months by Rothmann (Berlin) and Csiky (Budapest) and discussed by Oppenheim, Schuster and Liepmann; large individual differences and possible failure in practised observers are reported in the same volume. Modern laboratory work on the phenomenon exists (reviewer's knowledge, not in this text).
- Ethics: harmless; a strong isometric push for up to 60 s should be optional for players with shoulder problems, and the push length can stay at 30 s.
- Verdict: first-room candidate — short, needs only the player's own body and wall, the player sees the effect in themselves, and mixed reality matches the original setting exactly.
