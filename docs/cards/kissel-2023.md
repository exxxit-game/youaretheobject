## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: not stated; training + control scene + one dilemma + 1-min spoken reason is probably 10–15 min — fits.
- Task/tension: high — a push or switch decision with five lives at stake, after a training that makes both systems familiar.
- Within-person reveal: weak. Switch vs push is between groups; each player meets one dilemma. The player's own spoken reason can be played back, but there is no individual baseline.
- Works if the player expects tricks: poor — the paper itself reports players testing the VR world, accidental pushes (5 of 19 who explained), and training that suggests the "expected" action. A psychology-game player will expect exactly this test.
- Space tier: seated (two-thirds played seated; teleport movement; designed for home space).
- VR or mixed reality: VR — a control tower over railway tracks; mixed reality adds nothing.
- Quest 3 feasibility: proven — this very design ran on Quest 2 at home without a researcher. The choice is logged automatically; the spoken reason needs the microphone (unverified in our browser) and was coded by hand in the paper.
- Replication: it is itself a small at-home repeat of lab VR studies (Navarrete 2012; Patil 2014; francis-2016): 100% switch and 60% push match the lab pattern, but n = 30, unequal groups, no statistical test of the difference, power 0.22–0.27.
- Ethics: high — killing virtual workers. The paper used occlusion instead of gore, a sickness screen, consent and debrief inside the headset, and mental-health resources at the end; Object would need the same plus a warning and an always-available exit.
- Verdict: interlude — the strongest evidence that a trolley room works on a home Quest, but the result is a between-groups percentage from 30 people, distorted by accidents and players testing the game; best used as design guidance for francis-2016, not as a separate room.
