## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: a practice walk, 30 s of maths and an evacuation of well under a minute: about 3 minutes. Interlude length.
- Task/tension: low. A top-down "steer your dot out of the room" task with an emergency message but no threat, smoke or sound.
- Within-person reveal: weak. One treatment per person; the reveal can only set the player's exit next to the paper's shares (the sign followed by over 80%, the crowd near chance). A second round is contaminated by the first.
- Works if the player expects tricks: partly. The choice is low-stakes, and a suspicious player may defy the cue on purpose; a sign reads as an instruction, so its pull probably survives better than the crowd's.
- Space tier: seated.
- VR or mixed reality: the original is a flat top-down screen. Mixed reality keeps it closest: the floor plan as a tabletop model on the player's real table, steered with the controller ray as the mouse. A first-person VR walk through the room would be a different experiment. It can also run unchanged for desktop players.
- Quest 3 feasibility: easy in principle: a crowd of 80 agents with a social-force walking model, pointer steering, automatic logging. The simulation details are in the paper's supplementary material, which we do not have.
- Replication: one large study (541). Not a direct replication of anything; consistent with `bode-2018`'s weak crowd effect and with the same group's queue study (Bode et al. 2015, library item 17). Many comparisons, uncorrected.
- Ethics: harmless; no threat, no deception beyond the unannounced emergency message.
- Verdict: interlude — large sample, automatic measures, cheap and harmless, and a clear "the sign beats the crowd" result; but low tension, a top-down abstraction and one trial per player make it a short table-top interlude, not a room.
