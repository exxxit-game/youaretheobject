## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: about 8–10 min (three practice rides, five rides, short questions).
- Task/tension: low — ride and estimate; crowding itself adds discomfort, which is the mechanism.
- Within-person reveal: weak — the average difference between the emptiest and fullest ride is under 10 s against person-to-person SDs of 11–21 s, so one player's five estimates will often not show it; it works better as "you vs all players".
- Works if the player expects tricks: probably not; a player who suspects a time trick can count seconds.
- Space tier: standing (standing point in the car; no walking needed for the task).
- VR or mixed reality: VR; the subway car must replace the room. Mixed reality crowding (avatars in the player's own room) would be a new, untested condition.
- Quest 3 feasibility: feasible but heavy — up to 175 animated avatars must run at frame rate in the Quest browser; estimates can be typed on a virtual keypad without taking the headset off (a change from the paper).
- Replication: none found for the VR crowding-to-time effect; it fits a broader literature that negative emotion lengthens perceived duration (cited in the paper). Single study, 41 people.
- Ethics: mild discomfort and possible nausea (1 of 42 stopped).
- Verdict: interlude — a short standing scene with an automatic time-estimate measure, but a small, unreplicated, mostly group-level effect and a heavy avatar load; better as a "you vs other players" statistic than as a personal reveal.
