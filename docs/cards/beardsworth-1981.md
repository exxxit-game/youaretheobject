## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: far over 15 min — 11 recorded walks, then two viewing sessions (each a 30-trial tape shown twice, about 30 min each by the paper's trial times) a week later.
- Task/tension: clear and personal — "which walker is you?".
- Within-person reveal: yes — "that one was you" is an instant personal reveal.
- Works if the player expects tricks: not relevant; it is a recognition task, not a deception.
- Space tier: large — a straight walk of 5–6 paces (about 4 m; the paper gives paces, not metres). The 8 m camera distance does not matter in VR, where the "camera" is virtual.
- Quest 3 feasibility: a faithful copy is not possible. Quest 3 measures the head and two hands; legs are estimated, not tracked, while the paper's display depends on ankle, knee, hip and shoulder lights. The comparison walkers were the viewer's own friends, which a one-player game does not have, and viewing was a week after recording. A head-and-hands version with strangers as foils would be a different experiment.
- Replication: mixed and small — Wolff (1932) strong, Cutting & Kozlowski (1977) found no self advantage, this paper rests on 6 people with large individual differences.
- Ethics: recording a player's movement is biometric data; keep it on the device.
- Verdict: reject — needs a group of friends, full-body joint points, a week's delay and over 15 minutes; a home Quest 3 player alone cannot reproduce it, and the effect rests on 6 people.
