# aubert-1861 — The line that tilts when you tilt your head in the dark (Aubert phenomenon)

- paper: aubert-1861.txt
- citation: Aubert, H. (1861). Eine scheinbare bedeutende Drehung von Objecten bei Neigung des Kopfes nach rechts oder links. Archiv für pathologische Anatomie und Physiologie und für klinische Medicin (Virchows Archiv), 20, 381–393. | Main result: only in the dark | The effect needs a room so dark that nothing to orient by is visible | "Die Erscheinung findet nur statt, wenn das Zimmer so stark verdunkelt ist, dass keine Gegenstände sichtbar sind, nach denen man sich orientiren kann." | 383 |
| Main result: the door test | Opening the door (furniture visible) puts the line back at once; closing it, the line turns again within 1–2 s | "so wird die heile Linie in 1 — 2 Sekunden wieder vertikal" | 383 |
| Within-session replication | Repeated many times with constant result and confirmed by other people | "Ieh habe das sehr oft hintereinander mit constantem Erfolge wiederholt und aueh veo andern Personen bestätigen lassen" | 383 |

## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: 5–10 minutes (a handful of line settings at two or three head tilts, plus the "door" trials).
- Task/tension: an adjustment task: "turn the glowing line until it is exactly vertical" (or "is it tilted left or right?"), done with the head held tilted.
- Within-person reveal: very strong. The headset knows the exact head roll and the exact line angle the player chose, so the game can show "you set this line 20° off vertical while your head was tilted 40°", then reveal the same line in the lit room.
- Works if the player expects tricks: probably yes; Aubert and his colleagues knew the line was vertical and still saw it tilt. The effect is perceptual, not a guess.
- Space tier: standing (in place; Aubert stood next to the aperture and in one condition lay on the floor; no walking).
- VR or mixed reality: both, and the switch is the experiment. The dark-room condition is a VR (or fully occluded) view with only the line visible; Aubert's "open the door" condition, where furniture becomes visible and the effect vanishes, maps onto showing the player's real room in passthrough. Inside one mixed-reality session the game can draw a black occluder (dark room) and remove it (door opens) without restarting the session.
- Quest 3 feasibility: good for small and medium tilts. Head roll is tracked exactly; a head-locked or world-locked line in an otherwise black scene is trivial. The headset's own glow and lens edges are visible in the dark, which may give a weak frame cue that the 1860 room did not have (the effect may be smaller). Large tilts near 90° need the player to lie on their side, which is awkward with a headset; neck tilt alone reaches roughly 30–45°.
- Replication: inside the paper only self-repetition and four colleagues. The effect carries Aubert's name in later work (a whole paper on it: Nagel 1898, Z. Psychol. Physiol. Sinnesorg. 16, 373–398, open in the Virtual Laboratory, not read for this card) and in modern subjective-visual-vertical research (reviewer's knowledge); nothing from those is quoted here.
- Ethics: harmless; seated or lying versions avoid any fall risk from tilting in the dark.
- Verdict: room — a clean, automatic, self-revealing measure, and mixed reality reproduces Aubert's own door test; not a first room only because the strongest effect needs large tilts.
