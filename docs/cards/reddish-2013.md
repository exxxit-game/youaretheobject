## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: 6 minutes of joint activity, one game decision and a short questionnaire: about 10–12 minutes. Fits.
- Task/tension: moving or chanting together is pleasant; the tension is the single risky choice afterwards (trust the group for $10 or take the sure $7).
- Within-person reveal: weak. Each group is in one condition, so the player cannot compare themselves across conditions. The reveal can show the group's measured synchrony (from tracked hands or voice onsets) and the player's own choice next to the paper's 93% vs 43–62%.
- Works if the player expects tricks: mostly. There is no deception and the others are real. A player who guesses the hypothesis may cooperate to please; the paper cannot say how much.
- Live players needed: 3 at the same time (4 for Exp. 1), all for the activity and the decision.
- Space tier: standing (Exp. 1 and 3: arm, sway and stepping in place); Exp. 2 (chanting) is seated.
- VR or mixed reality: mixed reality suits the original better when the group is in one room, because they see each other's real bodies. For remote players, VR with avatars; whether moving with avatars works like moving with people is not tested here.
- Quest 3 feasibility: tracked hands or controllers, plus the head for stepping, give movement timing automatically (the paper needed foot-pedals for this). Exp. 2's chanting could be timed from the microphone, measuring only when each word starts; no audio needs to leave the headset. The catch is network delay: the mechanism is perceived synchrony, and between homes each player sees and hears the others late (tens to hundreds of milliseconds). Unison chanting over the internet sounds out of sync. Delay must be measured and kept low, or the group must be in one place.
- Replication: three experiments in one paper with different tasks (movement, chanting, stepping) point the same way, but samples are small (Exp. 2: 27 people) and in Exp. 1 the shared-goal vs synchrony difference was not significant. Independent replications are not checked here.
- Ethics: no deception. The original paid real money; a game would use points. Voice: if chanting is used, only word-onset times should be computed on the headset; no recording leaves it.
- Verdict: room (3 live players) — short, deception-free and largely automatic, with a voice variant; small samples and network delay that may destroy the felt synchrony make it a pilot-first room.
