## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: not stated. Finding the span (~2 minutes) plus 24 sequences with 15-second gaps — about 8–10 minutes.
- Task/tension: a memory game of ever-longer tapping patterns, played just beyond your limit (controls got only about a quarter of new sequences right) — steady, mild pressure.
- Within-person reveal: yes, two numbers: your block span (controls about 5) and the hidden repeat — "every third pattern was the same; you went from missing it to getting it" (controls 80% vs. 26%). The "without noticing" part depends on the player not spotting the repeat; how many controls noticed is not reported.
- Works if the player expects tricks: partly. A suspicious player may spot the repeat — learning then still shows, but the surprise is gone. The span is unaffected.
- Space tier: seated (a 9 × 11 inch board on a table).
- VR or mixed reality: mixed reality suits the original better. With the board on the player's own real table, each tap stops on a real surface, as on the wooden board; in a fully virtual booth the controller or hand passes through a virtual board, which changes the tapping movement and its timing. The memory part is the same in both. Hand tracking (unverified on our headset) would allow finger taps like the original; tapping with the controller tip also works.
- Quest 3 feasibility: automatic — the game is the examiner (an animated stick) and records the order of taps. The board has to be put on the real table (plane detection or depth hit test: Meta/press status, unverified on our headset) and its height checked by one touch of the table.
- Replication: the thesis's healthy sample is 16. The block span later became a standard test and the hidden-repeat learning has been studied since, but only this thesis was read; no replication is checked here.
- Ethics: none; keep the brain-surgery comparison out of the game.
- Verdict: room — seated, about 10 minutes, automatic, with two personal numbers and a "you learned it without noticing" reveal, and MR's real table is a genuine improvement over a virtual booth; but the healthy sample was 16 and suspicious players may spot the repeat.
