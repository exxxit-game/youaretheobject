## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: not stated. 40 rounds of one choice each, with all 14 players waiting for each other, could take about 10–20 minutes depending on the round timer.
- Task/tension: simple and social: whom do you give to, with everyone's balances and gifts visible after each round. The tension grows as reciprocity and group lines form.
- Within-person reveal: good. The player's own 40 gifts can be split into ingroup, outgroup and self, and shown over time next to the paper's curves, including whether the player kept giving across the colour line.
- Works if the player expects tricks: mostly. The group label is meaningless, and players may know that, yet the paper's effect is about what happens when they interact anyway. The cover story (painting taste) is a mild deception; a player who suspects random groups may care less about the colour.
- Live players needed: 14 at the same time for the whole game. That is the hardest requirement among these cards; it suits a scheduled event rather than drop-in play. Smaller groups would be a different design.
- Space tier: seated (a screen-based game; the room is not described).
- VR or mixed reality: VR suits it: the original is already a "virtual arena" of avatars, and giving a token by pointing at an avatar is natural. Mixed reality adds nothing.
- Quest 3 feasibility: easy to build (one pointing choice per round, synced by round over realtime). Every gift is logged automatically. Risks: 14 players online together, dropouts in a 40-round game, and avatars that leak identity (voice, names) must be off.
- Replication: Study 2 repeated Study 1 with money and a different group induction, with the same pattern, but both are from the same university and population. Independent replications are not checked here.
- Ethics: mild deception (groups said to be based on taste are random); a debrief is needed. No real money in a game version.
- Verdict: room (event, 14 live players) — the cleanest "minimal groups with real players" design, fully automatic and with a good reveal, but 14 simultaneous players makes it an occasional scheduled session, not a drop-in room.
