## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: not stated; one condition is 219 pointing trials, likely 10–15 min. Fits only if one VR condition is run (not all three).
- Task/tension: weak — repetitive pointing at a line on a wall; the interest comes only at the end.
- Within-person reveal: yes — after the shift is removed, the player's pointing lands to the left without seeing the hand; the game can draw each miss and then the reset trials.
- Works if the player expects tricks: probably yes; the paper cites (does not test) that knowing about the shift does not stop the after-effect.
- Space tier: seated (pointing at arm's length at a wall/screen in front; posture not stated in the paper, table-top setup implied).
- Quest 3 feasibility: good — rotating the rendered scene 10° about the head and hiding the arm in a virtual box needs no extra hardware; controller tip (as in the paper) or hand tracking for the fingertip; error measured automatically in degrees. Arm fatigue over 219 trials is a real risk.
- Replication: the VR-larger-than-goggles result is one sample of 20 in one lab, and the goggle comparison relies on rescaling 8.7° to 10°. The basic after-effect itself is classic (paper cites many sources).
- Ethics: temporary pointing bias and possible dizziness; the reset step must be kept and the player told the effect is temporary.
- Verdict: room — reliable, automatically measured after-effect with a clear personal reveal, but long and repetitive pointing; the "VR beats goggles" claim is too weak to show the player as a finding.
