## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: under a minute for the choice (plus a short filler task); it can sit inside any room as one moment.
- Task/tension: none for the player — a casual gift; the choice itself is the hidden measure.
- Within-person reveal: no. One binary choice says nothing about the person; the effect exists only as a difference between two groups (74% vs 24%). The game can only compare the player's pick with group percentages.
- Works if the player expects tricks: badly. A player in a psychology game will suspect a "pick one of five" offer. Also, the 2022 replication cites work (Yamagishi et al., 2008) in which Japanese participants chose the unique pen as often as Americans when nobody would choose after them — in a solo VR room there is no "next person", which may remove the very reason for the original difference.
- Space tier: seated (an object offered by hand; no walking).
- Quest 3 feasibility: trivial — a character offers five pens, the player grabs one, the colour and split are logged automatically.
- Replication: Otterbring et al. 2022 (card kim-markus-replication-2022, N = 729, text version online) found the same direction but a much smaller effect (r = .12 vs .52), and Chinese participants now chose the uncommon colour half the time.
- Ethics: none special; do not label the player's culture or personality from one choice.
- Verdict: interlude — a 30-second, automatically logged choice that can be hidden in any room, but it is only a group difference (a single player cannot see it in themselves) and its size has shrunk in the large replication.
