## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: not stated; from the trial timeline (≈3–3.5 s per trial) one condition of 256 trials is roughly 13–15 min, so all four conditions near an hour.
- Task/tension: press when you hear "your" tone; a partner who answers the other tone makes your answers slower when the tone comes from their side.
- Within-person reveal: weak — the effect is 10–15 ms and needs hundreds of trials; better shown as "players with a partner vs alone" across players.
- Works if the player expects tricks: yes; the effect is automatic, and in this paper it even survived an invisible partner.
- Space tier: seated.
- VR or mixed reality: the classic version (two people at one table) is closest in mixed reality with a real person beside you; this paper used VR with a recorded partner. The game can make the partner a real live player, removing the deception.
- Quest 3 feasibility: the paper ran on a Quest 2, but answers went through a physical keypad sampled at 600–700 Hz. In the Quest Browser the button timing is tied to the frame rate (unverified); an effect of ~14 ms is close to that resolution.
- Replication: the joint Simon effect is a standard lab result (the paper's review); here it appeared with full-body and invisible partners, only as a trend with hands-only, and the avatar differences were not significant.
- Ethics: the original deceived participants about a live partner; with real networked players no deception is needed.
- Verdict: interlude — a two-player reaction-time moment; too small and too timing-sensitive for a personal reveal, but a natural use of live multiplayer.
