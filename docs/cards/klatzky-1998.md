## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: not stated; five short trials (a few seconds of travel each) — 3–5 minutes per condition, under 10 minutes for both.
- Task/tension: simple and quick ("turn to face where you started"), confidence is high, which makes the error striking.
- Within-person reveal: strong if both conditions are run on one player (the paper ran them between groups): with a physical turn the player faces home; with a visual-only turn they face off by about the turn angle. The game can draw the true home arrow against the player's arrow.
- Works if the player expects tricks: partly. The paper used few trials and rapid responses to stop people from calculating; Gramann et al. (2005, JEP:HPP, read in full in scratch) show "nonturners" can switch frames when told, so a player who knows the rule may compute the answer.
- Space tier: seated (rotating stool; the real-turn arm needs a swivel chair, or standing and turning in place).
- VR or mixed reality: VR. The illusion of travel needs a full virtual scene; passthrough would show the real room standing still. The paper's own footnote reports that removing room memory (disorientation) changed both conditions, so the real room must be out of sight.
- Quest 3 feasibility: easy — a post field, scripted optic flow for the legs, and the player's head yaw logged at the end as the answer. The visual-turn condition is smooth artificial rotation, a known motion-sickness trigger; keep legs short (3 m and 2 m at walking speed) and offer the real-turn arm first.
- Replication: the failure to update heading from visually simulated turns recurs in later virtual path-integration work: Gramann et al. (2005) "nonturners" (7 of 12 in Exp. 1) and Riecke's consistent left–right reversals (Riecke 2008, Presence; abstract only). But not everyone fails: later studies find a turner/nonturner split, and Riecke et al. (2002) report visual homing is possible in richer scenes. Klatzky's own footnote shows the effect shrank after disorientation (slope 0.39 vs 1.05).
- Ethics: harmless; short artificial rotation can cause mild sickness.
- Second reviewer (card written twice in parallel; notes merged): the imagined (described) path needs only standing; the walked path needs about 5 × 2 m of real walking; for the walked half, real walking matters more than passthrough; guiding a blind walk without a helper is unsolved.
- Verdict: room — seated, a few minutes, automatic heading measure and a vivid "I can't believe I did that" reveal; replication shows an individual split (turner or nonturner) rather than everyone failing, which suits a personal "which navigator are you" result, but trick-aware players may calculate.
