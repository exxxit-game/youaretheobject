## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: about 1 minute in the tunnel plus a short training maze, so 3–5 minutes in the game. Short.
- Task/tension: on foot in a smoke-filled tunnel with a burning truck, decide where to go. The smoke moving towards the player gives some pressure. The other person changed HOW people went (the route), not WHETHER or WHEN they left.
- Within-person reveal: moderate. A replay from above can lay the player's own path over the runner's path and over the paper's two patterns (alone: along the wall; with the runner: down the middle). It is still one trial in one condition; a second run is not valid once the player has seen the runner.
- Works if the player expects tricks: better than the bystander rooms, because the measure is the shape of the path, which players rarely watch. But a game player may read the runner as "the game showing the way" and follow him for that reason, which is not the social effect the paper measured.
- Space tier: standing. Travel was by gamepad inside a projection room; nobody walked the tunnel. Thumbstick travel inside Meta's stationary 1 × 1 m boundary keeps that.
- VR or mixed reality: VR. A road tunnel with a burning truck cannot be placed in the player's room; the original was a fully virtual scene.
- Quest 3 feasibility: easy: tunnel model, particle smoke, one animated runner, thumbstick travel, head position logged for the path and timing measures. Smoke plus thumbstick travel raises the risk of motion sickness (the paper measured sickness but reports no group difference).
- Replication: one study, 21 per group. The same lab's follow-up with standing, passive bystanders (`kinateder-2016`) also found path changes but no change in destination or timing. Kinateder & Warren (2016) found that an active bystander increased leaving. No direct replication.
- Ethics: a fire and smoke in a tunnel; the paper measured tunnel anxiety beforehand. Needs a warning for fire and tunnel fear and a visible way out at any time; a warning gives away the emergency, which the original kept secret. The reveal serves as the debrief.
- Verdict: interlude — a one-minute scene with an automatic path measure and a clear replay, but the effect is modest (d = 0.64, 21 per group), touches only the route and not the decision to leave, and has not been replicated.
