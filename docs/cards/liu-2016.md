## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: not stated; learning plus 48 trials is probably 15–20 min.
- Task/tension: a memory-and-imagination pointing test under time pressure; the surprise is that the body's real turn makes one imagined direction easy.
- Within-person reveal: good — the player's own reaction times for "the way my body faces" vs. the other imagined headings, though the effect was in time, not in accuracy (Exp. 1).
- Works if the player expects tricks: yes; nothing is hidden, and the effect is in automatic reaction time.
- Space tier: standing (Exp. 1: learn, then turn 90° in place; objects 1 m around). Exp. 2 needs walking from one half of the 6.50 × 4.36 m lab to the other: large 6.5 × 4.4 m.
- VR or mixed reality: mixed reality suits the original better — it is a real room, and the paper itself blames the VR scene for Kelly's failure in a new room. Virtual objects placed in the player's own room, with a black screen as the blindfold.
- Quest 3 feasibility: good. Pointing with the controller ray replaces the joystick (time and angular error recorded automatically); spoken instructions through the headset; the 90° turn checked by head yaw. Standing blindfolded needs the guardian on.
- Replication: Exp. 1 reproduces Kelly et al. (2007) and Xiao & Liu (2014) in the original room; the new-room results differ between those studies, which is why this paper exists.
- Ethics: none special.
- Verdict: room (standing) — a clean body-in-space effect with automatic measures and a personal reaction-time reveal; the mixed-reality version is closest to the original.
