## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: 18 trials of about 30 s, so about 9 minutes, plus instructions. Fits 5–15 min.
- Task/tension: which line matches? Answers are said aloud in turn. The tension comes when the three others, who are honest, name a different line.
- Within-person reveal: good. The player's errors on the 12 critical trials can be set against their 6 neutral trials. The reveal can show the two pictures side by side ("they really saw a different line").
- Works if the player expects tricks: the paper's whole point is that there are no confederates. The majority were real, naive people who honestly saw something else, so suspicion had nothing to catch: nobody detected the trick. A one-player Object version must voice the majority with NPCs or recordings, which brings back confederates, exactly what this paper removed. Only real other players would keep its point. That means live multiplayer (listed as possible in `docs/headset-capabilities.md`), or recorded answers of earlier real players who were shown the other line. The second option is my extrapolation, not tested in the paper. The original also needed naive participants who knew each other.
- Space tier: seated (four chairs in two rows facing a screen; no walking).
- VR or mixed reality: either. The original is a seated room with a screen, so mixed reality can put the screen in the player's own room. VR makes the co-viewers easier to stage. In a headset, the "different line" needs no glasses: each player is simply shown a different image.
- Quest 3 feasibility: easy. Line stimuli on a virtual screen, the answer by pointing at 1/2/3, all logged automatically. No eye tracking is needed. It can also run on desktop.
- Replication: the paper is itself a replication of Asch (1956). It replicated for women (mean 3.44 vs Asch's 4.41 errors), not for men (0.6). Only 26 minority viewers, one lab.
- Ethics: mild deception (different images without telling anyone). The original debriefed. With real co-players, the minority player's public "wrong" answers may embarrass them, so the debrief must cover all players.
- Verdict: room — seated, about 9 minutes, measured automatically, with a clear reveal; but with one player and NPC voices it reverts to the confederate Asch design this paper replaced, and the effect appeared only in women in a small sample.
