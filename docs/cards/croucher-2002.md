## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: one walk and one stop, about 1–2 min with instructions (not stated in the paper).
- Task/tension: clear and bodily — "stop the moment you could first see your eyes in this covered mirror".
- Within-person reveal: strong — uncover the mirror and let the player walk the same line: the reflection appears only at the mirror edge, and the game marks where they stopped against the true point.
- Works if the player expects tricks: likely — the error is the player's own belief, not a hidden manipulation; physics students and ray-drawers were no better. Not tested in VR.
- Space tier: large about 3.1 × 1.5 m (Exp. 4: 120 cm mirror plus 186 cm start offset, walking line 120 cm from the mirror; tested in a 4.4 × 5.74 m room, where 2 people still ran out of room). Exp. 5's 25 cm mirror with the same offset still needs about 2.1 m of width. The drawing experiments (1A, 2, 3) are seated.
- Quest 3 feasibility: for players with the space, easy — a covered virtual mirror; the stop point comes from head tracking and the true point from exact geometry; the reveal needs a rendered mirror and an avatar head and hands (Quest 3 cannot show the player's real eyes). For everyone else, the seated top-view drawing task of Exp. 1A/3 is a faithful copy of those experiments (not a shrunk walk), but its error was smaller.
- Replication: within the paper, overestimation appears in Exps. 1A, 2, 3, 4 and 5 (drawing and real room, psychology and physics students); independent replications not checked in this card. Only one naive trial per player: after the reveal they know.
- Ethics: none special; sideways walking in VR needs the boundary visible.
- Verdict: room (large space only) — clear bodily task, automatic measurement from head position and a strong self-reveal; offered to players with about 3.1 × 1.5 m, with the seated drawing version (Exp. 1A) for the rest.
