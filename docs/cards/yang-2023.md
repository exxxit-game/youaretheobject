## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: probably 15–20 minutes (5 min of pictures, 4 min filler, 40 rated trials); not stated.
- Task/tension: mild: a memory test with an "assistant" whose advice you can take or ignore, and a bonus for being right.
- Within-person reveal: good. Every player produces their own trust curve; the game can show "each time the assistant was wrong your trust fell further than it rose when it was right" (the paper reports the direction, not a ratio), and that its errors hurt more when they cost you.
- Works if the player expects tricks: yes; the aid is openly an aid, and the asymmetry is a judgment pattern, not a hidden trick.
- Space tier: seated.
- VR or mixed reality: neither is needed (the original is a desktop task); in VR the aid could be an embodied assistant, which would be a change and could raise or lower trust.
- Quest 3 feasibility: easy and automatic (pictures, two-choice answers, a 0–100 slider after each trial); 150 pictures and the slider per trial are tiring in a headset, which favours the desktop version of the game.
- Replication: the success-up/failure-down pattern and the larger effect of failures agree with older studies cited in the paper; the outcome-bias and contrast effects come from this one study.
- Ethics: none special (a bonus in points instead of money changes the stakes).
- Verdict: interlude (desktop rather than headset): a clean within-person picture of trust in an automated helper; it does not use the body or space.
