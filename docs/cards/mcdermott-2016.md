## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: not stated; the chord, tone and voice blocks (40 + 40 + 44 + 15 short sounds) would take roughly 10–15 min.
- Task/tension: none — "do you like this sound?"; no trick, no surprise.
- Within-person reveal: no. The finding is purely a difference between cultures (and between musicians and non-musicians). A typical player of a Western-music culture will simply show the ordinary preference for consonance; the game could only say "the Tsimane' did not", which is a fact about a group, not something the player discovers in themselves.
- Works if the player expects tricks: yes, nothing is hidden — but there is also nothing to reveal.
- Space tier: seated (headphones and verbal ratings; no movement).
- Quest 3 feasibility: easy to build and log automatically (buttons for the 4-point scale). The roughness stimuli rely on sending different tones to each ear; the Quest's open near-ear speakers leak between ears, so faithful sound needs headphones.
- Replication: Study 2 in the same paper (other villages, US musicians) found the same; no independent replication in this paper.
- Ethics: none special; present the Tsimane' respectfully.
- Verdict: reject — a culture-level difference that a single player cannot see in themselves; for most players it would just confirm their expected taste.
