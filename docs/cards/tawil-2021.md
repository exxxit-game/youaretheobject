## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: one room (3 min exploring + ratings) is about 5 min; the four rooms took about 60 min in the paper — far too long. A two-room version (one angular, one curved, same style) would be about 10 min but is a reduced design.
- Task/tension: low — look around a living room and rate it. The interest is the prediction ("most people say curves are nicer") against the player's own answer.
- Within-person reveal: yes — within subject in the paper; the player's own ratings (and, new, time spent and distance walked in each room) can be shown side by side. Honest framing: the published "curves win" story did not hold here.
- Works if the player expects tricks: moderately — the player may notice the only difference is curves vs angles and answer by theory; the paper's long exposure and many questions partly hide the manipulation.
- Space tier: large 4.9 × 3.9 m (real walking in a room matched to the lab; size read from the PDF figure text, not quotable from the .txt).
- VR or mixed reality: VR — photorealistic furnished rooms replace the real room; mixed reality would mix the player's own furniture with the stimulus. The real couch in the lab is a hint that a mixed-reality anchor (the player's real sofa) could be used, but that is a new experiment.
- Quest 3 feasibility: photorealistic rooms are heavy for a browser; ratings with the controller are easy. Offered only to players with a 4.9 × 3.9 m boundary, or the whole thing turned seated/teleport (a different experiment). Behaviour (path, dwell time, approach to curved furniture) can be logged automatically but was not measured in the paper.
- Replication: the general curvature preference for shapes is meta-analytically reliable but not universal (Chuquichambi 2022, found in search, not read). For interiors the evidence is mixed: this preregistered VR study found no preference (two effects favouring angular), and Vartanian 2013's own approach-avoidance test was null.
- Ethics: none special; about 60 min in VR raised cybersickness — keep sessions short.
- Verdict: room (large space only) — a clean within-person test of a famous but shaky claim, measured by the player's own ratings; needs a big boundary and a shortened (two-room) form, and the expected result is "no difference".
