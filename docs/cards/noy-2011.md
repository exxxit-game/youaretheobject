## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: about 9 minutes of play plus pauses and practice, so roughly 12–15 minutes without the extra 180 s round. Fits.
- Task/tension: playful and absorbing. The tension is social: who leads, and can we move as one without anyone leading?
- Within-person reveal: strong. The player's own velocity trace can be drawn over the partner's, with the 2–3 Hz "follower jitter" highlighted and any "co-confident" stretches marked. The reveal shows who was really leading, which the players often cannot tell themselves.
- Works if the player expects tricks: yes. There is no trick; it is a joint skill measured from motion.
- Live players needed: 2 at the same time. Network delay is the main risk. The paper's key timing is under 40 ms; between homes, each player sees the other's hand late by the one-way network delay (often tens of milliseconds, more across countries). That alone may turn joint improvisation into leader-following, so a remote version is a different experiment until delay is measured and reported. Two headsets on the same local network, or in the same room, are closest to the original.
- Space tier: seated.
- VR or mixed reality: mixed reality suits the original better when both players are in the same room. They face each other across a real table and see each other's real body, as in the lab. With remote players, VR (or mixed reality with the partner's avatar across the player's own table) is needed; seeing an avatar instead of a person is a change.
- Quest 3 feasibility: controller or hand position is tracked every frame (72–120 Hz), finer in time than the paper's 20 ms, so dV, dT and jitter can be computed automatically. There is no physical track: the hand moves freely in 3-D and only the movement along a virtual rail counts, with no force feedback holding it on the rail. That is a change from a physical handle and should be piloted.
- Replication: one lab, 12 experts (about 9 games, 27 JI rounds), plus novices who showed the opposite pattern. Independent replications are not checked here. Most players are novices, so the paper predicts most pairs will do worse without a leader.
- Ethics: none special. No deception. The partner should be anonymous unless both agree otherwise.
- Verdict: room (2 live players) — short, deception-free, measured automatically from motion, with a strong reveal; it needs low network delay, and the headline result is about experts while novices showed the reverse.
