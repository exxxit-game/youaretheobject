# canzoneri-2012 — How close a sound must come before your hand reacts (peripersonal space boundary)

- paper: canzoneri-2012.txt
- citation: Canzoneri, E., Magosso, E., & Serino, A. (2012). Dynamic sounds capture the boundaries of peripersonal space representation in humans. PLoS ONE, 7(9), e44306. University of Bologna. Open access (CC BY).
- status: read

## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: 10–16 minutes for the full 188 trials; a shortened version with fewer delays fits 8–10 minutes.
- Task/tension: a plain speeded task: "say TAH (or press) the moment you feel the buzz, ignore the sound". The tension comes from the sound rushing towards the hand.
- Within-person reveal: yes. The game can draw the player's own reaction-time curve against the sound's distance and mark "your space bubble ends about here", then compare with other players.
- Works if the player expects tricks: likely yes; the effect is a reaction-time speed-up the player cannot easily control, though knowing the hypothesis is untested.
- Space tier: seated (arm on a table; sound source moves about 1 m).
- VR or mixed reality: both work; VR is closer to the original, which was blindfolded (no visual cue to the sound). Mixed reality adds a question the paper did not ask: does seeing the real room and one's real hand change the boundary?
- Quest 3 feasibility: good, with two substitutions. The electric pulse becomes controller vibration in the hand (or the hand resting on the controller); the two loudspeakers become a spatialised virtual sound source moving towards the hand, which is better controlled than intensity panning. The spoken "TAH" needs microphone permission and a simple loudness onset detector; a button press is the fallback (a different response mode). Headset audio latency adds a constant that cancels out within the curve.
- Replication: the same dynamic audio-tactile task has been reused by the Bologna/Lausanne group and others (reviewer's knowledge); within this paper there is one main experiment plus a localisation control.
- Ethics: harmless; vibration replaces electric stimulation.
- Verdict: room — automatic, short enough, a personal "bubble size" reveal, and a natural base for multiplayer versions (a real other player approaching).
