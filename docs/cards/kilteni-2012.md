- paper: kilteni-2012.txt
- citation: Kilteni, K., Normand, J.-M., Sanchez-Vives, M. V., & Slater, M. (2012). Extending body space in immersive virtual reality: A very long arm illusion. PLoS ONE, 7(7), e40867. EVENT Lab, Universitat de Barcelona, and IDIBAPS, Barcelona. Open access (CC BY).
- status: read

## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: 5–8 minutes (calibration, 2 minutes of stroking, pointing trials, saw, questionnaire).
- Task/tension: "keep stroking the surface while you watch your hand" — the arm stretches across the room; the saw at the end gives a jolt.
- Within-person reveal: yes. The game can show where the player pointed with eyes closed (drift towards the far virtual hand) and how much the hand flinched under the saw, next to their questionnaire answers.
- Works if the player expects tricks: probably; ownership questionnaires are open to expectation, but the flinch and pointing are harder to fake.
- Space tier: standing (feet fixed; arm movements only).
- VR or mixed reality: VR, as in the original: the real arm must be hidden so that only the long virtual arm is seen. A real surface is still needed for the touch (the original's carton box): the player's own table, registered once by touching its edge.
- Quest 3 feasibility: good. Hand tracking or a controller drives the virtual arm; the stroking surface is the real table; pointing with the other hand while the screen is black is easy to record; hand movement during the saw comes from tracking. Between-group design means each player gets one length; statistics across players give the curve.
- Replication: in the paper, a single 50-person study plus about 60 pilots from one lab; the broader virtual-hand ownership literature supports the basic effect (reviewer's knowledge), but the very-long-arm limit itself is not independently replicated here.
- Ethics: the saw threat is mild; the authors restored the normal arm before removing the headset and followed up two weeks later, which the game should copy (show the normal arm at the end).
- Verdict: room — a body-ownership room that needs only one player, the hands and a real table, with automatic measures; between-group design needs many players.
