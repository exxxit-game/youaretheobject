## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: not stated. The being-approached half (8 directions × light/dark = 16 approaches) is probably 6–10 min; all 32 trials perhaps 15–20 min.
- Task/tension: a stranger walks towards you, sometimes from behind, and you say "stop" when it feels too close. Mild, real discomfort; no trick needed.
- Within-person reveal: strong — the player's own "bubble" drawn from their 8 stop distances (long in front, short behind, growing behind in the dark).
- Works if the player expects tricks: yes, there is no deception; but the stop point is voluntary, so a player can "perform" a distance.
- Space tier: large 8 × 6.5 m (the original room; tape lengths not given). The being-approached measurements (A and C) need only standing in place, because the other person walks.
- VR or mixed reality: mixed reality suits the original better (a real room and a person stepping into it); the dark condition is easier to control in VR. A live second player's avatar would be closer to the original's real approacher than a scripted one.
- Quest 3 feasibility: stop distances come straight from tracked positions; footsteps from behind via spatial sound; head tracking can check the "face forward" rule that the original only instructed. No eye tracking: the front "eye contact" can only be the avatar looking at the player's head. Light levels in the headset are not the original 60 / 0.2 ft-L.
- Replication: none reported in this paper; the author herself limits the personality ratio to these conditions. Related distance behaviour towards virtual humans is in `bailenson-2003`.
- Ethics: being approached from behind can be unpleasant — always allow "stop" and exit. The original sorted people by a personality test; any extraversion score must stay on the device.
- Verdict: room — the being-approached half is a standing room with a vivid personal result; the walking half needs a large boundary.
