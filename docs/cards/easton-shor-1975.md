## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: the original is about 20 minutes (5 min practice and 24 trials of 36 s or more). A trimmed version with a few trials per condition fits 8–12 minutes.
- Task/tension: "imagine the pendulum swinging, but do not move it", while it starts to swing.
- Within-person reveal: yes, and stronger than in chevreul-biorxiv. Every condition is within one person. The game can show the player's own swing size with the bob visible vs hidden and with vs without the moving line. The earlier "imagine it swinging vs imagine it still" contrast (about 10 times) is the cleanest one-player reveal. Another striking reveal: the prompt people felt most (the moving tone) was not the one that moved the bob most (the moving line).
- Works if the player expects tricks: probably. Participants were told to imagine and not to move it on purpose, so naivety was not required. But this paper, like the preprint, never asked whether people felt they had moved it themselves, so the "it swung by itself" feeling is assumed, not measured.
- Space tier: standing (in place behind a backboard, arm extended, a screen 1 m in front).
- VR or mixed reality: VR suits the original better. It used a black backboard for contrast and a board to hide the bob; in VR the bob can be hidden simply by not drawing it, and the moving line is a virtual screen. Mixed reality is possible (a virtual pendulum in the player's room) but adds nothing the original had.
- Quest 3 feasibility: the measure (swing amplitude) is automatic from a simulated pendulum driven by the tracked hand, and the prompts (a moving line, a tone panning between the ears) are trivial to build. The open risks are those of chevreul-biorxiv. There is no real 86 g weight (no force feedback). The original's swings were only 2–7° on a 61 cm string, driven by very small hand movements, so tracking jitter and smoothing must be small compared with those movements, or they will drive or damp the virtual bob themselves. The physics must keep the real 61 cm period.
- Replication: one small study (15 people); its earlier conference study and the 2019 preprint agree that sight increases the effect. What it adds to chevreul-biorxiv: a peer-reviewed within-person protocol, an automatic amplitude measure, and two VR-friendly boosters (a moving line on a screen, a moving tone). It also adds that the swing persists, weaker, with the bob hidden and no prompt. It does not contradict the preprint's numbers (40 cm best, 31% success without vision), but it reports no success rates at all, only mean angles. The "60 of 75 people" figure on the chevreul-biorxiv card is the preprint's citation of Easton & Shor 1976 (J. Gen. Psychol.), a different paper; it is not in this one.
- Ethics: none special; the "imagination and concentration" framing is mild and needs only a short debrief.
- Verdict: room — a standing, within-person, automatically measured protocol with VR-native prompts and a strong own-hand reveal; still 15 people, a real weighted pendulum and no measure of felt agency, so a pilot with a weightless virtual bob comes first.
