## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: 3 min per scene; all four scenes with short ratings would be about 15 min, one awe scene about 4 min.
- Task/tension: low — walk a path to a vista; the experience is the point, there is no trick.
- Within-person reveal: mild — the player's own awe ratings across scenes (within subject, as in the paper); nothing hidden is revealed.
- Works if the player expects tricks: yes, there is nothing to see through; but every rating is self-report and players know the vistas are meant to impress.
- Space tier: standing (stood in place; moved along the path with the controller).
- VR or mixed reality: VR — vastness (deep space, mountain panorama) needs the walls gone; mixed reality would keep the player's room, the opposite of vastness.
- Quest 3 feasibility: easy; thumbstick movement along a fixed path at a fixed speed (or teleport, which changes the experience); ratings with the controller. Measured only by self-report; no automatic measure of awe.
- Replication: the paper validates its own scenes in one sample of 36; the authors' earlier 360-video study is cited. Independent VR awe studies exist but were not read for this card. Claims about what awe does afterwards are shakier: the "awe expands time" effect failed to replicate (van Elk & Rotteveel 2019, found in search, not read). The neutral scene is not a clean control (silent, walled in), and the statistics contain copy errors.
- Ethics: none special; smooth locomotion can cause sickness; the mountain scene may have heights.
- Verdict: interlude — a reliable, pleasant mood scene (awe roughly from 2 to 5 of 7) with no trick and only self-report; useful as a palate cleanser or as an awe induction before another room, not as an experiment with a reveal.
