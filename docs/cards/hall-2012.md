## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: duration not stated ("short survey"); 24 ratings plus reading and discussing three, likely 5–10 min.
- Task/tension: state your moral views, then defend them aloud — real personal stakes.
- Within-person reveal: the strongest in this set — show the player's original mark next to the reversed sentence and replay their own voice arguing the opposite.
- Works if the player expects tricks: doubtful — detections were self-corrections on re-reading, and in a game every player knows text can change; a trick-aware player who re-reads carefully will catch more reversals.
- Space tier: standing (in place) — clipboard survey handed to passers-by in a park; posture not stated; no walking.
- Quest 3 feasibility: a virtual clipboard and swapped text are trivial (no glue trick needed); marking ratings and whether the player changes a mark back are automatic. The arguments are spoken: the room needs voice capture and speech analysis (the paper used three human raters), and an NPC who summarises "so you don't agree that …?" in every language, with each statement translated in both directions.
- Replication: one study (160 people, two conditions) by the choice-blindness group; no independent replication in the paper.
- Ethics: heavy — real moral and political items (Israel–Hamas, buying sex, illegal immigrants) and recorded spoken opinions are sensitive personal data; the authors warn reversed attitudes might persist without debriefing. Object would need neutral statements, on-device processing, explicit consent and a full reveal.
- Verdict: room — the most powerful own-voice reveal and easy to build, but only with neutral statements and a full debrief, and trick-aware players will detect more; not a first room.
