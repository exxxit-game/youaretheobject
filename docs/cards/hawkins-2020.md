## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: not stated. 72 trials of describing and clicking is likely 20–30 minutes; 3–4 blocks would fit 10–15 minutes, but that is a shortened design (most of the reduction should happen early, which the paper's curve suggests but this card does not quantify).
- Task/tension: cooperative puzzle: make your partner find the right odd shape. Light tension, and funny names emerge.
- Within-person reveal: very strong. The pair's own first description of a figure can be shown next to their last ("the one that looks like an upside-down martini glass in a wire stand" → "martini" is the paper's example), with their own words-per-round curve against the paper's.
- Works if the player expects tricks: yes. There is no trick; the shortening happens even when you know about it.
- Live players needed: 2 at the same time, for the whole game. The paper lost games to server errors or disconnection, which is a warning for a web game.
- Space tier: seated.
- VR or mixed reality: neither matters for the task. The figures can lie on a virtual table, or on the player's real table in mixed reality; there is no advantage either way.
- Quest 3 feasibility: easy for text, but typing in a headset is slow. Speaking is the natural input and is what the classic spoken studies used ("text-based (vs. spoken)"). A voice version could measure speaking time per figure on the headset (voice activity only, no words), or word counts if speech-to-text is available in the Quest Browser (unverified). Clicks, accuracy and timings are logged automatically.
- Replication: the reduction effect is described as highly reliable across the literature, and this pre-registered online study replicated it with real pairs.
- Ethics: no deception. Voice and chat are personal: players may say unrelated things (the paper had to remove greetings and personal remarks). Audio should stay on the headset, with only durations or counts sent, and the partner's voice should be heard only live, never stored.
- Verdict: room (2 live players, voice) — robust, deception-free, automatic, with one of the best reveals in the set (your own words shrinking); it needs two players for the whole game and a shorter version than 72 trials.
