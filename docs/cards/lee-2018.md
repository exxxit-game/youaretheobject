## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: the original is long (40 round trips plus questionnaires; total not stated). A game version with a few passes per behaviour (for example 2 × 3 behaviours plus 1–2 empty walks) would take about 4–6 minutes. That is an estimate, not the paper's protocol.
- Task/tension: none. It is plain walking past someone, and the measure is hidden in the path.
- Within-person reveal: partial. The paper's headline contrast (virtual vs real human) needs a real actor, which a player at home does not have. What one player can see is their own path drawn around a standing, a jumping and a walking virtual person against their empty-runway baseline. The paper found that gap grew in that order.
- Works if the player expects tricks: probably yes. Walking clearance is hard to control consciously. But a player told to "walk naturally" in a game may walk straight through on purpose, to test it.
- Space tier: large 6.4 × 2.13 m (the runway; the analysed stretch was the central 4 m).
- VR or mixed reality: mixed reality is the original. But it was optical see-through with a 30° × 17° window. Quest 3 video passthrough has no "unaugmented periphery", so the paper's field-of-view manipulation does not transfer, and the effect sizes may differ.
- Quest 3 feasibility: the virtual-human half is straightforward: an animated person in the passthrough room and the head path from WebXR, with passing distance computed exactly as in the paper. The floor-vibration condition needed a transducer under a platform and is not available. The real-human condition is impossible solo.
- Replication: one study of 26. The virtual > real distance effect matches a prior VR study the authors cite.
- Ethics: none special beyond walking safety in the player's own room. The player must stay inside the boundary while looking at a virtual person.
- Verdict: interlude (large 6.4 × 2.13 m only): an automatic, hidden proxemics measure in MR, but the paper's key comparison with a real person cannot be run by one player at home.
