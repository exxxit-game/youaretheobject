## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: under a minute (two trials).
- Task/tension: none; a quick intuitive choice.
- Within-person reveal: weak in the paper's form: two choices, compared with 72% worldwide and the player's language group. Not a surprise for anyone who already knows the effect.
- Works if the player expects tricks: poorly. Bouba/kiki is one of the best-known effects in popular psychology; a player who knows it will "answer correctly" on purpose, and the game cannot tell knowing from feeling.
- Space tier: seated.
- VR or mixed reality: neither adds to the original (an online survey with sound); VR would only let the shapes be 3D objects, which is a change.
- Quest 3 feasibility: trivial (spatial sound, two objects, a pointer choice). The paper's audio files are on OSF (https://osf.io/w7crs, licence not checked).
- Replication: very robust (large cross-cultural sample, many earlier replications); the kiki half is weaker than the bouba half.
- Ethics: none.
- Verdict: interlude: robust and cross-cultural but only two trials, well known, and it does not use the body or space; at most a 30-second moment between rooms, never a room.
