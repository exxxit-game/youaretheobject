## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: shock part about 10 min — fits 5–15. The full original (baseline 5 + learning 10 + relaxation 5 + interview) is over 20 min; the 5-min rest periods served the skin-conductance baseline, which Quest cannot record anyway.
- Task/tension: very high — read word sets, turn a dial, press a shock button on a woman who protests, shouts "Stop the experiment!" and finally slumps silent.
- Within-person reveal: partial. Automatic and personal: shocks given, the shock at which the player stopped (if any), the pause before each shock, and the pause at the silent questions 28–29, comparable to the paper's medians (23 s Visible vs 7 s Hidden). The paper's headline contrast is between groups (Visible vs Hidden), and its stress evidence was skin conductance and ECG, which Quest cannot measure.
- Works if the player expects tricks: the paper used no deception — everyone knew she was virtual — so "knowing it is fake" is already built in. But knowing Milgram changes behaviour: one participant was excluded for deciding in advance to quit, and players of a psychology game will know Milgram and may stop or push on to "beat" the test. Real risk.
- Space tier: seated (participant seated at a desk with the shock machine).
- Quest 3 feasibility: seated desk, dial and button with controllers or hands — easy. Two real changes: (1) the original prod came from a real experimenter sitting beside the participant; at home it must be a virtual experimenter, which the paper only plans and did not test; (2) Cave projection vs headset. The hesitation timing in the paper ran from end of reading to saying "Incorrect" — at home this needs speech detection or a "wrong" button (a slightly different measure).
- Replication: none in the paper; one lab, n = 34 with unequal groups (23 vs 11) assigned "arbitrarily", not randomly.
- Ethics: high. The original had ethics approval, no deception, an experimenter present, a "Nothing happened, I'm fine" ending and a check for ill-effects. At home nobody is there: needs a content warning, an always-visible stop option with no penalty, a compulsory debrief ending as in the paper, no score that rewards finishing, and an age gate (simulated violence against a woman).
- Verdict: room — a strong, seated 10-minute scene with automatic stop/hesitation measures; not a first room, because distress is high at home, the authority figure must become virtual (untested), and the paper's stress evidence was physiological.
