## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: under a minute in the tunnel (a few seconds before moving, 35–43 s of walking) plus training. Short.
- Task/tension: the same smoke-filled tunnel as `kinateder-ronchi-2014`, now with people standing around who ignore the fire. The fire and the exit are visible from the start, so the situation is not ambiguous.
- Within-person reveal: weak. The only effect is that paths bent towards the first group of bystanders, shown as heat maps without a statistical test. Leaving, timing and destination did not change, so a reveal has little to show.
- Works if the player expects tricks: the main finding is a null result: bystanders who ignored a visible fire did not hold anyone back, even among naive participants. A suspicious player is unlikely to change that.
- Space tier: standing. Travel by joystick inside a projection room; nobody walked the tunnel.
- VR or mixed reality: VR. A road tunnel cannot be placed in the player's room.
- Quest 3 feasibility: easy: the same tunnel with 3 or 19 standing NPCs, thumbstick travel, logged head position.
- Replication: a variation of `kinateder-ronchi-2014` by the same lab; 20 per group, and the "alone" group is borrowed from the earlier study rather than randomised at the same time. Unlike Latané & Darley (1968) and the passive-bystander arm of Kinateder & Warren (2016), passive bystanders here did not delay or stop leaving; the exit was visible from the start, and the paper itself says the result may depend on where the exit and the fire are.
- Ethics: fire, smoke and a tunnel; warning for fire and tunnel fear and a way out at any time.
- Verdict: reject — the effect on leaving is null and the path effect is descriptive only, with a borrowed control group. Useful as evidence that passive NPCs do not hold people back when the fire is plainly visible.
