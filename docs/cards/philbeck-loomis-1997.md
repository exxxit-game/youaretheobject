## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: the paper's 1.5 h covered many conditions and spoken answers; walking only, 4 distances × lit/dark, is 8 trials of about a minute — 8–10 minutes.
- Task/tension: look, close your eyes, walk, stop — a small dare with your own legs; low drama.
- Within-person reveal: yes. A top-view replay of where the player stopped against where the target stood, lit room vs. dark: in the dark the stops bunch together, in the lit room they land on the targets. Each observer did every condition in the paper, so the comparison is within-person as in the original.
- Works if the player expects tricks: yes — nothing is hidden; knowing about the dark compression should not remove it (not tested here).
- Space tier: large about 5 × 1.8 m — targets up to 5 m straight ahead, walked in a 1.8 m wide hallway. Never shrunk: the dark compression shows mainly at the far targets, so a short version is a different experiment.
- VR or mixed reality: mixed reality suits the lit condition much better. The original lit condition is a real, lit room; in MR the player sees their real room with the virtual target on the real floor, while in VR the room is virtual — and blind walking in headsets falls short of targets (not in this paper; Thompson et al. 2004 on the same Loomis page report VR judgments "consistently underestimated", not carded). The dark condition is the same in VR and MR (black screen, one glowing rectangle). So the room could measure, per player, the gap between their real room and darkness — and answer the open question whether MR passthrough brings blind walking back to real-world accuracy.
- Quest 3 feasibility: automatic — the headset position where the player stops; the black screen replaces closed eyes. Passthrough cameras have their own distortions, so whether a virtual target on the real floor is seen at its true distance is unknown (that is the measurement). Needs a clear straight 5 m inside the boundary, and the boundary must still warn during the black-screen walk (to check); the original's dim guide lights along the floor could be a faint line.
- Replication: the paper's three experiments agree, and the authors list several labs with accurate blind walking (Rieser 1990, Loomis 1992, Thomson 1983 and others); only 8 people in Exp. 3.
- Ethics: walking blind for 5 m at home — trip and bump risk; needs a cleared floor.
- Verdict: room (large space only) — the cleanest test of whether MR removes the distance shrinkage VR is known for, automatic and personal; but it needs about 5 m of straight floor, Exp. 3 had 8 people, and the dry task needs a game frame.
