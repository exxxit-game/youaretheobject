## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: Exp. 2 is 8 pours, about 3–5 minutes; Exp. 1 needs two sessions a week apart (the game could not reproduce that and would use Exp. 2).
- Task/tension: a plain everyday act (pour a shot, or leave room for one); no tension, the measure is hidden in the poured amount.
- Within-person reveal: strong. Every player pours into both glasses under both instructions, so the game can show "you poured a third more 'shot' into the wide glass" and then the reversal when they left room. In the paper 92% of people showed the shot bias.
- Works if the player expects tricks: uncertain. A player who notices that the two glasses hold the same volume may correct; in Exp. 2 most participants guessed the volumes were identical and the bias still appeared, which is encouraging.
- Space tier: seated.
- VR or mixed reality: mixed reality suits the original better: a desk, a jug and glasses in the player's own room, on their real table.
- Quest 3 feasibility: the poured amount is exact and automatic (volume = flow integrated over time, level from the glass shape). The weak point is the hands: the original jug weighed about 1.5 kg and the glass could be touched; in the headset the pour is controlled by tilting a controller or hand with no weight and no flow feel, so the motor side differs and the size of the effect may not transfer. Liquid can be drawn simply (a level plane in a cylinder) without fluid simulation.
- Replication: this study is itself an independent test of the US pouring findings: the shot bias held, the full-glass case reversed. The original US pouring studies come from the Wansink lab, many of whose other papers were later retracted, so this Dutch study is the safer source. Samples are small (42 and 24).
- Ethics: none special (water or lemonade only; no alcohol needed, the paper used lemonade and water).
- Verdict: interlude: a 3–5 minute hidden within-person measure that fits mixed reality on the player's table; the missing weight of the jug is an untested change, so pilot whether the bias survives tilt-to-pour.
