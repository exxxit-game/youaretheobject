## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: the original takes 100 judgments per session over several sessions on different days, which is far beyond 5–15 minutes. A playable version would need far fewer trials (for example 20–30 alone, then 20–30 with the group). That shortened design is untested in this paper.
- Task/tension: low tension: "how far did the dot move?". The surprise is all in the reveal: the dot never moved, and your numbers drifted toward the voices.
- Within-person reveal: strong. Sherif's own design is within-person (alone first, then group, or the reverse). The player's own median before and after the group can be shown as the paper's "funnel".
- Works if the player expects tricks: partly. The illusion itself survives knowing that the light is still (p. 18). But the original used naive subjects, and Sherif hid the direction question so that they would not discover the illusion. Original groups were all naive people with no confederates. In Object the "group" would be scripted NPC voices. That is a different design (closer to later confederate versions), and a suspicious player may simply ignore the voices.
- Space tier: seated (the 5 m to the light is a virtual distance; the subject sat, with head in a head-rest in the first experiment).
- VR or mixed reality: VR only. Mixed reality shows the real room, which gives exactly the frame of reference that destroys the illusion.
- Quest 3 feasibility: uncertain. The effect needs total darkness and a single tiny, faint point with no frame of reference ("The addition of one more light disturbs the illusion"). Quest 3's LCD panels do not show true black. Lens glare and the headset's visible edge may act as reference points. Also, a world-locked dot moves on the screen with every small head motion. Whether the autokinetic effect appears at all in Quest 3 must be pilot-tested before anything else. If it does, the answers (a slider or a number) are logged automatically.
- Replication: within this paper, the direction (suggestion) experiment was run at three sites (Ankara 1932, Harvard 1933, Columbia 1934). The group range experiment is one series of 16 groups at one site.
- Ethics: mild deception (the instructions say the light will move; it is fixed; the group voices would be scripted). A debrief is needed. Being in near-total darkness in a headset may be uncomfortable for some players.
- Verdict: room — seated, short when trimmed and with a clean within-person reveal, but whether the illusion even works in a Quest 3 (no true black) is unknown, and scripted voices replace Sherif's naive groups; pilot first.
