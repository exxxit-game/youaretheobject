## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: not stated. A still-ball series (2 cm steps, up and down, three heights, two sides) is perhaps 5–10 minutes; the wall version of Exp. 6 (two judgments standing, two kneeling, then one real touch) about 3–5 minutes.
- Task/tension: a small bet on your own body — "could you touch it?" — then the truth. Low drama, but about the player themself.
- Within-person reveal: very strong and automatic: judged distance against the measured arm reach, in centimetres and percent ("you thought you could reach 13–18% farther than you can"); kneeling shows the error shrink.
- Works if the player expects tricks: partly. There is no hidden trick, but a player who knows the bias can correct for it; an informed sample was not tested.
- Space tier: standing for Exp. 1–2 still ball (back against a board). Roomscale for Exp. 6 (moving toward a real wall from up to 184 cm). The moving-ball condition's pitching machine 4 m away does not matter in a headset (the ball is virtual).
- VR or mixed reality: mixed reality suits the original clearly better. The judgment is about the player's own arm against real surroundings: in MR the player sees their real arm and body and the real wall; the ball or dot sits on the real wall (plane detection), and the actual reach can be taken by touching that same wall. In a fully virtual booth the player sees no real arm (controllers or a generic avatar), and distances in VR are known to look shorter (see philbeck-loomis-1997), both of which would change a body-scaled distance judgment.
- Quest 3 feasibility: automatic — yes/no by button, ball position known, actual reach from the controller at full arm extension, standing position from the headset's distance to the wall plane. Risks: the Meta boundary is usually drawn inside the walls and may interrupt the approach; a controller can knock the wall; plane detection is "Meta" status, unverified on our headset; leaning cannot be prevented, only detected from head position.
- Replication: six experiments in the paper all found overestimation; the authors cite earlier overestimates (Carello et al. 1989; Bootsma et al. 1992) and say their own effect is larger than others' — the size (9–28%) depends on the setup.
- Ethics: none special; kneeling optional.
- Verdict: room — short, automatic, a clean personal "you overestimate your reach" number, and MR is clearly closer to the original than a virtual booth (real arm, real wall); but the effect size depends on the setup and a tricks-aware player may correct for it, so it is a solid room rather than a big surprise.
