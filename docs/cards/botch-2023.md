## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: not stated. 54 scene trials of up to 30 s and 180 letter trials of up to 10 s suggest 20–35 min. About 18–27 scene trials, balanced across clutter bins, would take about 5–8 min.
- Task/tension: game-like. "Find the green bottle" in a real room photographed in 360°, against the clock.
- Within-person reveal: modest. The game can show "each step of clutter cost you X seconds", but nobody is surprised that clutter slows search. The paper also found the personal clutter cost in scenes unreliable (split-half 0.29).
- Works if the player expects tricks: yes; nothing is hidden.
- Space tier: standing (turning in place; posture not stated; the scene covered 270° and needed head turns).
- VR or mixed reality: VR, as in the original (real-world photospheres replace the room). Searching for virtual objects among the player's own real furniture in mixed reality would be a different experiment.
- Quest 3 feasibility: very high. The original ran on a Quest 2 in participants' homes, with answers by head direction and trigger and no eye tracking, almost exactly Object's setup. The game would need its own licensed 360° indoor photos (the paper used Flickr photospheres) and a clutter measure for each.
- Replication: the set-size effect is one of the most replicated findings in attention (Treisman & Gelade 1980 onward); here it was seen in both tasks with 75 people. The cross-task link (r = 0.36) is modest and comes from this one study.
- Ethics: none.
- Verdict: interlude. It is directly feasible on Quest at home, but the reveal is unsurprising. It is best used as a short warm-up or tutorial that also gives a personal attention baseline for other rooms.
