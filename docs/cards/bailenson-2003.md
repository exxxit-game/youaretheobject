## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: one block of 5 label-reading trials took 5–15 min — fits; the full Exp. 1 (two blocks plus recall test) is longer.
- Task/tension: low. A memory errand (read the back label, then the front) is the cover; the measure is how close the player walks. Exp. 2's walk-through is a short startle.
- Within-person reveal: yes, for the front/back bubble — distances are logged automatically from the headset, and a top-down replay can show the player's own path and the gap left at the face vs the back (within person in the paper, t(79) = 5.01). The difference is small (about 6 cm), so one player's result will be noisy. Gaze and agency effects were between-person; the female/male difference was within-person.
- Works if the player expects tricks: uncertain. The memory cover fooled everyone in the paper, but a player of a psychology game will suspect the stranger and may walk up close on purpose. The "avatar" condition needs a rigged coin draw and confederates — not possible honestly at home, so only the "computer person" condition can be offered.
- Space tier: large 7.2 × 6.4 m (Exp. 1, real walking around the virtual person); Exp. 2 large 3 × 2.4 m (physical room; the key walk-through moment itself is standing).
- Quest 3 feasibility: the measure is a natural fit — Quest logs head position far faster than 18 Hz, no eye tracking needed (the gaze is the virtual person's). Exp. 1 is offered only to players with about a 7.2 × 6.4 m boundary, Exp. 2 to players with 3 × 2.4 m; not shrunk.
- Replication: Exp. 1 repeats the same lab's 2001 study; no independent replication in the paper.
- Ethics: none special for the agent-only version; the avatar version needs deception and is dropped.
- Verdict: room (large space only) — automatic, covert distance measure with a personal front/back replay; not a first room, because it needs 3 × 2.4 m to 7.2 × 6.4 m and the effect per player is only centimetres.
