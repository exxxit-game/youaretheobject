## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: 1 hour in the original, including forms and sickness questionnaires. 60 trials per mode at about 5–8 s each is about 5–8 minutes per mode, so both modes plus the session switch take about 12–18 minutes. That is an estimate, at the upper edge.
- Task/tension: dry. "Which cube was bigger?" sixty times per mode; there is nothing to resist and no surprise in the moment.
- Within-person reveal: possible. The paper fitted a curve per participant, so a player can see their own precision in VR and in their own room. But the paper's group difference is small (PSE 1010.9 vs 1022.6 cm³, about 1%), and 60 trials per mode give noisy personal curves.
- Works if the player expects tricks: yes; there is no deception.
- Space tier: seated.
- VR or mixed reality: both, as the two conditions. This is the only paper in this batch that compares VR with video passthrough on one headset, which is exactly what Quest 3 offers (`immersive-vr` vs `immersive-ar`). The paper itself names the low-resolution passthrough background (480p vs 1440p) as a possible cause of the AR result. Quest 3's passthrough is a different camera system (likely sharper, not checked here), so its result may differ.
- Quest 3 feasibility: easy to build: a cube attached to the controller, two buttons, automatic logging. Switching between VR and mixed reality ends one session and needs a button press (`docs/headset-capabilities.md`). The size steps are tiny: 3% in volume is about 1% in edge length, roughly 1 mm on a 10 cm cube at forearm distance. Whether Quest 3 renders that difference cleanly is unverified.
- Replication: one study of 22 on one headset. The reported JNDs do not match the paper's own table and definition. Half of the 25–75% range is (1081.6 − 952.5) / 2 ≈ 64.6 cm³ for AR and (1062.2 − 949.8) / 2 ≈ 56.2 cm³ for VR, not 17.05 and 6. With the recomputed values, the "VR is more sensitive" headline shrinks to a small difference that the paper does not test. The tested result is the PSE shift. Both cubes in a trial are always in the same mode, so that shift is a bias toward the reference cube, not evidence that "objects are perceived as larger in VR" as the paper words it. No data are shared to check this.
- Comparison with jones-2008: the authors expected VR to be worse (following Jones's VR distance shortfall) and found the opposite for size. Both papers are small and used older headsets, so neither settles how perception differs between VR and Quest 3 passthrough.
- Ethics: mild eyestrain and fatigue in the original; none special otherwise.
- Verdict: interlude — a seated, automatic VR-vs-mixed-reality perception check on exactly the comparison Quest 3 offers, but the task is dry, the effect tiny, the paper's own threshold numbers are inconsistent, and its likely cause (a blurry passthrough) differs on Quest 3.
