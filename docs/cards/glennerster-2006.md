## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: not stated; 120-trial runs are far too long. A version with about 20–30 walks across the room of a few seconds each would take about 5–10 min (estimate, untested).
- Task/tension: remember a cube, walk across, say bigger or smaller — a simple memory/size game; the tension comes in the reveal.
- Within-person reveal: very strong — replay the walk and show that the room quadrupled while the player crossed it, and that their "same size" cube was 2–4× off.
- Works if the player expects tricks: probably — two of the five subjects were authors who knew the design, and the paper says no subject noticed; but that is only five people.
- Space tier: large, about 3.5 m of sideways walking (the walk length is not in the text; the Figure 1 inset graph runs from 0 to about 3.5 m of distance). Offered only to players whose boundary allows about 3.5 m in one direction, never shrunk.
- Quest 3 feasibility: 6DoF stereo with lower lag than the 48–50 ms here; no eye tracking or force feedback needed; the scene change is pure software. The lab used a real table edge to keep the walk straight — at home a floor line in VR would replace it (untested).
- Replication: only this paper (5 subjects, 2 of them authors); no independent replication in the text.
- Ethics: walking with a headset — needs a clear, straight path inside the boundary; otherwise none special.
- Verdict: room (large space only) — the strongest within-person reveal in this set and it held for informed observers, measured automatically; but only five subjects and it needs about 3.5 m of walking.
