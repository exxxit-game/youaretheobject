## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: not stated. A one-player version of the Exp. 2 method (a few 5-second shouts or claps alone, "with one other" and "with five others", with rests) would take about 4–6 minutes. That is an estimate, not the paper's protocol.
- Task/tension: plain effort on command; the loafing is not noticed by the person. At home, shouting at full voice is awkward and disturbs others (the paper saw embarrassment even in a soundproofed lab). Clapping is gentler and showed the same group-size effect in Exp. 1.
- Within-person reveal: strong in principle. The pseudogroup design is within-person by construction: "when you believed five others were shouting, you shouted N% as hard as alone", next to the paper's 82% and 74%.
- Works if the player expects tricks: doubtful for the solo version. The player must believe that others shout too, and a player in a known psychology game may see through a recorded crowd. With real live players no belief is needed (next line).
- Live players needed: none for the paper's own Exp. 2 method (the player is alone and only told that others shout, with a recorded crowd in the ears). Real groups need 2–6. Separate headsets give something the original could not: each voice goes into its own microphone, so a player's own effort inside a real group is measured with no coordination loss and no deception. The paper's explanation is that individual output is unidentifiable, so the game must not show individual scores during group trials. It reveals them in the debrief.
- Comparison with the Ringelmann/Ingham note in objekt-files/notes/archive/docs/experiments.md: that note files social loafing under "need extra hardware" (a force sensor for the rope). This paper reproduces both Ringelmann's group loss (Exp. 1) and Ingham's believed-partner loss (Exp. 2) with sound instead of force. The hardware blocker becomes a microphone question.
- Space tier: seated (Exp. 1: seated in a semicircle 1 m apart; Exp. 2: posture not stated).
- VR or mixed reality: VR suits Exp. 2 better: blindfold plus earphones is a dark or neutral headset view with the crowd sound in the headset. Exp. 1 (seeing each other) needs co-located players in mixed reality, or avatars.
- Quest 3 feasibility: the measure is a loudness number per trial from the headset microphone, computed on the headset. Three things are unverified. (1) The microphone works inside a Quest Browser WebXR session. (2) Browsers apply automatic gain control and noise suppression to voice by default, which would flatten a shout; both must be switched off, and Quest Browser must honour that. (3) A shout a few centimetres from the headset microphone may clip. The paper's 90-dB masking noise should not be copied through the headset speakers (hearing safety). Without it the player hears their own voice and their quiet room, which may give away a pseudogroup.
- Replication: two experiments in the paper, with only 8 and 6 groups as the units of analysis and only male students. Exp. 1 conceptually replicates Ringelmann and Exp. 2 replicates Ingham et al. with a different task. Independent replications are not checked here.
- Ethics: Exp. 2 deceives about the others shouting; real live players avoid this. Shouting brings voice strain, embarrassment, and disturbance to family and neighbours. Any masking sound must stay at a safe level. No audio may leave the headset, only one loudness number per trial.
- Verdict: room (pilot first) — short, automatic and within-person, and the headset microphone can replace Ringelmann's force sensor; it depends on unverified microphone loudness (no auto-gain, no clipping), on shouting or clapping being acceptable at home, and, for the solo version, on players believing in the other shouters.
