## Fit for Object (reviewer's judgment, not facts)
- Participant time: not stated. Learning rounds of 30 s until correct twice, then 24 trials with a turn, a look, eyes closed and a point — roughly 10–15 minutes.
- Task/tension: an effortful mental puzzle ("imagine you are at the jar facing the brush"); little drama and tiring.
- Within-person reveal: yes for imagined heading: the player's error and time per heading — "you point well only from the direction you first looked from, even after you turned your body". The body-facing comparison was between groups in the paper; a within-person version is untested.
- Works if the player expects tricks: yes — nothing hidden; it measures how memory is organised.
- Space tier: large about 3.3 × 3.3 m — the whole procedure (layout, the walk to its centre, the turns) fitted in the 3.3 m round room of Exps. 3–4; the size of Exps. 1–2's "large room" is not stated. The walk itself is short.
- VR or mixed reality: MR is somewhat closer: real objects on a real floor are best matched by virtual objects on the player's real floor, walked to and turned among for real, and a virtual booth with stick movement would remove the walking and turning the paper manipulates. But the paper itself shows the room's shape did not matter (a round room gave the same pattern), so the real room adds little beyond real walking; VR with real walking would serve almost as well.
- Quest 3 feasibility: controller pointing replaces the joystick and the headset gives heading — automatic. Eyes-closed tests are a black screen. Needs about 3.3 m of open floor. The spoken "imagine you are at X facing Y" prompts need translation and recording.
- Replication: inside the paper, Exps. 2–4 (egocentric pointing; the round room, 11–12 people) repeated the imagined-heading effect; it builds on the same lab's Shelton & McNamara (2001) and Mou & McNamara (2002). No independent replication checked. It argues against Wang & Spelke (2000) (card wang-spelke-2000).
- Ethics: none special.
- Verdict: room (large space only) — automatic, within-person and replicated inside the paper, but a dry, effortful imagined-pointing task, and MR adds little beyond real walking because the room's shape did not matter; best paired with wang-spelke-2000 as one "pointing in your own room" session.
