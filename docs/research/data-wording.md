### 3b. Article 29 Working Party, "Guidelines on transparency under Regulation 2016/679", WP260 rev.01 (adopted 29 Nov 2017, revised 11 Apr 2018) (read 2026-10-12, full PDF, pdftotext; saved objekt-files/papers/wp29-2018-transparency.txt)
- https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/article29/redirection/document/51025
  - para 8: "present the information/ communication efficiently and succinctly in order to avoid information fatigue."
  - para 9: "intelligible" means "it should be understood by an average member of the intended audience"; test through "user panels, readability testing".
  - para 10: people "should not be taken by surprise at a later point about the ways in which their personal data has been used"; "spell out in unambiguous language what the most important consequences of the processing will be".
  - para 12: "information should be provided in as simple a manner as possible, avoiding complex sentence and language structures. The information should be concrete and definitive; it should not be phrased in abstract or ambivalent terms or leave room for different interpretations."
  - Poor practice example: "We may use your personal data for research purposes" ("as it is unclear what kind of 'research' this refers to").
  - para 13: "Language qualifiers such as "may", "might", "some", "often" and "possible" should also be avoided." ... "Writing should be in the active instead of the passive form and excess nouns should be avoided." ... "should not contain overly legalistic, technical or specialist language"; translations must make sense "so that the translated text does not have to be deciphered or re-interpreted."
  - para 35 (layers): "layered privacy statements/ notices should be used to link to the various categories of information ... rather than displaying all such information in a single notice on the screen, in order to avoid information fatigue"; layers "not merely nested pages that require several clicks"; layers must not "provide conflicting information."
  - para 36: the first layer "should also contain information on the processing which has the most impact on the data subject and processing which could surprise them."
  - para 18: other means: "'just-in-time' contextual pop-up notices, 3D touch or hover-over notices, and privacy dashboards"; also "cartoons, infographics or flowcharts".

### 3c. UK ICO, "How should we draft our privacy information?" (read 2026-10-12 in the browser pane; page notes it is "under review" after the Data (Use and Access) Act; no date shown)
- https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/individual-rights/the-right-to-be-informed/how-should-we-draft-our-privacy-information/

  - Conciseness: "Use an appropriate technique to deliver the information, such as a layered approach." / "Keep your sentences and paragraphs short. Omit any irrelevant or unnecessary information."
  - "Being transparent is about being open, honest and truthful with people"; "Don't offer individuals choices that are counter-intuitive or misleading."; "make sure that you clearly bring to people's attention any uses of data that may be unexpected"
  - "Align your privacy information with your organisation's values and principles. People will be more inclined to read it, understand it, and trust your handling of their personal data."
  - "Ensure that what you say is unambiguous. Be as precise as you can about what you do with people's data."
  - "Use common, everyday language." / "Avoid confusing terminology, jargon, or legalistic language."
  - "it is good practice to provide your privacy information in the language that your intended audience is most likely to understand."
  - Testing: "Carrying out user testing will provide useful feedback on draft privacy information."; example: "you provide a link to more detailed information in all your just-in-time notices"
  - Review: "analyse complaints from the public about how you use their personal data and in particular any complaints about how you explain your use of it."
  - Confirms: "WP29 adopted guidelines on Transparency, which have been endorsed by the EDPB."

### 3c (cont.). UK ICO, "What methods can we use to provide privacy information?" (read 2026-10-12 in the browser pane; same "under review" note)
- https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/individual-rights/the-right-to-be-informed/what-methods-can-we-use-to-provide-privacy-information/
  - "It is good practice to use the same medium you use to collect personal data to deliver privacy information."
  - Layered: "providing people with a short notice containing key information, such as the identity of your organisation and the way you use the personal data"; "There will always be pieces of information that are likely to need to go into the top layer, such as who you are, what information you are collecting and why you need it."
  - "the top layer should always give people prominent, early warning of any use of their information that is likely to be unexpected, objectionable, or significantly affect them."
  - "Look at the experience of others in your sector or industry to see if there has been an approach that has been welcomed by customers or worked particularly well."
  - Just-in-time: "A just-in-time notice appears at the point where an individual provides you with a particular piece of information. The notice gives the individual a brief message explaining how you will use the information they are about to provide."
  - Devices: "Inform individuals when a smart device is observing or recording them using a red light or a sound alert."
  - "Try not to adopt approaches that people won't find intuitive or that result in giving individuals constant alerts regarding their information."

### 5. What drew complaints (read 2026-10-12)

## 6. Wording rules that recur (each with its sources above)
R1. Say plainly what is NOT collected, then at once what IS collected (and what the server sees for a short time). LabintheWild ("We will not collect your name ... However, we will collect ..."), Sea Hero Quest, Edinburgh vet ("We do not collect ... your IP address"), YourMorals (IP explained), Shattered PD (table "What Data is Collected | How it is Used"), WebAsk ([Russian, at the cited source]), ICO top layer ("who you are, what information you are collecting and why").
R2. "Anonymous" only when no one, the team included, can link it to a person; otherwise "confidential"; never call a hash anonymous. Smith IRB (replace 'anonymous' with 'confidential'), TCD ("not be known by anyone, including the researchers"), FTC hashing post, FTC Avast, Crisis Text Line case, AEPD ("false sense of anonymity"), Loughborough (codes make data "no longer anonymous").
R3. Give the reason and the point of no return in one sentence: leave any time by closing/quitting; after submit it cannot be removed because it cannot be found. Loughborough, TCD, Smith, Open University, Edinburgh vet, Aberdeen, Gorilla, Sea Hero Quest ("we will not be able to identify which data is yours"), AHRECS, BPS IMR (final confirm button; say what happens to partial data), Pavlovia post (warn that quitting does not delete).
R4. Concrete, definite words, active voice, no "may/might/some/often/possible", no legal jargon, short sentences, reading age 8-12. WP260 paras 12-13 (incl. poor example "We may use your personal data for research purposes"), GDPR Art 12(1), EDPB 05/2020 para 67, ICO drafting page, UCL (reading age ~8), TCD (age 12, 15-20 words a sentence), AEPD (vague wordings criticised).
R5. Short first layer at the moment of choice, the full page one link away, layers never contradicting; the surprising thing goes in the first layer. WP260 paras 35-36 and 18 (just-in-time), ICO methods page, AEPD 5.2.1 (onboarding, "ongoing dialogue"), Shattered PD ("Let's get right to the important part ... Feel free to read on"), Steed 2016 (voiceover says the answer is not recorded).
R6. State who holds the data, who pays, and that it is not sold or used for ads, in values language; thank people; make "no" a real option. YourMorals ("non-profit academic venture, with no fees or advertising"), Lichess ("does none of these things and never will"), Zooniverse (not commercial, cookies "not used for advertisements"; Data Controller named), TestMyBrain ("will never sell"), TCD ("It is self-funded."; "Thank you for taking the time ..."), UCL ("a friendly invitation", "a thank you"), LabintheWild ("Thank you for your participation!"), ICO ("Align your privacy information with your organisation's values"), Open University (an explicit "not willing" option), Steed 2016 (non-consenters play, nothing recorded); against: PNAS on Facebook (consent buried in a Data Use Policy), Crisis Text Line ("By texting further with us, you agree to our Terms").
