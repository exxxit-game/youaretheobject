# Wording research: data page and consent texts (read 2026-10-12)

> Kept as the helper wrote it on 12.10.2026, for the owner's request that the data page and the consent say it politely and plainly, so no one has a claim (docs/owner-decisions.md). Russian passages are left out of this English copy; their sources are linked. Nothing here is acted on until the wording goes to the owner as options.


Question: how online research sites, ethics templates, regulators and games word anonymity,
"cannot be withdrawn once submitted", no ads / no selling, thanks, playing without recording,
who holds the data; and what drew complaints.

## Findings (appended as found)

### 1a. Project Implicit (read 2026-10-12, WebFetch)
- https://implicit.harvard.edu/implicit/takeatest.html
  - "IP addresses are routinely recorded but are completely confidential."
  - "For research purposes, data without directly identifying information is made publicly available."
  - "Data exchanged with this site are protected by SSL encryption."
  - "You must be 18 years of age or older to participate." / "Visitors between 14-17 years of age may visit Project Implicit Youth."
  - "If you are unprepared to encounter interpretations you might find objectionable, please do not proceed further."
  - Points to "our Data Privacy page". No anonymity, withdrawal, advertising or thanks wording on this page.
  - Note: says "confidential", not "anonymous" (they do record IPs).

### 1b. LabintheWild (read 2026-10-12, WebFetch of the study's locale file)
- https://studies.labinthewild.org/frameline/i18n/en.json
  - "We will not collect your name and contact information."
  - "However, we will collect the city and country you are located in, what browser you are using, your answers to our questions, and data about how you use our website."
  - "We will securely store this data."
  - "No, being in this study is up to you. You can leave now or at any time later."
  - Independent contact: "If you want to talk about the study with someone who is not part of the study team, contact the University of Washington Human Subjects Division"
  - Consent box: "I agree to participate in this research."
  - Elsewhere in the same file: "We take your privacy very seriously." (demographics) and "Thank you for your participation!" (comments)
  - Note: names what IS collected right after what is not ("However, we will collect ...").

### 2a. Loughborough University, Ethics Review Sub-Committee, "Questionnaires and Online Questionnaires" (read 2026-10-12, WebFetch)
- https://www.lboro.ac.uk/internal/research-ethics-integrity/research-ethics/guidance-investigators/questionnaires/
  - "If the Questionnaire is anonymous participants should be advised that they can withdraw by closing the browser during completion of the questionnaire."
  - "However, following submission of the questionnaire the data cannot be withdraw as it will not be identifiable." (sic)
  - "We do not recommend the use of unique or self-created codes to allow otherwise anonymous participants to withdraw their data."
  - "Use of such codes means that the data collected is no longer anonymous" ... "increasing the risk of a breach of personal data."
  - "Consent cannot be assumed by completion of the questionnaire or by selecting 'next' to continue the questionnaire."
  - "We recommend that there should be one box for participants to tick confirming their consent to take part in the study."
  - "investigators should be mindful to ensure that the demographic questions do not result in participants becoming identifiable."
  - The template itself is "available through LEON" (internal, login): not read.

### 2b. Trinity College Dublin, Data Protection Office, "Anonymous research studies" (updated 26 November 2024; read 2026-10-12, WebFetch)
- https://www.tcd.ie/dataprotection/anonymousresearchstudies/
  - "Also advise them that once submitted the survey cannot be withdrawn."
  - "Always ensure that the survey tool is configured to not collect IP addresses and location data"
  - "This means that no data protection rights apply to the project."
  - "Therefore, there should be no reference to data protection law anywhere in the information leaflet."

### 1a (cont.). Project Implicit, Data Privacy page (read 2026-10-12, WebFetch)
- https://implicit.harvard.edu/implicit/privacy.html
  - research data "is associated with an anonymous user number and is stored separately from email addresses"
  - "IP addresses are routinely recorded, but are completely confidential."
  - "Email addresses will never be disclosed or shared with any third-party."
  - Selling, advertising, withdrawal: not addressed on this page.
- (https://www.projectimplicit.net/privacy-policy/ is the WordPress site's comment/cookie policy, not the study's.)

### 1c. TestMyBrain (Many Brains) privacy policy, "Effective Date January 2024" (read 2026-10-12, WebFetch)
- https://testmybrain.org/privacy-policy.html
  - "Anonymous responses to the digital cognitive tests offered on the Website are collected by the institutions ... not by Many Brains."
  - "Procedures for collecting, storing, and securing these data are reviewed by local Institutional Review Boards"
  - "Many Brains will never sell or distribute your personal information."
  - but also "We may share your information with third-party vendors and service-providers that help us with specialized services" and lists "device and advertising identifiers" among data collected; uses cookies "to gather statistics on how you use the site."
  - The per-study consent ("Study Information" form) was not opened: unverified.

### 1d. YourMorals.org (read 2026-10-12 in the browser pane; WebFetch got only the title, the site is a script app)
- https://www.yourmorals.org/
  - "This site is a non-profit academic venture, with no fees or advertising. We will guard your privacy carefully."
  - "By filling out a few of our surveys, you'll help us answer those questions. We, in return, will give you an immediate report"
- https://www.yourmorals.org/privacy ("This statement is effective as of May 18, 2021.")
  - "we will never give out or sell your email address to anyone for any reason."
  - "You are free to withdraw your consent at any point while taking the questionnaire. To withdraw consent, close your browser window."
  - "These files of information do NOT contain your email or password, so these merged files are essentially anonymous."
  - plain IP explanation: "most IP addresses are not fixed and are traceable only to the organization which provides you with internet access."
  - "we will always strip out IP address information from any file that is sent out beyond our small group of researchers."
  - "We will write up scientific articles that report the results of our studies by reporting the averages and other statistics computed across many people's data."
  - "We do not use cookies."
  - (registered accounts, so deletion is possible there: "You can request all your data to be completely deleted from our servers.")
  - Note the hedges they still use: "essentially anonymous", "We may also use common 3rd party services from companies like Google and Facebook".

### 1e. Zooniverse "User Agreement and Privacy Policy" (read 2026-10-12 in the browser pane; WebFetch got an empty page)
- https://www.zooniverse.org/privacy
  - "This document describes what will happen to your contributions if you choose to contribute and what data we collect, how we use it and how we protect it."
  - not logged in: "Otherwise, they are solely associated with your IP address."
  - "We will not use your contact information for commercial purposes."
  - cookies "are not shared with other sites and are not used for advertisements."
  - "We will also never share data we collect about your use of the site unless (a) it cannot be associated with you or your username, and (b) it is necessary to accomplish our research goals."
  - "If you do not register, we will never contact you."
  - Removal: "Due to the way in which we archive data, it is generally not possible to completely remove your personal data from our systems. However, if you have specific concerns, please contact us and we will see what we can do."
  - Thanks: "You may optionally provide your real name to be included when we publically thank participants"
  - Who holds: "The Data Controller is the University of Oxford"
  - Note (against our aim): broad licence, "trusting us to do the right thing with your data"; "Acceptance of cookies is implied if you continue to access our website" (implied consent).

### 2e. University of Aberdeen, School of Psychology, "PEC Consent v7.00 - anonymous data" (a filled copy archived on UK Data Service ReShare; read 2026-10-12, pdftotext of the fetched PDF)
- https://reshare.ukdataservice.ac.uk/854753/6/DoubleRegistrationCueOnlyExpt_Consent.pdf
  - "After my testing session is complete it will not be possible to remove my data since it will be stored anonymously."
  - "I understand that the information provided by me will be held anonymously so that it is impossible to trace this information back to me individually."
  - "the anonymous dataset maybe archived in an online repository (e.g., the Open Science Framework, https://osf.io/) or sent to other researchers upon request for inspection." (sic "maybe")
  - shows the reader what publication looks like: (E.g. "Forty students from the University of Aberdeen took part. Gender was self-identified: 20 female, 18 male, two non-binary").
  - (The template URLs on abdn.ac.uk returned 404 on 2026-10-12.)

### 2d. University of Edinburgh, Royal (Dick) School of Veterinary Studies, "Example - Consent Form/Statement" (file dated 2024-09 in its path; read 2026-10-12, text extracted from the fetched .docx)
- https://vet.ed.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2024-09/Example%20Consent%20Form.docx
  - "as the survey is anonymous, once the 'submit' button is clicked, it will not be possible to withdraw"
  - "Your responses will be confidential. We do not collect identifying information such as name, your email, or your IP address."
  - "Your participation is voluntary. You may choose not to participate and you may withdraw at any time. If you decide not to participate or to withdraw from the study, you will not be penalised in any way, and your responses will not be used."

### 2e (cont.). The Open University (UK), HREC "Questionnaire consent for student surveys (template)" (read 2026-10-12, pdftotext of the fetched PDF)
- https://research.open.ac.uk/sites/research.open.ac.uk/files/files/Documents/srpp-questionnaire-consent-form%20template.pdf
  - "I am aware that I can withdraw from the study by closing my browser."
  - "I am aware that I am unable to withdraw from this study once submitted as my responses are collected anonymously."
  - "Project findings may be published in various forms, including project reports, academic journal articles, conference papers and blog posts."
  - Two answer options, the second being "I confirm that I am not willing/able to take part in the questionnaire."

### 2c. UCL Research Ethics, "Guidance, training and resources" (read 2026-10-12 in the browser pane; WebFetch 403; the old ethics.grad.ucl.ac.uk PDF fails "certificate has expired")
- https://www.ucl.ac.uk/research-innovation/funding-and-support/research-ethics/guidance-training-and-resources
  - "Participant information sheets should be accurate, clear, and written in plain English. Aim for a reading age of around eight, using short words, sentences, and paragraphs. Avoid technical terms, jargon, abbreviations, bias, or coercion."
  - should include "a friendly invitation to participate"
  - "a statement that participation is voluntary, that refusal will involve no penalty, and that the participant can withdraw at any time without penalty"
  - "a statement describing the extent to which confidentiality of records will be maintained"
  - "a thank you to the participant for considering taking part"
  - "Ask someone else to review your information sheet before it is circulated. See the Plain English Campaign website for guidance on accessible writing."
  - The templates themselves (PIS template, consent template, "Guidance on obtaining consent from research participants online") need a UCL login: not read.

### 2e (cont.). Smith College IRB (USA), "Condensed Informed Consent Language for 'Click Consent' For use with low-risk, anonymous online surveys", "Updated November 2020" (read 2026-10-12, pdftotext of the fetched PDF)
- https://new.libraries.smith.edu/sites/default/files/media/Documents/Institutional-Review-Board/Click_Consent_Template_0.pdf
  - "Your responses will be strictly anonymous (if it is not anonymous, replace 'anonymous' with 'confidential' and explain how personal information will be kept confidential)."
  - "Your participation in this study is anonymous, therefore it will not be possible to withdraw your survey answers from the study after you have submitted the survey."
  - "The decision to participate in this study is entirely up to you."
  - "If you like, a summary of the results of the study will be sent to you."
  - "By clicking below to be taken to the survey, you indicate that you have read and understood the above and volunteer to participate in this study."
  - Note: the template itself draws the line anonymous vs confidential (say "confidential" when it is not truly anonymous).

## 6. Wording rules that recur (each with its sources above)
R1. Say plainly what is NOT collected, then at once what IS collected (and what the server sees for a short time). LabintheWild ("We will not collect your name ... However, we will collect ..."), Sea Hero Quest, Edinburgh vet ("We do not collect ... your IP address"), YourMorals (IP explained), Shattered PD (table "What Data is Collected | How it is Used"), WebAsk ([Russian, at the cited source]), ICO top layer ("who you are, what information you are collecting and why").
R2. "Anonymous" only when no one, the team included, can link it to a person; otherwise "confidential"; never call a hash anonymous. Smith IRB (replace 'anonymous' with 'confidential'), TCD ("not be known by anyone, including the researchers"), FTC hashing post, FTC Avast, Crisis Text Line case, AEPD ("false sense of anonymity"), Loughborough (codes make data "no longer anonymous").
R3. Give the reason and the point of no return in one sentence: leave any time by closing/quitting; after submit it cannot be removed because it cannot be found. Loughborough, TCD, Smith, Open University, Edinburgh vet, Aberdeen, Gorilla, Sea Hero Quest ("we will not be able to identify which data is yours"), AHRECS, BPS IMR (final confirm button; say what happens to partial data), Pavlovia post (warn that quitting does not delete).
R4. Concrete, definite words, active voice, no "may/might/some/often/possible", no legal jargon, short sentences, reading age 8-12. WP260 paras 12-13 (incl. poor example "We may use your personal data for research purposes"), GDPR Art 12(1), EDPB 05/2020 para 67, ICO drafting page, UCL (reading age ~8), TCD (age 12, 15-20 words a sentence), AEPD (vague wordings criticised).
R5. Short first layer at the moment of choice, the full page one link away, layers never contradicting; the surprising thing goes in the first layer. WP260 paras 35-36 and 18 (just-in-time), ICO methods page, AEPD 5.2.1 (onboarding, "ongoing dialogue"), Shattered PD ("Let's get right to the important part ... Feel free to read on"), Steed 2016 (voiceover says the answer is not recorded).
R6. State who holds the data, who pays, and that it is not sold or used for ads, in values language; thank people; make "no" a real option. YourMorals ("non-profit academic venture, with no fees or advertising"), Lichess ("does none of these things and never will"), Zooniverse (not commercial, cookies "not used for advertisements"; Data Controller named), TestMyBrain ("will never sell"), TCD ("It is self-funded."; "Thank you for taking the time ..."), UCL ("a friendly invitation", "a thank you"), LabintheWild ("Thank you for your participation!"), ICO ("Align your privacy information with your organisation's values"), Open University (an explicit "not willing" option), Steed 2016 (non-consenters play, nothing recorded); against: PNAS on Facebook (consent buried in a Data Use Policy), Crisis Text Line ("By texting further with us, you agree to our Terms").
