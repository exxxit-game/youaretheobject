### Part 3: keeping purchases apart from research data (library texts, read 12.10)
- EDPB Guidelines 01/2025 on Pseudonymisation (adopted 16 Jan 2025, public-consultation version; library objekt-files/papers/edpb-2025-pseudonymisation.txt; no source URL in papers.json):
  para 5: controllers "need to keep additional information for attributing the personal data to a specific data subject separately, i.e. separate from those who are to be prevented from achieving such an attribution";
  para 22: pseudonymised data stays personal "if pseudonymised data and additional information could be combined having regard to the means reasonably likely to be used by the controller or by another person";
  para 33: linkage "not planned at the outset ... should likewise be performed only by persons specifically authorised";
  para 117: "a data subject may be assigned different pseudonyms depending on whether the data concern their relationship with controllers as employees or customers" (relationship pseudonyms); para 116: person pseudonyms (one for everything) "may not significantly reduce the severity of risks".
- AEPD (Spain) + Belgian DPA, "Recommendations and best practices for data protection in video games", June 2026, https://www.aepd.es/en/guides/videogames-recommendations-industry.pdf (library aepd-2026-videogames.txt):
  p.13: "A pseudoidentifier cannot be attributed to a specific person without additional information kept separately."; Annex 4, Checklist for Publishers, p.93, Release/Security: "Clearly separate database systems (or at least schemas and access controls) for core game/service data (accounts, progression, payments, matchmaking, etc.), and marketing/CRM/analytics data"; legal basis example: Art. 6(1)(c) "for accounting and regulatory requirements associated with purchases".
- BPS, "Ethics guidelines for internet-mediated research", June 2021 (library bps-2021-imr.txt): "Where identifying information such as an email address is required; for example, ... send a participation reward voucher, then researchers should strive to store these details separately from the actual study responses."; "even an IP address stored alongside online survey responses may be linked to an identifiable individual".
- UK Data Service, "Introduction to anonymisation techniques for social sciences research data", 22 May 2025, https://ukdataservice.ac.uk/app/uploads/introanonymisation2025-05-22.pdf (library ukds-2025.txt): "where only de-identification is applied data remains identifiable, hence data protection legislation applies to any additional processing of the data."

### Part 4b: a data leak (GDPR Art. 33/34; EDPB 9/2022)
- Art. 33(1), text via https://gdpr-info.eu/art-33-gdpr/ (reproduction; official https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj): the controller notifies the supervisory authority "without undue delay and, where feasible, not later than 72 hours after having become aware of it", unless the breach "is unlikely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons"; 33(2) processor tells the controller "without undue delay"; 33(5) "The controller shall document any personal data breaches".
- Art. 34(1), https://gdpr-info.eu/art-34-gdpr/ : when "likely to result in a high risk", tell the data subject "without undue delay"; 34(3)(a) not needed where measures "render the personal data unintelligible to any person who is not authorised ... such as encryption".
- EDPB Guidelines 9/2022 v2.0 (adopted 28 March 2023), https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2023-04/edpb_guidelines_202209_personal_data_breach_notification_v2.0_en.pdf (saved to library as edpb-2023-breach.txt): para 31 "aware" = "a reasonable degree of certainty that a security incident has occurred that has led to personal data being compromised"; para 73 (non-EU controller): "the mere presence of a representative in a Member State does not trigger the one-stop-shop system. For this reason the breach will need to be notified to every supervisory authority for which affected data subjects reside".
- Meta (if on its Store): Data Use Policy 6.4, notify Meta "using this form as soon as practicable" (Part 1).

### S3 Our own site sells packs through a merchant of record (Quest Browser only; the Store app must not unlock them: store-fees-own-sales.md)
- Card: the MoR's hosted checkout; prefer a redirect (PCI SSC FAQ 1588: the script criterion does not apply to redirects); Paddle: "You don't carry chargeback or PCI scope".
- Buyer identity: held by the MoR (Paddle entities "act as Controllers"). Webhooks send us name, email (customer.created), card brand, last 4, expiry, cardholder name (transaction.completed). [inference] Store none of it: keep only transaction id, SKU, hash of the unlock code; drop the rest in the function.
- Unlock without accounts, two routes:
  A) Lemon Squeezy licence keys: generated and emailed by LS; the game validates with the License API (no API key in the example); nothing on our server; the response carries the buyer's own name and email to his own headset. Risks: LS's future under Stripe unverified; payout by PayPal.
  B) Paddle + one Supabase Edge Function: on the signed webhook, issue a signed code (Keygen method), checked offline by the game with a public key. Needs the Paddle webhook secret and a signing key as Edge Function secrets (dashboard, never in Git).
- Separation [inference from EDPB para 5, 117; AEPD Annex 4 "Clearly separate database systems (or at least schemas and access controls)"; BPS IMR]: purchases in a separate Supabase project (separate logs, keys, grants; about $10/month), never the research random key in checkout `custom_data`, never the unlock code in a research request; no table joins the two.
- Owner: MoR account (2FA by hand: Paddle does not enforce it), paste the webhook secret into Supabase's Edge Function Secrets page once.
- Machine: signature check tested in the provider's sandbox; grant probe on the purchases project; test that research requests carry no purchase fields.
