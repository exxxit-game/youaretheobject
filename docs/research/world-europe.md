# Continental Europe, Israel, Latin America: experiments outside the lab, ethics, reach (research pass 2026-10-10)

Scope: what the English-language (US/UK), DGPs and Russia passes did not cover. Already covered elsewhere, not
repeated: Mottelson (Copenhagen), Slater EventLab (Barcelona), Moral Machine (Toulouse co-author), choice
blindness (Lund), Rivu 2021 (Munich), Science XL (Dufau 2011), DGPs ethics committee, Sea Hero Quest.
Every claim has its link and a short quote from the page read; "(our translation)" marks translated quotes;
"unverified" marks what was seen only in a search listing.

## Parts (each gets an answer or "not found")
1. Labs and projects running psychology experiments outside the lab at scale, by country:
   1a France; 1b Germany beyond DGPs; 1c Netherlands and Belgium; 1d Spain and Italy; 1e Scandinavia;
   1f Switzerland; 1g Poland and Czechia; 1h Israel; 1i Latin America (Brazil, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Colombia).
   For each: who, recruitment, pay or reward, consent and debrief, data quality rules, ethics review, results.
2. VR psychology studies outside the lab in these regions; data quality versus the lab.
3. Humour, narrator or host character, game elements inside a measured task; home participants less serious.
4. Ethics approval for a team without a university, by country (independent committees, national bodies,
   fees, time); European networks (EUREC); GDPR practice for motion data in research.
5. Science games or apps that reached many participants in these regions, and what got them there.
6. What these regions add that the English-language sources did not.

## Findings

### 1e/3/5. Denmark: Skill Lab: Science Detective (ScienceAtHome, Aarhus University) - full text read
- Pedersen, Castano Diaz, Wang, ... Hertwig, ... Sherson, "Measuring Cognitive Abilities in the Wild: Validating a
  Population-scale Game-Based Cognitive Assessment", arXiv:2009.05274 (published Cognitive Science 2023 per the
  project page in search: unverified). https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.05274 ; text saved objekt-papers/pedersen-2023.txt
- What: six casual mini-games with "a detective narrative theme intended to motivate and guide the participant
  between the games", plus 14 standard tasks; games measure by "stealth assessment" and are "not a one-to-one
  redesign of any particular standard cognitive task" (e.g. Rat Catch as a Go/No-Go analogue).
- Recruitment: "launched publicly in Denmark in collaboration with the Public Danish Broadcast Company (Danmarks
  Radio, DR) on the 4th of September 2018"; "a series of DR news articles with themes varying from AI and
  technology to psychology and computer games"; an event "at 208 high schools"; "more than 16,000 people signed up".
  The thanks section: DR's collaboration "without which the recruitment to the study would not have been" possible.
- Pay: none. Players did "the rather tedious validation tasks without any form of extrinsic reward"; "there was no
  nudging towards the tasks within Skill Lab and no requirement to do so".
- Ethics and consent: "The Committee of Research Ethics for Region Midtjylland (Denmark) exempted the study from
  ethical oversight, and the project received ethical approval from the Institutional Review Board at Cornell
  University"; "players provided informed consent before taking part"; they "could, at any time, leave the study
  and request their data to be anonymized". Minimum age 16, "the minimum age for granting informed consent
  according to the EU's General Data Protection Regulations" (the paper's reading).
- Data quality: models predicted 8 abilities; players beyond "3 SD's from the mean" excluded from fitting; task
  reliability Cronbach alpha 0.70-0.96 except visual processing 0.25 (Table 2). Limitation: played once only, so
  "a follow-up test-retest study is needed".
- Time: games "14 minutes (SD = 5 min), in comparison with 72 minutes" for the tasks: "one-fifth of the time".
- Sample: mobile players "3181 female, 3296 male, and 47 other"; validation subset 64% female vs 49% overall.
- Lesson for us: a national broadcaster plus a story frame (detective) carried 16,000 unpaid players through
  72 minutes of plain tasks; Danish regional ethics exempted it, a US IRB approved it.

### 1a/3/5. France: a 2026 review from CNRS Lyon, Institut Jean Nicod and the Sea Hero Quest studio - full text read
- Salouhou, Dubourg, Scott-Slade (Glitchers), Spiers (UCL), Coutrot (CNRS, LIRIS Lyon), "From Players to
  Participants: Citizen Science and Video Games to Understand Cognition", arXiv:2604.24321 (2026).
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.24321 ; text saved objekt-papers/salouhou-2026.txt
- Cost argument (their arithmetic): Sea Hero Quest in a lab "would have taken 500 years for a full-time PhD
  student (15 min/participant, 2000 hours per year)" and about "25 million euros in staff salary, 40 million euros
  in participants compensation".
- Game cost: custom serious games are estimated at "50,000 to 300,000" (euro sign lost in the text; cites a
  GameMaker blog). Why researchers fail with studios: developers favour "fluidity, visual appeal, or user
  enjoyment, whereas researchers need tight control over variables"; the problem is that "the game was not built
  for science".
- Data quality warning (part 3): in the cognitive game Tunnel Runner "infrequent gamers showed significantly lower
  precision and validity in more complex cognitive tasks, such as response-rule switching and inhibition"
  (Markovitch, Kamps, Markopoulos & Birk 2025, Computers in Human Behavior 172:108720; not read: unverified);
  remedy suggested: "preparatory mini-games, clearer instructions".
- Ethics: concerns "especially when corporate entities are involved"; children's consent "more complicated".
- Long tail: "only a fraction of participants sustaining activity beyond the first month".
- Table 1 lists European projects with reach and channel: Skill Lab (Denmark, "TV collaboration, school
  outreach"), CitieS-Health Barcelona (cognitive mini-games and NO2, "EU Horizon 2020", "Local campaigns, health
  orgs, social media"), Hexxed.io (Champalimaud, Lisbon, "no instructions, find the patterns", 10,000), Neureka
  (Dublin, 9,918, 54 countries).
- New Belgian lead it cites: Van den Bussche, Verhaegen, Reynvoet & Hughes (2026), "Citizen science in psychology:
  Challenges, opportunities, and recommendations", Behavior Research Methods 58(5):139 (see 1c).
- Conflict of interest stated: Glitchers "explores commercial opportunities for Sea Hero Quest as a screening
  tool for dementia".

### 4. France: which law covers a psychology study (Code de la sante publique) - page read
- Article L1121-1 (version in force since 31 July 2022), https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000046125746 :
  the regime (CPP committee opinion, the "Jarde" law) covers research on people "en vue du developpement des
  connaissances biologiques ou medicales" ("aimed at developing biological or medical knowledge", our
  translation), in three categories; category 3 is "non-interventional research with no risk or burden" (our
  translation). Reading (ours, from the definition): a psychology study whose aim is not biological or medical
  knowledge is outside this regime; such studies go to university research ethics committees (CER). A CER's
  legal status and whether any CER takes a private team: not confirmed on a primary page (unverified).

### 4. Netherlands: no legal duty of review outside medical research; faculty committees under one national code
- Government-commissioned survey: Boeckhout, Beusink, Bouter et al., "Niet-WMO-plichtig onderzoek en ethische
  toetsing", Verkenning in opdracht van het Ministerie van VWS, 14 February 2020.
  https://elsi.health-ri.nl/sites/elsi/files/2020-07/Verkenning%20nWMO-onderzoek%20DEF.pdf (text read via pdftotext)
  - Law: running WMO (medical) research without an approved METC "is bijvoorbeeld strafbaar" ("is for example a
    criminal offence", our translation); for other research review "is weliswaar niet wettelijk verplicht, maar
    ligt wel voor de hand" ("is not legally required, but is the obvious thing to do", our translation).
  - DPIA: for high-risk data processing a data protection impact assessment "voorafgaand aan de verwerking
    verplicht" ("mandatory before the processing", our translation), likened to "an ethical (self-)review".
  - Psychology: review is mandatory for researchers of social and behavioural faculties, whose committees use one
    national "Code of Ethics for Research in the Social and Behavioural Sciences Involving Human Participants"
    and are joined in Nethics. Outside organisations ask in: "Nethics ontvangt ook verzoeken van
    maatschappelijke organisaties buiten de universiteit (zoals het RIVM of GGZ-instellingen)" ("Nethics also
    receives requests from civil-society organisations outside the university, such as RIVM or mental-health
    institutions", our translation) - an ask, no answer given. About "2 procent" of studies get sent on to a METC.
  - What committees check in psychology: "misleiding" and "debriefing" ("deception" and "debriefing", explaining
    the goal only afterwards), "methods that are of particular importance for psychological research" (our
    translation).
- A paid route that takes companies (medical committee, non-WMO opinion): METC LDD tariffs "geldig per 1 januari
  2023": non-WMO review for "Profit organisaties" 500 euros, ex-VAT. https://www.metc-ldd.nl/over-ons/beoordelingstarieven
  (Whether a METC would review a non-health psychology game: not stated; turnaround not on the page.)

### 4. Sweden: national ethics authority, open to companies, review required when a method aims to affect the mind
- Ethical Review Act SFS 2003:460 (lagen.nu, as amended through SFS 2026:1229; some sections change on
  2027-07-01). https://lagen.nu/2003:460
  - Who can apply: "forskningshuvudman" is "en statlig myndighet eller en fysisk eller juridisk person i vars
    verksamhet forskningen utfors" ("a state agency or a natural or legal person in whose operations the research
    is carried out", our translation) - so a company can be the applicant.
  - When review is required (sec. 3-4): sensitive data under GDPR art. 9.1; or research that "utfors enligt en
    metod som syftar till att paverka forskningspersonen fysiskt eller psykiskt" ("uses a method intended to
    affect the research person physically or psychologically", our translation), or with "an obvious risk" of harm.
  - Consent: research only if the person "har samtyckt"; ages 15-17 who understand the research consent for
    themselves (sec. 18). Penalty for intentional breach: fines or up to two years' prison (sec. 38).
- Fees, Ordinance SFS 2003:615 (consolidated to SFS 2023:64 on lagen.nu). https://lagen.nu/2003:615
  - One research principal: 5,000 SEK; more than one: 16,000 SEK; personal-data-only research: 5,000 SEK;
    change to an approval: 2,000 SEK. Decision "within 60 days" (35 days for changes).
  - A government document in search gives an average handling time of 36 days in 2024 (not opened: unverified).
- Reading (ours): a home experiment that manipulates what a player sees to affect him likely falls under sec. 4
  when the research is done in Sweden; whether it reaches a foreign team with Swedish players: not found.

### 4. Switzerland: the Human Research Act stops at health; psychology of the normal mind is outside it
- University of Basel, Faculty of Psychology, "Guidelines and template for review" (undated PDF), for its
  Department of Psychology's Ethics Committee, based on the Swiss Psychological Society's Ethical Guidelines and
  the Human Research Act (HRA). https://psychologie.unibas.ch/fileadmin/user_upload/psychologie/Forschung/Guidelines_and_template_for_review_.pdf
  - Scope: basic research on the psyche falls under the HRA only if it "generates findings on the causes and
    development of (usually psychological) diseases"; research on "the normal structure, function, and development
    of the human psyche" is "not included in its scope". Health data (ECG, fMRI, EEG) pulls a study in.
  - Consent may be waived when "The experiment design requires deception and retrospective agreement is obtained
    in the context of debriefing" (D17 ER SPS). Deception allowed only if "the purpose of the research allows no
    alternative procedure without deception"; it must be explained "no later than at the end of the data-collection
    phase", with the right to withdraw data (D22).
- Whether a Swiss committee reviews outside companies, fees: not found.

### 4. Brazil: one national system (CEP/CONEP) that covers psychology too, with its own rules for human sciences
- CNS Resolution 510 of 7 April 2016 (text on the Ministry of Health's library).
  https://bvsms.saude.gov.br/bvs/saudelegis/cns/2016/res0510_07_04_2016.html
  - Scope: "normas aplicaveis a pesquisas em Ciencias Humanas e Sociais" that use data from participants,
    identifiable information or risks above everyday life. Not reviewed: "pesquisa de opiniao publica com
    participantes nao identificados" and research on public information.
  - Consent can be "expressao oral, escrita, lingua de sinais ou de outras formas"; its record is "nao ...
    necessariamente escrito" ("not necessarily written", our translation); "midia eletronica e digital" listed.
  - Covert research (art. 2 XV): "pesquisa conduzida sem que os participantes sejam informados sobre objetivos e
    procedimentos do estudo" ("research run without participants being told the study's goals and procedures",
    our translation), allowed when telling would change the behaviour studied; consent afterwards when feasible.
  - Participants are guaranteed "acesso aos resultados da pesquisa" (art. 17 VI). Risk graded "minimo, baixo,
    moderado ou elevado" (art. 21) with handling by level. Projects go through Plataforma Brasil (art. 23).
  - Who may submit: the responsible researcher needs at least a bachelor's or equivalent degree (art. 2 XVII);
    how a company without its own CEP gets one assigned: not found on the page. A newer law (Lei 14.874 of
    28 May 2024 on research with humans) is cited by a university CEP page in search (not read: unverified).

### 4. Europe-wide: EUREC position on review outside biomedical research (15 March 2021) - full text read
- "Position of the European Network of Research Ethics Committees (EUREC) on Ethics Reviews of Research Projects
  involving Persons outside Biomedical Research", to the European Commission, the Council of Europe and research
  institutions. https://lumsa.it/sites/default/files/ricerca/EUREC%20Positionpaper%20on%20ethics%20reviews%20outside%20biomedical%20research_March-2021.pdf
  - The gap, in their words: researchers outside medicine "are in some cases not bound by specific provisions";
    "Often an ethics review for such studies is not required either by national laws or by professional laws or
    guidelines. However, funding organisations and peer-reviewed journals are requesting ethics review"; "In some
    jurisdictions it is difficult for these researchers to find an ethics committee that will review their projects."
  - The bridge: "In some European countries medical RECs have taken on the additional task of reviewing projects
    outside the field of biomedicine"; new non-medical committees could start as "chambers" or "panels" within
    existing REC structures.
  - Who EUREC is: "the European representation of national networks of medical research ethics committees";
    seat Bonn. Its site has a "RECs in Europe" section with country profiles (search listing: 21 EU and 3 non-EU
    countries; not opened: unverified).
- Lesson for us: the journals' demand for review is the binding force, not the law; a medical committee that
  also reviews non-medical work (as METC LDD in the Netherlands sells non-WMO opinions to companies) is the
  practical door for a team without a university.

### 4. GDPR and motion data: two European regulators' readings
- Belgium, Data Protection Authority, "Recommandation relative au traitement de donnees biometriques", version
  1.1, 01/12/2021 (text read via pdftotext). https://www.dataprotectionauthority.be/publications/recommandation-01-2021-du-1-decembre-2021.pdf
  - The strict reading: "Lorsque les donnees ne sont pas traitees en vue de l'identification unique de personnes
    mais que cela est toutefois possible, compte tenu de la nature des donnees, il s'agira donc egalement d'un
    traitement de donnees biometriques au sens du RGPD" ("When data are not processed to identify people uniquely
    but this is nonetheless possible given the nature of the data, it is still processing of biometric data under
    the GDPR", our translation).
  - Behaviour counts: "Chaque caracteristique comportementale permettant l'identification unique d'une personne
    est logiquement censee etre une donnee biometrique" ("every behavioural characteristic that allows unique
    identification of a person is logically deemed biometric data", our translation); examples given: keyboard,
    touch-screen and mouse use, and "la demarche unique de personnes (en anglais gait recognition)".
  - The way out it names: biometric data in the scientific sense "sans possibilite d'identifier ou de
    reidentifier les individus ne releve donc pas du champ d'application du RGPD" ("without any possibility of
    identifying or re-identifying individuals is outside the GDPR", our translation).
  - Biometric data are an art. 9.1 special category: processing "en principe interdit" unless an art. 9.2
    exception applies (explicit consent is one; the GDPR text is in ethics-law.md 4a).
  - Reading (ours): with the head-and-hand identification results already in ethics-law.md 4b, a Belgian-style
    regulator would treat raw Quest motion traces as biometric (art. 9) data even if we never try to identify
    anyone; storing only derived measures that cannot re-identify keeps them out.
- France, CNIL innovation lab (LINC): Chatellier, "Metavers : realites virtuelles ou collectes augmentees ?",
  4 November 2021. https://linc.cnil.fr/metavers-realites-virtuelles-ou-collectes-augmentees
  - Cites Ubisoft's Fun II project seeking "une signature physiologique du plaisir" from heart rate, gaze, facial
    expression "ou meme sur l'analyse des mouvements"; headsets "potentiellement dotes de capteurs" could enable
    such uses; interfaces become "de nouveaux moyens de collecter des donnees, toujours plus sensibles".
  - No rule on motion data; "transparence, information et dans certains cas du consentement" remain the issues.

### 1i/3. Argentina: TEDxperiments - a host on stage runs the experiment on thousands (local full text)
- Navajas, Niella, Garbulsky, Bahrami & Sigman (2018), Nature Human Behaviour 2:126-132 (text already in
  objekt-papers/navajas-2018.txt; card docs/cards/navajas-2018.md). Read here for method, not result.
  - Setting: "a TEDx event in Buenos Aires, Argentina ... on September 24, 2015. This was the third edition of an
    initiative called TEDxperiments ..., aimed at constructing knowledge on human communication by performing
    behavioural experiments on large TEDx audiences" (earlier editions: cost of interruptions; a "zero-sum
    fallacy" game).
  - The host: "The event's speaker (author M.S.) conducted the crowd from the stage"; 20 seconds per answer; pen
    and a folded A4 sheet "linked to their seat number".
  - Consent: participants were told participation was "completely voluntary and they could simply choose not to
    participate or withdraw"; "All data were completely anonymous". N = 5180.
  - Ethics: "approved by the ethics committee of CEMIC (Centro de Educacion Medica e Investigaciones Clinicas
    Norberto Quirno)" - a private medical research centre's committee, not the university's.
  - Quality: valid data from all five members needed: 280 groups (1400 people) of 5180 entered the key analysis;
    a lab replication with 100 students ran alongside.
- Lesson for us (part 3): the scientist himself as a live host, framing the task as a show, did not stop a Nature
  Human Behaviour result; the measured part (20-second answers) stayed plain and timed.

### 2. Spain: Slater's EventLab did run VR studies at home (2020-2021) - full text read (open access)
- Slater, Cabriera, ... Gallego (2023), "The sentiment of a virtual rock concert", Virtual Reality (Springer),
  open access, published 05 June 2023 (online). https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10055-022-00685-9 ;
  text saved objekt-papers/slater-2023.txt. (Corrects field-labs-2.md entry 6, which found no EventLab home study.)
  - Setting: "carried out during the COVID-19 pandemic"; a Qualtrics link held "the information sheet, ethics consent
    form, instructions including installation of the software ... instructions on how to run the experience in the
    head-mounted display, and follow-up questions. Hence, participants could participate in their own time and
    place." "All responses were anonymous and IP addresses were not transmitted."
  - Ethics: "approved by the Bioethics Committee of the University of Barcelona, IRB00003099"; written consent.
  - Pay: "Participants were not paid for their participation." Recruitment: social-media adverts plus students in
    US, Australian and New Zealand classes; Study 1 used Quest 1 headsets "supplied by their University".
  - The drop-off that matters for us: "65 individuals started the Qualtrics survey and 26 completed it. All of those
    who did not complete it stopped at the point that they were asked to download the application and upload it
    to their head-mounted display." (60% lost at sideloading.)
  - Method lesson: open written essays plus sentiment analysis found what Likert questionnaires missed: "a
    standard VR experimental design, with a questionnaire asked after the experience with Likert scale questions,
    would never have picked up on these deeper findings" (the virtual audience, not the band, drove the feelings).
- Lesson for us: our browser-only WebXR entry removes exactly the step where EventLab lost 39 of 65 people.

### 2. Germany: TU Berlin remote VR study with shipped headsets and a Zoom instructor (2024) - full text read
- Warsinke, Kojic, Vergari, Spang, Voigt-Antons & Moller, "Comparing Continuous and Retrospective Emotion Ratings in
  Remote VR Study", QoMEX 2024, arXiv:2404.16487v2. https://arxiv.org/html/2404.16487v2
  - Quality and Usability Lab, Technische Universitat Berlin (plus Hochschule Hamm-Lippstadt).
  - "Participants received the VR-Setup with the pre-installed experiment application via parcel delivery"; a
    "scheduled appointment to meet on Zoom with the instructor", casting the Quest to their computer.
  - Ethics: "approved by the local ethics committee of the Technical University of Berlin"; consent signed digitally.
  - 21 took part, 1 excluded "Due to incomplete ratings"; "mostly male students"; limit named: "one cannot adjust,
    show, or help participants in person". No lab condition: home-vs-lab "has not yet been explored".
  - Finding: continuous in-VR ratings differed from retrospective ones for valence (moderate to strong effects),
    not for arousal - a reason to measure during the moment, not only after it.

### 2. Outside the regions, directly relevant: browser VR timing on players' own headsets (Australia, 2023)
- Loetscher, Jurkovic, Michalski, Billinghurst & Lee (2023), Multimodal Technologies and Interaction 7(3):32.
  https://www.mdpi.com/2414-4088/7/3/32 (read in the browser; WebFetch got 403). Prolific, own headsets, browser
  (PsychoPy/PsychoJS on Pavlovia): 68 VR vs 68 desktop after 14 exclusions ("phone-based VR headset" n = 5, "phone
  or tablet" n = 8, revoked consent n = 1). Inhibition of return "only partially replicated for the VR headset
  group"; VR reaction times slower (median 441 vs 374 ms) and more variable (individual SD median 80 vs 67 ms);
  headset type mattered (Quest 2 vs PlayStation VR). Advice: "limit participation to certain headset models" and
  record hardware automatically; "WebXR ... may become an interesting option".
