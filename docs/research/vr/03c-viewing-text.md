# Sub-area 03: comfortable viewing and text legibility in VR

Scope: head rotation and comfort zones, content distances, the vergence–accommodation conflict (VAC) and minimum distance, letter size in degrees, reading distance, target size.
Everything below was read in this session (2026-10-08) from the files listed under "Local files saved". Conversions: 1 dmm = 1 mm at 1 m = atan(0.001) = 0.0573°; 1° = 60′ (arcmin); D = diopter = 1/metres.
"Verified" = I read the primary source myself. "Full text read" = whole document read, not only the abstract or a snippet.

Letter-size terms differ between sources, so each one is labelled:
- **cap height**: height of a capital letter.
- **x-height**: height of a small letter such as "x".
- **font size / em**: the nominal type size (CSS `px`, Unity `pt`). Cap height and x-height are fractions of the em, and the fractions depend on the font.

Our code (`src/engine/ui/choice.js`) sets `letterMm = size / density`, where `size` is the canvas font size. So **our MIN_LETTER (24 mm at 1 m, 1.375°) is a font size (em), not a cap height**. To compare it with a cap-height norm, multiply by the cap-height fraction of the font we ship, read from the font file (`capHeight / unitsPerEm`). I did not read that file; I am not stating the ratio from memory.

## Head rotation and comfort zones

- **Head yaw: comfortable 30° from centre, maximum 55°.** Content zone 77° to each side (DK2 94° FOV/2 + 30°); peripheral zone to 102° (94/2 + 55); behind that is the "curiosity zone". — Alger, M., *Visual Design Methods for Virtual Reality*, MA thesis, Ravensbourne, Sept 2015, http://aperturesciencellc.com/vr/VisualDesignMethodsforVR_MikeAlger.pdf, pp. 37–38 (numbers taken from Chu 2014). — Alger verified yes, full text read yes; Chu's head-rotation slide **not verified** (see "Could not open"). — "comfortably rotate their heads horizontally 30° from the center" — For us: the sheet straight ahead in the player's yaw is well inside the 30° comfortable band.
- **Head pitch: up 20° comfortable / 60° max; down 12° comfortable / 40° max.** — Alger 2015, p. 45, again citing Chu 2014. — Alger verified yes, full text read yes; Chu primary unverified. — "Downwards is less with 12° comfortably and 40° maximum" — For us: Chu's 12° is how far the **head** pitches comfortably. It is **not** a rule for where to place content. Our DROP_DEG = 12 matches the number but not the meaning.
- **Reading area: 15°–50° below eye level, beyond 0.6 m.** Alger slices this from Ankrum (1999, *Visual Ergonomics in the Office*). Alger's VR OS mock-up opens a document "1.3 meters away between 15° and 50° downward". — Alger 2015, pp. 45 and 69. — Alger verified yes, full text read yes; Ankrum 1999 not read (secondary through Alger). — "recommended angle of viewing for longer working periods tends to be between 15°-50° downwards" — For us: desktop-derived. It places reading lower than our 12°.
- **Eyes move comfortably about ±30–35° horizontally and vertically; with the neck the field is about 120°; the comfortable visual centre is about 6° below the horizon. Street View put its UI 2.5 m away, about 6° below the horizon.** — McKenzie, C. & Glazier, A., *Designing Screen Interfaces for VR*, Google I/O 2017, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ES9jArHRFHQ (transcript from the human "English – CC" captions, 18:03–19:30 and 21:30). — verified yes, full text read yes (whole transcript). — "visual center ends up being comfortably around plus six degrees below the horizon" — For us: a VR-specific source puts the reading centre about 6° below the horizon, which is half our 12°.
- **Preferred vertical position of a text box: −1.0 ± 2.6° from horizontal** (n = 18, Oculus Rift CV1, participants set it themselves). Dingler also cites Dix (2009): the eye covers about 60° comfortably, horizontally and vertically, and neck rotation raises that to about 120°. — Dingler, T., Kunze, K. & Outram, B., *VR Reading UIs: Assessing Text Parameters for Reading in VR*, CHI 2018 Extended Abstracts, LBW094, doi:10.1145/3170427.3188695 (author copy https://kaikunze.de/papers/pdf/dingler2018vr.pdf), p. 4 Table 1 and p. 2. — verified yes, full text read yes. — "The preferred vertical position was -1.0 ± 2.6 degrees from the horizontal" — For us: when free to choose, readers put text almost at eye level.
- **Desktop office norm: screen centre 15°–25° below horizontal eye level (§5.2.4.3); 15°–20° below eye level (§7.2.2.2); best gaze angle "about −17° or slightly higher"; whole visual area between 0° and 60° below eye level; eyes do not rotate comfortably more than 30° downward relative to the head during sustained viewing.** — ANSI/HFES 100-2007, *Human Factors Engineering of Computer Workstations*, HFES 2007, §5.2.4.3 (pp. 22–23) and §7.2.2.2 (p. 58). PDF hosted by a vendor: https://www.xybix.com/hubfs/ANSI_HFES_100-200727E2.pdf. — verified yes; full text read **no** (only these clauses). — "the best gaze angle is about -17° or slightly higher" — For us: this is the seated desktop case with a fixed screen. Our 12° sits between this (15°–20°) and the VR sources (−1° to 6°).
- **Viewing "at a glance" without moving the head: a horizontal viewing angle of at most ±15°.** — ISO 9241-306:2018, §5.1.1 (official SIS preview, pp. 2–3). — verified yes; full text read no (preview clauses only). — "maximum horizontal viewing angle of ±15°" — For us: a sheet 0.56 m wide at 1 m spans about ±15.6°, roughly the "at a glance" width.
- **Meta: keep important content in the field of view; content at the edge of the FoV or to the sides can demand a large range of quick head and neck motion; put interactables at a comfortable height so users do not keep looking down.** Meta gives no angle numbers. — Meta Horizon design pages "Comfort" (last_updated 2025-12-17) and "Head" (2026-03-13), https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/comfort/ and …/design/head/. — verified yes (fetched live). — "positioned at a comfortable height to prevent users from constantly looking down" — For us: Meta agrees in direction but gives no number for "a little below".

## Content distances

- **Meta: objects the user fixates for a long time at least 0.5 m away; "many have found" 1 m comfortable for menus and GUIs read for long periods.** — Meta Horizon design "Display", section "Comfortable viewing distances" (last_updated 2026-03-11), https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/display/. — verified yes (live copy identical to the saved `meta_display.md`). — "Many have found that 1 meter is a comfortable distance for menus" — For us: supports READ_DIST = 1.0. It is a soft recommendation, not a measurement.
- **Meta: indirect (ray) interaction is comfortable from about 0.8 m to 3 m. Do not put ray UI closer than 0.8 m. Avoid the 0.5–0.8 m middle distance: bring UI under about 46 cm for touch, or push it to 1 m or more for rays. Touch UI goes at 42–46 cm. Keep angular size, not physical size, when UI moves in depth.** — Meta "Hands UI best practices" (2026-09-24) and "Indirect interaction best practices" (2026-09-09), https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/hands-ui-best-practices/ and …/indirect_interactions_bp/. — verified yes (live). — "Indirect interaction is comfortable from roughly 0.8m to 3m." — For us: 1 m is inside the ray range and clear of the middle zone that Meta says to avoid.
- **Meta: current headsets have a fixed focal distance; anything about 1 m away appears clear.** — Meta blog, "Demo or Die: How Reality Labs' Display Systems Research team…", 31 Jul 2023, https://www.meta.com/blog/reality-labs-research-display-systems-siggraph-2023-butterscotch-varifocal-flamera/. — verified yes (read the relevant sentences; not the full article). — "Anything that's about 1 meter or so away from your eyes appears clear" — For us: a Meta statement written in the Quest 3 era that supports 1 m.
- **Meta captions: start about 1 m away (half the far-field distance); size text-size settings from 10% of the FoV as baseline; offer 50/75/100/150/200%.** — Meta "Accessibility" (2026-04-06), https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/accessibility/. — verified yes (live). — "place captions at about half the distance of the far-field or 1 meter away" — For us: another Meta 1 m figure for reading.
- **Meta ray specs: the ray reaches 10 m; the visible beam is 0.25 m (hand) or 0.35 m (controller).** — Meta "Ray casting specs" (2026-05-06), https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/raycasting_specs/. — verified yes (live copy identical to `meta_raycasting_specs.md`). — "The visible laser extends 25 cm from the hand toward the target" — For us: no distance limit applies at 1 m.
- **Chu: below about 0.5–1 m, eyes strain and go cross-eyed. 0.75 m was Oculus's 2015 minimum. Strong stereo depth from there to 10 m, weaker to 20 m, almost none beyond 20 m. Alger's "no-no zone" is a 0.5 m radius around the head.** — Alger 2015, pp. 36–39 (Chu 2014; Oculus 2015 Best Practices). — Alger verified yes, full text read yes. Chu's depth chart is confirmed by his own slides 31 and 33, reproduced on Voices of VR #117 (https://voicesofvr.com/117-alex-chu-on-designing-milk-vr-for-the-samsung-gear-vr/): the bands are "Cross-eyed" from 0 to about 1 m, "Strong 3D" about 1–10 m, "Some 3D" 10–20 m and "Totally Flat" beyond 20 m (I read the edges off the chart). — "The distance that he gives where this starts to become noticeable is about 0.5 - 1 meters." — For us: 1 m sits at the near edge of Chu's "strong 3D" band, which is fine for a flat sheet.
- **Far limit for meaningful depth: about 20 m (DK2: 0.1° per pixel with a 63 mm IPD gives 20.34 m; IPD 52–78 mm gives 14.9–22.3 m).** — Alger 2015, pp. 41–44. — verified yes, full text read yes. — "subtracting 0.1° yields a perceived distance of 20.34 meters" — For us: not relevant to the sheet. This is the source of the "20 m" in Kojic's zones.
- **Long reading text belongs at the lens focal distance: 1.3 m for DK2, "likely 2.5 m for future devices".** — Alger 2015, p. 45, citing Answers.oculus.com (2014, not read). — verified yes (Alger); the Oculus answer page unverified. — "at a distance that matches the focal distance of the device's lenses" — For us: on Quest/Quest 2 (focal distance 1.3 m, below) the ideal is 1.3 m. At 1 m the mismatch is only 0.23 D (see the VAC section).
- **Reading in VR, n = 18, Rift CV1 (fixed focus about 1.3 m). Median "closest comfortable" distance 0.89 m (quartiles 0.61–1.5 m); median "most comfortable" 2.7 m (1.3–8.6 m); median "farthest comfortable" 9.8 m (2.6–64 m). 59% of "most comfortable" choices were beyond 3.45 m. Rough guideline: 1–10 m, ideally about 3 m.** — Dingler et al. 2018, p. 5 (Table 3) and p. 5 text. — verified yes, full text read yes. — "place text … at a distance of between 1 and 10 meters from the user, but ideally at around 3m" — For us: **caution.** Half of Dingler's readers put their *closest* comfortable distance at 0.89 m or farther, so 1 m is near the near end of their preferences. Meta and Google endorse 1 m. A headset test should ask whether 1 m feels close.
- **Google Daydream: every virtual screen has an intended viewing distance; the identity position is 1 m (40 dmm = 40 mm); a screen meant for 2 m is scaled ×2.** — McKenzie & Glazier 2017, 9:01–9:30. — verified yes, full text read yes. — "intended to be viewed from one meter away. I call this the identity position" — For us: our "letter mm at 1 m" convention is exactly Google's dmm.

## Vergence–accommodation conflict and minimum distance

- **The eye's depth of focus is about ±0.3 D (±0.25 to ±0.3 D); Panum's fusion area is 15–30 arcmin.** — Hoffman, D.M., Girshick, A.R., Akeley, K. & Banks, M.S. (2008), *Vergence–accommodation conflicts hinder visual performance and cause visual fatigue*, Journal of Vision 8(3):33, doi:10.1167/8.3.33 (PMC2879326 author manuscript), sections "Focus cues, fusion, and stereopsis" and "General methods". — verified yes, full text read yes. — "depth of focus, which is roughly ±0.3 diopters (D) under normal circumstances" — For us: a conflict under about 0.3 D lies inside the eye's own focus tolerance.
- **A conflict of ±0.67 D, kept for about 45 min, caused significantly more eye tiredness, blur, eye discomfort and headache than the same task without conflict (11 observers aged 23–31; Wilcoxon p < 0.025). In a pilot, 5 of 11 young adults could not fuse, or found it too tiring to fuse, stimuli with conflicts.** — Hoffman et al. 2008, Experiment 4 and Discussion. — verified yes, full text read yes. — "the first demonstration that mismatches … cause visual fatigue and discomfort" — For us: keep text that is read for long within a small fraction of a diopter of the lens focus.
- **Practical guideline: focus cues have little influence beyond 1 m (Watt et al. 2005). Minimise the conflict by matching the simulated distance to the focal distance.** — Hoffman et al. 2008, "Practical guidelines". — verified yes, full text read yes. — "there should be little influence beyond 1 m" — For us: 1 m is the threshold distance in this rule.
- **Zone of comfort (young adults, 19–34): far boundary Df = 1.129·Dv + 0.442; near boundary Df = 1.035·Dv − 0.626 (Df = focal distance, Dv = vergence distance, both in D). Example ranges from the paper: cinema 1.6 m to infinity; mobile phone 0.28–0.44 m.** — Shibata, T., Kim, J., Hoffman, D.M. & Banks, M.S. (2011), *The zone of comfort: Predicting visual discomfort with stereo displays*, Journal of Vision 11(8):11, doi:10.1167/11.8.11 (PMC3369815), section "Summary of results on comfort zone" (Figure 17) and "Vergence and viewing distance in meters". — verified yes, full text read yes (Table 3 correlation values skimmed). — "In cinema, the range extends from 1.6 m to infinity." — For us, solving the two lines (my arithmetic, cross-checked with Dingler 2018 p. 5, which gets 0.74 and 3.45 m for 1.3 m):

  | Headset focal distance | Near comfort limit | Far comfort limit | Conflict at our 1 m |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1.3 m (Quest, Quest 2) | 0.74 m | 3.45 m | 0.23 D |
  | 1.5 m (if Quest 3 were 1.5 m) | 0.80 m | 5.0 m | 0.33 D |
  | 2.0 m (older headsets, per Carmack) | 0.92 m | 19.5 m | 0.50 D |

  1 m is inside the zone in every row. At 1.3 m focus the conflict at 1 m (0.23 D) is smaller than the eye's depth of focus (±0.3 D, Hoffman 2008). At 0.5 m the conflict would be 1.23 D, about the 1.2 D that caused discomfort in Shibata's Experiment 1.
- **Shibata also found: for the same dioptric conflict, near viewing is less comfortable overall; content in front of the screen is worse at near distances; content behind the screen is worse at far distances. Discomfort built up steadily over 20-minute sessions.** — Shibata et al. 2011, Experiments 1–2 and Figure 16. — verified yes, full text read yes. — "negative conflicts (stereo content behind the screen) are less comfortable at far distances" — For us: the sheet at 1 m is nearer than a 1.3 m focal plane (a small positive conflict), the milder sign at mid distance.
- **Quest / Quest 2 design focal distance: 1.3 m; some older headsets were 2.0 m.** — John Carmack (then consulting CTO, Oculus/Meta), post on X, 15 Mar 2021, https://x.com/ID_AA_Carmack/status/1371485209603022853 (text confirmed through the X syndication API). — verified yes (a primary statement by a Meta engineer; not Meta documentation). — "The design focal distance for the Quest/Quest 2 optics is 1.3 meters." — For us: use 1.3 m for Quest 2. **Quest 3: unverified** (below).
- **Rift CV1 focal distance about 1.3 m ("according to the Oculus developer documentation").** — Dingler et al. 2018, p. 3. — verified yes (Dingler); the Oculus page it cites was not read. — "optics equivalent to looking at a screen approximately 1.3m away" — For us: the same 1.3 m figure from a second, independent route.
- **Quest 3 focal distance: unverified.** I found no Meta document and no measured review. A Meta community forum thread asks for an official figure and gets none; retail and accessory blogs say "1.3–1.5 m" without a method. — For us: until a measurement exists, treat Quest 3 as 1.3 m (same family). The 1.5 m row of the table shows that 1 m still passes if it is 1.5 m.
- **Meta on VAC: accommodative demand is fixed while vergence demand changes; objects outside the comfortable range are fine if users do not have to focus on them for long; depth-of-field blur can help.** — Meta "Display". — verified yes. — "accommodative demand is fixed but vergence demand can change" — For us: only the sheet needs the 0.5 m+ rule; the corridor scenery does not.

## Text size and reading distance

- **Google Daydream (2017): comfortably readable body text was 1.375° "with today's current headsets" (82 arcmin, i.e. 24 dmm). In their Sketch files 1 px = 1 dmm, and a 24 px font size is 24 dmm. They said these sizes "will go down over time as pixel density increases".** — McKenzie & Glazier 2017, 6:02–6:32, 17:32, 20:02. — verified yes, full text read yes. — "comfortably readable body text. And that happens to be 1.375 degrees" — For us: 1.375° as a **font size** (Sketch type size) is above our 1.2°. Daydream on a Pixel phone had lower pixel density than Quest 3, and Google expected the size to fall as density rises.
- **Google's dmm table (headline 40, title 32, subheading 28, body 1 and body 2 24, caption 20, button 24 dmm): unverified.** The talk shows the sizes on a slide but does not read them out. Cauz et al. 2024 (Table 3, "Google (2023)") and Kojic et al. 2020 (headline 40, titles 32) quote them. The Google Fonts "Using type in AR & VR" lesson sources (github.com/google/fonts, `cc-by-sa/knowledge/modules/using_type_in_ar_and_vr`) do not contain the table. — Cauz, M., Clarinval, A. & Dumas, B. (2024), *Text readability in augmented reality: a multivocal literature review*, Virtual Reality 28:59, doi:10.1007/s10055-024-00949-6, Table 3. — verified no (secondary); Cauz full text read no (§3.6–3.8 and Tables 2–3 only). — For us: if real, caption 20 dmm = 1.15° and body 24 dmm = 1.375° as font sizes. Our 1.2° falls between them.
- **Preferred text size (participants set size and distance themselves; Rift CV1; Arial; n = 18): x-height 23 ± 7.8 dmm (1.3 ± 0.44°), cap height 32 ± 11 dmm (1.8 ± 0.61°), "body height" 41 ± 14 dmm (2.3 ± 0.77°). Preferred view box 7.3 ± 1.7 lines by 40 ± 6.5 characters. 13 of 18 preferred white text on black; 14 of 18 preferred sans-serif.** — Dingler et al. 2018, p. 3 and Tables 1–2 (p. 4). — verified yes, full text read yes. — "The preferred height was 32 + / - 11dmm." — For us: preferences, not minimums, and on an older headset. **Kojic's "41 dmm ± 14" is Dingler's *body height*, not the cap height that Dingler's text reports (32 ± 11).**
- **Preferred angular size on Oculus Quest (1st generation) and Go (n = 22; Arial; participants set font size 5–40 pt and distance 0–10 m). Medium text (21 words): Quest 16.18 ± 5.25 dmm, Go 17.17 ± 4.90. Long text (51 words): Quest 17.87 ± 24.52, Go 16.01 ± 9.25. Short text (2 words): Quest 32.06 ± 18.75, Go 27.48 ± 9.86. All best-condition contrast ratios were at least 7:1.** — Kojic, T., Ali, D., Greinacher, R., Möller, S. & Voigt-Antons, J.-N. (2020), *User Experience of Reading in Virtual Reality – Finding Values for Text Distance, Size and Contrast*, QoMEX 2020, arXiv:2004.01545, Table I. — verified yes; full text read earlier (per the task, `kojic.txt`); today I re-read Table I and the related-work and discussion sections. — "short text … resulted in significantly larger angular size" — For us: 16–18 dmm = 0.92–1.03°, computed from font size and distance, so it is a **font-size** measure. Our 1.2° em is above the Quest preference for paragraphs. The standard deviations are very large.
- **Solum (2019, NTNU thesis; HTC Vive and Pimax 5K+): 21–23 dmm (1.2–1.3°) comfortable, 14–16 dmm (0.8–0.92°) minimum.** — Quoted in Kilpeläinen & Häkkinen 2023, p. 2. — verified no (the thesis could not be opened); full text read no. — For us: if confirmed, our 1.2° equals Solum's comfortable lower bound. Which letter dimension Solum measured is unknown.
- **Hoffman, McKenzie, Koprowski, Iqbal & Balram (2019, J. SID 27:207–222): on a 13.8 ppd display the legibility threshold for high-contrast letters was 45 arcmin (0.75°) and the comfort limit 70.2 arcmin (1.17°). Cauz 2024 reports from the same paper a comfortable 26 dmm (1.49°) for labels and body.** — Quoted in Kilpeläinen & Häkkinen 2023, p. 2, and Cauz et al. 2024, §3.8. — verified no (closed access); full text read no. — For us: on a 13.8 ppd display the comfort limit was 1.17°. Quest 2 has 20.6 ppd and Quest 3 more, so the needed size should be lower.
- **Objective legibility threshold on Oculus Quest 2 (20.6 ppd, 72 Hz; 29 observers aged 19–40; single upper-case Verdana letters shown for 200 ms on white noise (RMS contrast 0.32); 4-choice task): Quest 2 thresholds about 0.38° (staircase) and 0.31° (method of constant stimuli), measured as the height of an upper-case letter (= cap height). Varjo VR-2 about 0.26–0.28°; Vive Pro Eye about 0.45°.** I read these values off Figure 3 (p. 7); the paper gives no numeric table. The device effect was large (staircase F(2,50) = 76.5, ηp² = 0.75). Kilpeläinen adds that comfortable text is 2× the legibility threshold for young people with normal vision and 3× for older people or those with visual problems (Kochurova et al. 2015). — Kilpeläinen, M. & Häkkinen, J. (2023), *An effective method for measuring text legibility in XR devices reveals clear differences between three devices*, Frontiers in Virtual Reality 4:1243387, doi:10.3389/frvir.2023.1243387, Table 1 (p. 4), Figure 3 (p. 7), p. 9. — verified yes, full text read yes; Kochurova 2015 not read. — "comfortable text size is twice the legibility threshold value for young persons" — For us: this is the strongest **objective** Quest-class measurement. Derived comfort on Quest 2: cap height about 0.6–0.8° (×2) or 0.9–1.1° (×3), and the noise background makes these conservative. Our 1.2° em is in that range only if the font's cap height is a large share of the em. Check the font file, then test in the headset.
- **Effective resolution matters more than ppd. On a Varjo XR-4 (55 ppd VR) with resolution degraded on a logMAR scale, reading speed, critical print size and simulator sickness all worsened exponentially below 0 logMAR. Fitted critical print size (English) = 0.1091·e^(2.54·x) + 0.30 logMAR of x-height, where x is the effective resolution in logMAR (R² = .998).** — Wang, J., Cheng, X., Hou, B. & Liang, H.-N. (2026), *Resolution deficits drive simulator sickness and compromise reading performance in virtual environments*, arXiv:2601.02829 (preprint), §2 eqs. 1–7 and §3.5.4. — verified yes; full text read **no** (methods and results sections only; preprint). — "reading performance and user comfort degrade exponentially as resolution drops below 0 logMAR" — For us: by my arithmetic from their fit, the critical x-height is 0.25° at 0.2 logMAR, 0.33° at 0.4 and 0.53° at 0.6. Quest 3's effective logMAR is unknown (the OVVA paper that measures it is closed), so this only shows the method.
- **Desktop standard: minimum character height (an upper-case "H" in Figure 7-4, so cap height) "shall" be 16 arcmin (0.27°) and "should" be 22–30 arcmin (0.37–0.50°). Recognising single characters needs 16–18′; reading speed keeps rising up to 22′; footnotes may go down to 10′. In §5.2.4.2: at least 16′ (2.3 mm at 50 cm) for legibility, 20–22′ for readability.** — ANSI/HFES 100-2007, §7.2.6.1 (p. 68) and §5.2.4.2 (p. 22). — verified yes; full text read no (these clauses). — "The minimum character height (see Figure 7-4) shall be 16 arc minutes" — For us: the desktop floor at high resolution. VR at 20–25 ppd needs much more, as the HMD studies above show.
- **ISO: 20–22′ at desktop distances (citing ISO 9241-303); the maximum viewing distance is based on 16′; generally accepted legibility on 21′; rule of thumb: viewing distance ≈ 165 × character height (±30% for most users).** — ISO 9241-306:2018, §5.1.1, Table 1 and notes (official SIS preview). — verified yes; full text read no (preview). ISO 9241-303 itself was not read (paid; the free sample had only front matter). — "Generally accepted legibility … is calculated based on 21′ of arc." — For us: by that rule a 21 mm letter is meant for about 3.5 m on a desktop. In a headset this rule does not hold.
- **Meta typography: font size at least 14 px for minimal legibility, 18 px or more for comfortable reading; sans-serif with a high x-height; no italics; Inter is the system typeface. Test on device.** — Meta "Typography" (2026-04-19), https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/styles_typography/; also "Immersive 2D apps" (pwa, 2026-02-18). — verified yes (live). — "a font size no smaller than 14px is required for minimal legibility" — For us: Meta gives no px-to-degree conversion. **Derived, not stated by Meta:** the accessibility page equates 48 dp with "3° FOV at 0.42 m" (22 mm), which gives 1 dp ≈ 0.0625°. If px = dp (Meta uses them interchangeably for hit targets), then 14 px ≈ 0.88° and 18 px ≈ 1.13° as font size. Our 1.2° em is just above Meta's derived "comfortable" size.

## Target size

- **Meta: minimum angular collider size about 2.5°–3°, for both direct touch and ray casting. Minimum hit target 48 dp at panel scale (about 22 mm at touch distance), with the visible button inside a larger collider; 12 mm between interactables. Accessibility: 22 mm × 22 mm / 48 dp × 48 dp / 3° FOV at 0.42 m; 60 × 60 dp for primary controls with hand tracking.** — Meta "Hands UI best practices" (2026-09-24, "Sizing and density"); "Accessibility" (2026-04-06); "Inputs and hit targets" (2026-04-19). — verified yes (live). — "Minimum angular collider size: roughly 2.5° to 3° for both direct touch and ray casting." — For us: MIN_TARGET_DEG = 2.5 is exactly Meta's lower bound. Our buttons are 70 mm at 1 m = 4.0°.
- **Google Daydream: the minimum comfortable ray-based hit target is about 64 dmm (3.67°) with 16 dmm (0.92°) padding. It "won't go down over time" (limited by hand tremor, not pixels) and is the same on Vive or any well-tracked platform.** — McKenzie & Glazier 2017, 20:32–21:00. — verified yes, full text read yes. — "the minimum hit target size we found that's comfortable is about 64 dmms" — For us: stricter than Meta. Our actual 4.0° buttons pass; the 2.5° floor would not.
- **Fitts-style note: rays get more erratic with distance, so sizing UI in angular units keeps hit areas fair at any distance.** — McKenzie & Glazier 2017, 14:03–14:30; Meta "Hands UI" ("maintain its angular size rather than its physical size"). — verified yes. — "maintain its angular size rather than its physical size" — For us: our checks are angular already. Keep them angular if the sheet distance ever changes.
