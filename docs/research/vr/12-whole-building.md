## 1. Real buildings of the period with plans and photos

- **Science and Engineering Offices (SEO), University of Illinois at Chicago Circle**, 851 S. Morgan St.: 13 stories,
  opened 1968; two sections "slightly off-set from one another" with "matching concrete-framed scissor staircases"
  (UIC Library exhibit, https://exhibits.library.uic.edu/the-historic-netsch-campus/science-and-engineering-offices,
  opened). Photo records (UIC Library Digital Collections, Facility Information Management; creator Skidmore, Owings &
  Merrill; "In copyright", the University of Illinois Board of Trustees; public access), each looked at:
  completed building, 29 Dec 1967 (id 422731, ark:/81984/d3sx64f8v); "View of elevator lobby", 25 Jan 1968 (422713,
  d3zp3w54f: two elevator doors with up/down lanterns on one wall, a door at the end); "a hallway with office doorways
  opening on to it", 25 Jan 1968 (422707, d3bz61d6h: doors on one side, fluted concrete on the other, surface-mounted
  fluorescent strips); "View of stairway", 28 May 1968 (422669, d37p8tj7c); scissor stairway 25 Apr 1968 (422679);
  office doorways 22 Feb 1968 (422699). Plans (current, UIC Environmental Health and Safety evacuation drawings, one a
  floor, https://ehso.uic.edu/fire-safety/building-specific-emergency-action-plans/, PDFs of 2023, opened): two wings,
  each a corridor with offices on both sides, offset; in the middle a north-south elevator hall joining the two wings'
  corridors, four elevators (marked E, not in the legend; read as elevators, as the 1968 lobby photo shows), two facing
  two; one scissor stair beside it on each side, about three offices long, the west one north of its corridor, the east
  one south of its; on floor 1 each stair has its own door straight outside, and the way in is under an overhang at the
  east end; floor 5 has 37 numbered rooms. Measured on the floor-5 drawing (no scale printed; door swings taken as 3 ft):
  each elevator with its shaft about one office module, about 3 m; the hall one module wide; the core three modules
  along the corridor and two deep. That the 1968 core is unchanged: consistent with the 1968 photos, not proven.
- **University Hall (UH), same campus**, 601 S. Morgan St., first named the Staff and Administrative Offices Building:
  SOM drawings of 2 July 1962 (sheet A-12, section: OFFICE | CORRIDOR | ELEV. LOBBY | CORRIDOR | OFFICE, "25 FLOORS @
  11'-6""), built 1963-65, 28 floors; photos of 1965 labelled on the print (a 2nd-floor entrance beside an elevator, 5/3/65;
  an unfinished corridor, 1/4/65) and the east side about 1966 (id 333599, "Copyright not evaluated"). Current plans (same
  EHS page): a central core with a stair near each end and elevators between, a ring corridor round it, offices on the
  outer walls.
- Not usable: Harvard's William James Hall (15 stories, 1963, psychology; drawings closed to Harvard staff); UCLA's
  Psychology Tower (Franz Hall II, 1965-67, eight floors above ground, rebuilt inside 2021); no 10+ floor academic
  building of 1960-79 found in Evanston (search).

## 2. The rules of the time: The BOCA Basic Building Code/1978 (opened, full text)

https://archive.org/details/boca-basic-building-code-1978 (text: objekt-files/papers/boca-1978.txt). Offices and
"testing and research laboratories" are use group B (204.1).
- 609.2: "not less than two (2) approved independent exitways serving every story" (an exitway is any protected way
  out, 607.1: on upper floors the stairs, on the entrance floor an outside door counts).
- 607.3: "as remote from each other as practicable, and shall be arranged to provide direct access in separate
  directions from any point in the area served".
- 610.2: "The length of a dead end corridor shall not be more than twenty (20) feet" (6.1 m).
- 1610.1: "Elevators shall not be accepted as a required element of an exitway"; 1607.2: where elevators are installed,
  one serves the fire department, car at least 4'3" x 6'8" for an ambulance cot; 315.7: if elevators give access, one
  meets the handicapped rules (25 sq ft car, 32 in door).
- 618.2-618.3: in a use group B building over six stories or 75 ft, at least one exit stair is a smokeproof enclosure,
  reached through a vestibule or balcony 72 in deep; in a high-rise with sprinklers throughout it "may be omitted, but
  required stairways shall be pressurized" (431.3.1, item 7).
- 607.2.1: "All exitways shall discharge directly at a public way or at a yard, court or open space"; 611.3, 611.5: a
  stair may discharge into a sprinklered lobby; not more than half the stairs through one passageway.
- 616.11: "A sign shall be provided at each landing in all interior stairways more than three (3) stories in height
  designating the floor level above the floor of discharge" (the stair sign, see 10-floor-directory.md, 3).
- NFPA 101-1973 (helper's reading of the scanned book's search, OCR, not opened by me): two exits a floor (13-2411),
  elevators not an exit (13-42). The 1976 edition, and which code Evanston enforced in 1979: **unverified**.
- Pages only a person can open (listed by the helpers on 15.10, given to the owner on 16.10); none changes what is
  built, which follows BOCA 1978 as read above. The Uniform Building Code of 1976
  (https://archive.org/details/uniformbuildingc0000unse_g4g9), **opened** on 16.10 by the owner's loan, his
  screenshots in objekt-files/imports/owner-screens-2026-10-16: Sec. 3302 (a), p. 497, floors above the first with an
  occupant load over 10 have "not less than two exits"; (c), p. 498, two exits "placed a distance apart equal to not
  less than one-half of the length of the maximum overall diagonal dimension of the building or area to be served
  measured in a straight line between exits" (so by 1976 this had replaced the 1964 edition's "one-fifth of the
  perimeter"); (d), 150 ft to an exit unsprinklered, 200 ft sprinklered. Ours passes: the two stair doors 12.8 m apart
  (plan.js, bays -2 and 2), half the floor's overall diagonal 9.7 m (its outline 15.0 x 12.2 m with walls, the rooms
  3.2 m deep as room 01, the core 6.4 m: 19.3 m corner to corner; it passes up to a 20.7 m deep outline). The 1979
  edition (https://archive.org/details/uniformbuildingc0000vari_l1f7) is then not needed for this. NFPA's 1981 Life
  Safety Code Handbook (https://archive.org/details/lifesafetycodeha0000lath), **searched** on 16.10 by the owner's
  loan (its search-inside list for "college" on his screenshots, same folder): p. 23 "College classroom buildings are
  considered Business Occupancies"; p. 24 the business list holds "College and university-instructional buildings,
  classrooms under 50 persons, and instructional laboratories"; p. 254 "Because of the maturity of the occupants,
  college buildings more properly resemble office occupancies". So our floor is an office (business) floor in 1981 as
  in BOCA 1978's use group B; which edition first moved colleges there: **unverified**, not needed. The 1973 Life
  Safety Code (https://archive.org/details/lifesafetycode1900nati), **searched** on 16.10 by the owner's loan for
  "dead end" (33 results, his screenshots, same folder): p. 91, 9-1271, educational, no dead-end corridor "more than
  20 feet beyond the stairway"; p. 115, 10-1236, none "exceeding 30 feet"; p. 144, "a dead-end corridor up to 35 feet
  long may be permitted"; p. 171, 14-2163, "no dead end may be more than 50 feet deep"; p. 201, Table A-5-118 compiles
  them by occupancy (the table itself not read). The least found is 20 ft (6.1 m); ours are 0.9 m. Closed to us both:
  Harvard's William James Hall drawings (https://harvardplanning.emuseum.com/objects/111748, Harvard staff only);
  UIC's record drawings of SEO (its facilities site https://fimweb.vcas.uic.edu/Public/BuildingSummary opens to a
  guest and lists "0631 - Science & Engineering Offices (SEO)"; whether its "Record Drawings" archive needs a
  university login: **unverified**).

## 3. Our floor against them

src/app/lobby/plan.js: one stair, in the middle of the south wall (x 0.7), the end walls 7.3 m from it on both sides; no
elevator. So one exit where two are required (609.2); 610.2's dead-end limit is written for corridors serving more than
one exitway, so with one stair it is the count that fails.
Three plans of our floor were described to him in words on 16.10 (the page with them was still being fact-checked): A,
modelled on SEO (the elevator hall where the stair is now, a stair on each side of it, 7 rooms); B, stairs at both ends;
C, as now. He chose A ("Yes, A"). Then an error of mine: the elevators were drawn a third of their size. To scale (section
1: a car with its shaft about one 3.2 m bay, the core three bays along the corridor and two deep) the core takes the
middle three bays of the south wall and the stairs move to the next bays out: A1, the same length, 5 rooms, dead ends
0.9 m; A2, one bay longer at each end (21.0 m), 9 rooms, dead ends 4.1 m (stair door centre to end wall). He chose
"A1, 5 rooms". Ours differs
from SEO: one straight corridor, so both stairs on the same side as the hall; each stair one bay wide; on floor 1 each
stair gets its own door straight outside, as in SEO (607.2.1, 611.5), and one stair a 72 in vestibule (618.3).
The way in differs too: ours is at the hall's far end, a glass wall with a pair of swinging doors after University
Hall's 1965 entrance (UIC record 427827); in SEO it is at the east end under an overhang, reached along a corridor. One
of SEO's four cars also serves the basement (its 2023 basement plan shows one elevator there): ours has the down lantern
on that car only. The directories: the main one at the hall's far end; the floor directory where one comes out of the
hall into the corridor (the 1979 report's place for its panel frames, a lobby's opening), found by the practice review.
