## Answer

Not found: a game, VR or flat, in which the player undergoes re-created, named, published psychology experiments
and is then shown how he was caught, what the paper found and how he compares with other players. The pieces exist
apart. Flat apps and course labs re-create classic experiments and show the user his result next to the classic one
(CogLab, manual of 2003; The Great Brain Experiment, 2013; Psych Lab 101, 2015). VR apps make the player the subject
of psychology tasks (PsychLabVR 2017, Self-knowledge VR 2019, HURRAH 2025), but none of them names the experiments
it re-creates or reveals a deception. Live shows did the "caught, then explained" part (Mind Field 2017-2019; David
Byrne's Neurosociety 2016 and Theater of the Mind 2022). "Not found" covers only the search below (14.10.2026).

## Closest to us (ranked)

1. The Great Brain Experiment, UCL, iPhone/Android, March 2013 (part 3, 7a). Four classic paradigms as games,
   consent, anonymous data for a published paper, scores against other users, the paradigm's background to read;
   20,800 completers in month one. Differs: flat; performance tasks, no deception, no "how you were caught"; no rooms.
2. Psych Lab 101, Neurobehavioral Systems, iOS 2015 and Android (part 3, 7c). "35 well-known cognitive and clinical
   psychology experiments" tried on yourself, results against classic results, background with references.
   Differs: a flat course companion, no game, no data for science, no deception or reveal; 2 ratings.
3. CogLab, Francis & Neath, Wadsworth / Cengage, manual of 2003 (part 3, 7b). About 45 classic experiments with the
   student as participant and a data summary after each. Differs: a paid course tool, flat, cognitive only.
4. Self-knowledge VR, Aerolito, Steam, 9 Jan 2019 (part 2). VR; four mini-games "based on real-world psychological
   theories", a trait report at the end; 29 reviews. Differs: no experiment or paper named, personality-style
   readings, no reveal of a trick, nothing sent for science.
5. HURRAH, University of Helsinki, Quest 3, 14 Aug 2025 (part 2). VR from a social-psychology group, anonymous
   data for research, "explore yourself". Differs: social-change encounters; no named experiment or reveal on the
   page; no reviews.
6. Theater of the Mind, Byrne and Gaonkar, Denver, 2022, and Neurosociety, Menlo Park, 2016 (part 3, 8). Lab
   phenomena undergone by visitors and carried by a story; Neurosociety re-created the "Being Barbie" body
   experiment in VR goggles. Differs: physical theatre, closed, no data.
7. Trolley Problem, Inc., Steam, 2022, 1602 reviews (part 2). Scenes "based on genuine philosophical papers,
   complete with references", choices compared with the world. Differs: flat; dilemmas answered knowingly.
8. Milgram re-creations on itch.io: You Must Continue, The Milgram Simulation, Milgram Revisited (2023) (part 3, 5).
   The player sits in the teacher's chair. Differs: one experiment, horror or art, no debrief, flat.
9. Teacher's Lens Beta, Debias VR, Rift (part 2). The IAT turned into a VR game. Not on sale; no date.
10. Refind Self, Steam, 2023, 3811 reviews (part 2). The player is measured by how he plays, gets a personality
    reveal and an online comparison. Differs: one personality estimate, no experiment re-created.
Already known and not re-ranked: PsychLabVR (2017), Sea Hero Quest VR (2017), VERA and Ouvrai (research platforms).
